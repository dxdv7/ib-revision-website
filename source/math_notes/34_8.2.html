Expected frequency in a contingency table

E = (row total × column total) / grand total, where E is the expected frequency for a cell, assuming the two variables are independent.
Worked exampleIn a survey of 100 people, 50 are male and 50 are female. Overall, 45 people prefer tea and 55 prefer coffee.
Row total (male) = 50, column total (tea) = 45, grand total = 100.
E(male, tea) = (50 × 45) / 100 = 22.5
E(male, coffee) = (50 × 55) / 100 = 27.5

The χ2 test statistic

χ2 = the sum of (O − E)2 / E over all cells, where O is the observed frequency and E is the expected frequency of each cell.
Worked exampleA 2 by 2 table gives observed frequencies: Smokers with disease = 30, Smokers without disease = 20, Non-smokers with disease = 10, Non-smokers without disease = 40 (row totals 50 and 50, column totals 40 and 60, grand total 100).
E(smoker, disease) = (50 × 40)/100 = 20, E(smoker, no disease) = (50 × 60)/100 = 30
E(non-smoker, disease) = (50 × 40)/100 = 20, E(non-smoker, no disease) = (50 × 60)/100 = 30
χ2 = (30−20)2/20 + (20−30)2/30 + (10−20)2/20 + (40−30)2/30
χ2 = 5 + 3.33 + 5 + 3.33 = 16.67

Degrees of freedom

v = (r − 1)(c − 1), where r is the number of rows and c is the number of columns in the contingency table (not counting the totals row/column).
Worked exampleA contingency table has 4 rows (types of pet: dog, cat, bird, fish) and 3 columns (age groups).
v = (4 − 1)(3 − 1) = 3 × 2 = 6

Reaching a conclusion: test statistic vs critical value, or p-value vs significance level

If χ2calc > critical value, reject H0; if χ2calc ≤ critical value, accept H0. Equivalently, if the p-value < significance level, reject H0; if the p-value ≥ significance level, accept H0.
Worked exampleA χ2 test for independence between favourite music genre and gender gives χ2calc = 1.62 with 3 degrees of freedom, and a p-value of 0.654. The test is carried out at the 1% significance level, with critical value 11.345.
Since 1.62 < 11.345 (equivalently, 0.654 > 0.01), accept H0: favourite music genre is independent of gender.
