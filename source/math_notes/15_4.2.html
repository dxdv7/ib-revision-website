Gradient as a rate of change

gradient = change in y / change in x = Δy / Δx, and in a real-world graph this represents the rate of change of the quantity on the y-axis with respect to the quantity on the x-axis (for example, speed, cost per unit, or growth per year).
Worked exampleA car travels at constant speed. Its distance-time graph is a straight line passing through (0, 0) and (3, 240), where x is time in hours and y is distance in km.
gradient = Δy / Δx = (240 − 0) / (3 − 0) = 240 / 3
gradient = 80
Final answer: the car’s speed is 80 km/h.

Classifying a line by the sign of its gradient

Gradient > 0: the line rises left to right (the quantity is increasing). Gradient < 0: the line falls left to right (the quantity is decreasing). Gradient = 0: the line is horizontal (the quantity is constant, no rate of change). Gradient undefined: the line is vertical (the change happens with no change in x, so the rate cannot be calculated).
Worked exampleA tank contains 500 litres of water and is drained steadily. After 30 minutes it contains 140 litres. Let x = time in minutes and y = volume in litres. Find the gradient and interpret it.
gradient = (140 − 500) / (30 − 0) = −360 / 30
gradient = −12
Since the gradient is negative, the volume is decreasing.
Final answer: the tank drains at a rate of 12 litres per minute.

Comparing the steepness of two rate graphs

When comparing two lines representing real-world rates, the line with the greater |gradient| has the greater rate of change (it is steeper). Find each gradient using gradient = Δy / Δx, then compare the two values.
Worked exampleTwo car rental companies charge based on distance driven. Company A charges USD 70 for 100 km and USD 150 for 300 km. Company B charges USD 90 for 100 km and USD 130 for 300 km. Which company charges a higher rate per km?
Company A gradient = (150 − 70) / (300 − 100) = 80 / 200 = 0.4
Company B gradient = (130 − 90) / (300 − 100) = 40 / 200 = 0.2
Since 0.4 > 0.2, Company A’s line is steeper.
Final answer: Company A charges the higher rate, USD 0.40 per km, compared to Company B’s USD 0.20 per km.

Gradient expressed as a percentage incline

percentage gradient = (rise / run) × 100, where rise is the vertical change and run is the horizontal change. A larger percentage means a steeper incline.
Worked exampleSafety regulations state that a wheelchair ramp must not exceed a gradient of 0.08 (8%). A ramp has a rise of 0.9 m over a run of 12 m. Does it meet the safety regulation?
gradient = rise / run = 0.9 / 12 = 0.075
percentage gradient = 0.075 × 100 = 7.5%
Since 0.075 < 0.08 (7.5% < 8%), the ramp meets the requirement.
Final answer: the ramp’s gradient is 7.5%, which satisfies the safety regulation.

Using a known gradient (rate) to find an unknown value

If the gradient (rate) m of a line and one point on it are known, an unknown y-value at a given x can be found from gradient = (y − y1) / (x − x1), rearranged as y = y1 + m(x − x1).
Worked exampleA ski lift departs a station at height 40 m (when the horizontal distance travelled is 0 m). The slope has a constant gradient (rate of ascent) of 0.25. Find the height of the lift when the horizontal distance travelled is 640 m.
y = y1 + m(x − x1)
y = 40 + 0.25(640 − 0)
y = 40 + 160
Final answer: the lift is at a height of 200 m.
