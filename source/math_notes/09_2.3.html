Volume of a cuboid

\[\text{Volume} = \text{length} \times \text{width} \times \text{height} = \text{area of base} \times \text{height}\]
Worked exampleA storage box is a cuboid with dimensions 12 cm by 8 cm by 5 cm. Find its volume.
\[V = 12 \times 8 \times 5\]\[\boxed{V = 480 \text{ cm}^3}\]

Volume of a prism

\[V = \text{area of cross-section} \times \text{height (length)}\]where the cross-section is the constant 2D shape running along the length of the prism.
Worked exampleA triangular prism has a cross-section that is a triangle with base 8 cm and height 6 cm, and the prism has length 15 cm. Find its volume.
\[\text{Area of cross-section} = 0.5 \times 8 \times 6 = 24 \text{ cm}^2\]\[\boxed{V = 24 \times 15 = 360 \text{ cm}^3}\]

Surface area of a cuboid

\[SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)\]where \(l\), \(w\) and \(h\) are the length, width and height of the cuboid.
Worked exampleFind the surface area of the cuboid from the previous example, with dimensions 12 cm, 8 cm and 5 cm.
\[SA = 2(12 \times 8 + 12 \times 5 + 8 \times 5) = 2(96 + 60 + 40)\]\[\boxed{SA = 2 \times 196 = 392 \text{ cm}^2}\]

Volume of a cylinder

\[V = \pi r^2 h\]where \(r\) is the radius of the circular cross-section and \(h\) is the height.
Worked exampleCalculate the volume of a cylinder with radius 4 cm and height 10 cm.
\[V = \pi \times 4^2 \times 10 = 160\pi\]\[\boxed{V = 503 \text{ cm}^3 \text{ (3 sf)}}\]

Surface area of a cylinder

\[\text{Curved surface area (CSA)} = 2\pi r h\]\[\text{Total surface area} = 2\pi r h + 2\pi r^2\](the curved surface plus the two circular ends).
Worked exampleFind the total surface area of the cylinder with radius 4 cm and height 10 cm.
\[CSA = 2\pi \times 4 \times 10 = 80\pi\]\[\boxed{CSA = 251 \text{ cm}^2 \text{ (3 sf)}}\]\[\text{Total SA} = 80\pi + 2\pi(4^2) = 80\pi + 32\pi = 112\pi\]\[\boxed{\text{Total SA} = 352 \text{ cm}^2 \text{ (3 sf)}}\]

Volume of a cone

\[V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h\]where \(r\) is the base radius and \(h\) is the vertical (perpendicular) height.
Worked exampleCalculate the volume of a cone with radius 3 cm and vertical height 8 cm.
\[V = \frac{1}{3} \times \pi \times 3^2 \times 8 = 24\pi\]\[\boxed{V = 75.4 \text{ cm}^3 \text{ (3 sf)}}\]

Surface area of a cone

\[\text{Curved surface area (CSA)} = \pi r l\]where \(l\) is the slant height. If \(l\) is not given, use \(l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}\) (Pythagoras).
\[\text{Total surface area} = \pi r l + \pi r^2\](the curved surface plus the circular base).
Worked exampleFind the total surface area of the cone with radius 3 cm and vertical height 8 cm.
\[l = \sqrt{3^2 + 8^2} = \sqrt{73} = 8.544 \text{ cm}\]\[CSA = \pi \times 3 \times 8.544 = 80.6 \text{ cm}^2\]\[\text{Total SA} = 80.6 + \pi(3^2) = 80.6 + 28.3\]\[\boxed{\text{Total SA} = 109 \text{ cm}^2 \text{ (3 sf)}}\]

Volume of a sphere

\[V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\]where \(r\) is the radius of the sphere.
Worked exampleCalculate the volume of a sphere with radius 6 cm.
\[V = \frac{4}{3} \times \pi \times 6^3 = \frac{4}{3} \times \pi \times 216 = 288\pi\]\[\boxed{V = 905 \text{ cm}^3 \text{ (3 sf)}}\]

Surface area of a sphere

\[SA = 4\pi r^2\]where \(r\) is the radius of the sphere.
Worked exampleFind the surface area of the sphere with radius 6 cm.
\[SA = 4 \times \pi \times 6^2 = 4 \times \pi \times 36 = 144\pi\]\[\boxed{SA = 452 \text{ cm}^2 \text{ (3 sf)}}\]

Volume of a pyramid

\[V = \frac{1}{3} \times \text{base area} \times \text{height}\]where height is the perpendicular (vertical) height from the apex to the base.
Worked exampleA rectangular pyramid has a base measuring 6 cm by 9 cm and a vertical height of 10 cm. Find its volume.
\[\text{Base area} = 6 \times 9 = 54 \text{ cm}^2\]\[\boxed{V = \frac{1}{3} \times 54 \times 10 = 180 \text{ cm}^3}\]

Surface area of a pyramid

\[SA = \text{base area} + \text{sum of the areas of the triangular faces}\]Each triangular face has area \(\frac{1}{2} \times \text{base edge} \times \text{slant height of that face}\).
Worked exampleA right square-based pyramid has a base of side 6 cm, and each triangular face has a slant height of 5 cm. Find the total surface area.
\[\text{Base area} = 6 \times 6 = 36 \text{ cm}^2\]\[\text{Area of each triangular face} = \frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 5 = 15 \text{ cm}^2\]\[\boxed{\text{Total SA} = 36 + 4 \times 15 = 36 + 60 = 96 \text{ cm}^2}\]

Space diagonal of a cuboid

\[d = \sqrt{l^2 + w^2 + h^2}\]the length of the diagonal connecting opposite vertices of a cuboid with dimensions \(l\), \(w\) and \(h\) (from applying the Pythagorean theorem twice in 3D).
Worked exampleCuboid ABCDEFGH has dimensions 4 cm, 5 cm and 6 cm. Find the length of the space diagonal.
\[d = \sqrt{4^2 + 5^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{16 + 25 + 36} = \sqrt{77}\]\[\boxed{d = 8.77 \text{ cm (3 sf)}}\]

Angle between a line and a plane

To find the angle between a line and a plane, drop a perpendicular from a point on the line to the plane, forming a right-angled triangle. The required angle \(\theta\) satisfies
\[\tan \theta = \frac{\text{perpendicular height}}{\text{horizontal distance}}\]found using right-angled trigonometry.
Worked exampleA right pyramid has a square base of side 8 cm, with apex E vertically above the centre O of the base, and \(EO = 10\) cm. Find the angle between edge [EA] (where A is a base vertex) and the base.
OA is half the diagonal of the base:
\[OA = \frac{8\sqrt{2}}{2} = 5.657 \text{ cm}\]\[\tan \theta = \frac{EO}{OA} = \frac{10}{5.657} = 1.7678\]\[\boxed{\theta = \tan^{-1}(1.7678) = 60.5^{\circ} \text{ (3 sf)}}\]

Angle between two lines in 3D (cosine rule)

To find the angle between two lines meeting at a point, find the lengths of the two lines and the segment joining their other endpoints (using the 3D Pythagorean theorem), then apply the cosine rule:
\[\cos \theta = \frac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab}\]
Worked exampleUsing the same pyramid (square base side 8 cm, \(EO = 10\) cm), find the angle AEB between the slant edges [EA] and [EB], where A and B are adjacent base vertices.
\[EA^2 = OA^2 + EO^2 = 5.657^2 + 10^2 = 32 + 100 = 132\]so \(EA = EB = \sqrt{132}\) cm.
\(AB = 8\) cm (a side of the square base).
\[\cos(AEB) = \frac{132 + 132 - 8^2}{2 \times 132} = \frac{200}{264} = 0.7576\]\[\boxed{AEB = \cos^{-1}(0.7576) = 40.7^{\circ} \text{ (3 sf)}}\]

Angle between two planes (dihedral angle)

To find the angle between a triangular face and the base of a pyramid, let M be the midpoint of a base edge and O the centre of the base. Since OM and EM are both perpendicular to that base edge, the required angle is EMO, with
\[\tan(EMO) = \frac{EO}{OM}\]
Worked exampleUsing the same pyramid (square base side 8 cm, \(EO = 10\) cm), find the angle between a triangular face and the base.
\(OM\) is half the base side: \(OM = 4\) cm.
\[\tan(EMO) = \frac{EO}{OM} = \frac{10}{4} = 2.5\]\[\boxed{EMO = \tan^{-1}(2.5) = 68.2^{\circ} \text{ (3 sf)}}\]
