Volume of a cuboid

Volume = length × width × height = area of base × height.
Worked exampleA storage box is a cuboid with dimensions 12 cm by 8 cm by 5 cm. Find its volume.
V = 12 × 8 × 5 = 480 cm3

Volume of a prism

V = area of cross-section × height (length), where the cross-section is the constant 2D shape running along the length of the prism.
Worked exampleA triangular prism has a cross-section that is a triangle with base 8 cm and height 6 cm, and the prism has length 15 cm. Find its volume.
Area of cross-section = 0.5 × 8 × 6 = 24 cm2
V = 24 × 15 = 360 cm3

Surface area of a cuboid

SA = 2(lw + lh + wh), where l, w and h are the length, width and height of the cuboid.
Worked exampleFind the surface area of the cuboid from the previous example, with dimensions 12 cm, 8 cm and 5 cm.
SA = 2(12 × 8 + 12 × 5 + 8 × 5) = 2(96 + 60 + 40) = 2 × 196 = 392 cm2

Volume of a cylinder

V = πr2h, where r is the radius of the circular cross-section and h is the height.
Worked exampleCalculate the volume of a cylinder with radius 4 cm and height 10 cm.
V = π × 42 × 10 = 160π = 503 cm3 (3 sf)

Surface area of a cylinder

Curved surface area (CSA) = 2πrh. Total surface area = 2πrh + 2πr2 (the curved surface plus the two circular ends).
Worked exampleFind the total surface area of the cylinder with radius 4 cm and height 10 cm.
CSA = 2π × 4 × 10 = 80π = 251 cm2 (3 sf)
Total SA = 80π + 2π(42) = 80π + 32π = 112π = 352 cm2 (3 sf)

Volume of a cone

V = (1/3)πr2h, where r is the base radius and h is the vertical (perpendicular) height.
Worked exampleCalculate the volume of a cone with radius 3 cm and vertical height 8 cm.
V = (1/3) × π × 32 × 8 = 24π = 75.4 cm3 (3 sf)

Surface area of a cone

Curved surface area (CSA) = πrl, where l is the slant height. If l is not given, use l = √(r2 + h2) (Pythagoras). Total surface area = πrl + πr2 (the curved surface plus the circular base).
Worked exampleFind the total surface area of the cone with radius 3 cm and vertical height 8 cm.
Slant height l = √(32 + 82) = √73 = 8.544 cm
CSA = π × 3 × 8.544 = 80.6 cm2
Total SA = 80.6 + π(32) = 80.6 + 28.3 = 109 cm2 (3 sf)

Volume of a sphere

V = (4/3)πr3, where r is the radius of the sphere.
Worked exampleCalculate the volume of a sphere with radius 6 cm.
V = (4/3) × π × 63 = (4/3) × π × 216 = 288π = 905 cm3 (3 sf)

Surface area of a sphere

SA = 4πr2, where r is the radius of the sphere.
Worked exampleFind the surface area of the sphere with radius 6 cm.
SA = 4 × π × 62 = 4 × π × 36 = 144π = 452 cm2 (3 sf)

Volume of a pyramid

V = (1/3) × base area × height, where height is the perpendicular (vertical) height from the apex to the base.
Worked exampleA rectangular pyramid has a base measuring 6 cm by 9 cm and a vertical height of 10 cm. Find its volume.
Base area = 6 × 9 = 54 cm2
V = (1/3) × 54 × 10 = 180 cm3

Surface area of a pyramid

SA = base area + sum of the areas of the triangular faces. Each triangular face has area (1/2) × base edge × slant height of that face.
Worked exampleA right square-based pyramid has a base of side 6 cm, and each triangular face has a slant height of 5 cm. Find the total surface area.
Base area = 6 × 6 = 36 cm2
Area of each triangular face = (1/2) × 6 × 5 = 15 cm2
Total SA = 36 + 4 × 15 = 36 + 60 = 96 cm2

Space diagonal of a cuboid

d = √(l2 + w2 + h2), the length of the diagonal connecting opposite vertices of a cuboid with dimensions l, w and h (from applying the Pythagorean theorem twice in 3D).
Worked exampleCuboid ABCDEFGH has dimensions 4 cm, 5 cm and 6 cm. Find the length of the space diagonal.
d = √(42 + 52 + 62) = √(16 + 25 + 36) = √77 = 8.77 cm (3 sf)

Angle between a line and a plane

To find the angle between a line and a plane, drop a perpendicular from a point on the line to the plane, forming a right-angled triangle. The required angle θ satisfies tan θ = (perpendicular height) / (horizontal distance), found using right-angled trigonometry.
Worked exampleA right pyramid has a square base of side 8 cm, with apex E vertically above the centre O of the base, and EO = 10 cm. Find the angle between edge [EA] (where A is a base vertex) and the base.
OA = half the diagonal of the base = (8√2) / 2 = 5.657 cm
tan θ = EO / OA = 10 / 5.657 = 1.7678
θ = tan-1(1.7678) = 60.5° (3 sf)

Angle between two lines in 3D (cosine rule)

To find the angle between two lines meeting at a point, find the lengths of the two lines and the segment joining their other endpoints (using the 3D Pythagorean theorem), then apply the cosine rule: cos θ = (a2 + b2 − c2) / (2ab).
Worked exampleUsing the same pyramid (square base side 8 cm, EO = 10 cm), find the angle AEB between the slant edges [EA] and [EB], where A and B are adjacent base vertices.
EA2 = OA2 + EO2 = 5.6572 + 102 = 32 + 100 = 132, so EA = EB = √132 cm
AB = 8 cm (a side of the square base)
cos(AEB) = (132 + 132 − 82) / (2 × 132) = 200 / 264 = 0.7576
AEB = cos-1(0.7576) = 40.7° (3 sf)

Angle between two planes (dihedral angle)

To find the angle between a triangular face and the base of a pyramid, let M be the midpoint of a base edge and O the centre of the base. Since OM and EM are both perpendicular to that base edge, the required angle is EMO, with tan(EMO) = EO / OM.
Worked exampleUsing the same pyramid (square base side 8 cm, EO = 10 cm), find the angle between a triangular face and the base.
OM = half the base side = 4 cm
tan(EMO) = EO / OM = 10 / 4 = 2.5
EMO = tan-1(2.5) = 68.2° (3 sf)
