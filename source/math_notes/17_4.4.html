Condition for parallel lines

Two lines with gradients m1 and m2 are parallel if and only if m1 = m2.
Worked exampleLine L1 passes through (2, 4) and (-1, -2). Line L2 passes through (0, -4) and (2, 0). Determine whether L1 and L2 are parallel.
Gradient of L1 = (-2 − 4) / (-1 − 2) = -6 / -3 = 2
Gradient of L2 = (0 − (-4)) / (2 − 0) = 4 / 2 = 2
Since the gradients are equal, L1 and L2 are parallel.

Equation of a line parallel to a given line

To find the equation of a line parallel to a line with gradient m, through a point (x1, y1): use y − y1 = m(x − x1), since the new line has the same gradient m.
Worked exampleFind the equation of the line parallel to y = 3x + 2 that passes through (4, 5).
Parallel lines have the same gradient, so m = 3
y − 5 = 3(x − 4)
y = 3x − 12 + 5
y = 3x − 7

Condition for perpendicular lines

Two lines with gradients m1 and m2 are perpendicular if and only if m1 × m2 = -1, that is, m2 = -1 / m1 (negative reciprocals).
Worked exampleRoof line L1 passes through (-6, 2) and (-1, 4). Bracket line L2 passes through (3, 5) and (5, 0). Determine whether the bracket is perpendicular to the roof line.
Gradient of L1 = (4 − 2) / (-1 − (-6)) = 2 / 5
Gradient of L2 = (0 − 5) / (5 − 3) = -5 / 2
(2/5) × (-5/2) = -1
Since the product of the gradients is -1, L1 and L2 are perpendicular.

Equation of a line perpendicular to a given line

To find the equation of a line perpendicular to a line with gradient m, through a point (x1, y1): use y − y1 = (-1/m)(x − x1), since the new line has gradient -1/m.
Worked exampleA street is built perpendicular to an existing street with equation y = 0.5x + 3, passing through point B(-1, -0.2). Find the equation of the new street.
Gradient of existing street m = 0.5, so perpendicular gradient = -1 / 0.5 = -2
y − (-0.2) = -2(x − (-1))
y + 0.2 = -2x − 2
y = -2x − 2.2
