Combinations, nCr

nCr = n! / (r! × (n − r)!), where n! = n × (n − 1) × (n − 2) × ... × 2 × 1, and nCr counts the number of ways to choose r successes from n trials.
Worked exampleCalculate 6C2.
6C2 = 6! / (2! × 4!) = (6 × 5) / (2 × 1) = 30 / 2 = 15

Binomial probability distribution: P(X = x)

If X ~ B(n, p), then P(X = x) = nCx × px × (1 − p)n−x, for x = 0, 1, 2, ..., n, where n is the fixed number of independent trials, p is the constant probability of success on each trial, and x is the number of successes.
Worked exampleA multiple-choice quiz has 8 questions, each with 4 equally likely options. X is the number of correct guesses, so X ~ B(8, 0.25). Find P(X = 3).
P(X = 3) = 8C3 × 0.253 × 0.755
8C3 = 56, 0.253 = 0.015625, 0.755 = 0.2373046875
P(X = 3) = 56 × 0.015625 × 0.2373046875 = 0.208 (3 sf)

Cumulative binomial probability

P(X ≤ x) = P(X = 0) + P(X = 1) + ... + P(X = x), found by summing the individual binomial probabilities from 0 up to x.
Worked exampleX ~ B(5, 0.4). Find P(X ≤ 2).
P(X = 0) = 0.65 = 0.07776
P(X = 1) = 5C1 × 0.4 × 0.64 = 5 × 0.4 × 0.1296 = 0.2592
P(X = 2) = 5C2 × 0.42 × 0.63 = 10 × 0.16 × 0.216 = 0.3456
P(X ≤ 2) = 0.07776 + 0.2592 + 0.3456 = 0.683 (3 sf)

Mean of a binomial distribution

If X ~ B(n, p), then E(X) = μ = n × p.
Worked exampleX ~ B(20, 0.25). Find E(X).
E(X) = 20 × 0.25 = 5

Variance and standard deviation of a binomial distribution

If X ~ B(n, p), then Var(X) = n × p × (1 − p), and the standard deviation is √(Var(X)) = √(n × p × (1 − p)).
Worked exampleX ~ B(15, 0.6). Find the variance and standard deviation of X.
Var(X) = 15 × 0.6 × 0.4 = 3.6
Standard deviation = √3.6 = 1.90 (3 sf)
