The Pythagorean theorem

For a right-angled triangle, c2 = a2 + b2, where c is the length of the hypotenuse (the longest side, opposite the right angle) and a and b are the lengths of the other two sides.
Worked exampleIn right-angled triangle ABC, the right angle is at A, the hypotenuse BC = 15 cm and AC = 7 cm. Find the length of AB.
AB2 + AC2 = BC2
AB2 = 152 − 72 = 225 − 49 = 176
AB = √176
AB ≈ 13.3 cm (3 sf)

The converse of the Pythagorean theorem

If a triangle has sides a, b and c (c the longest side) and c2 = a2 + b2, then the triangle must be right-angled, with the right angle opposite side c.
Worked exampleDetermine whether a triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 9 cm is right-angled, and whether a triangle with sides 17 m, 144 m and 145 m is right-angled.
Triangle 1: the longest side is 9, so check 92 = 62 + 82
81 compared with 36 + 64 = 100
81 ≠ 100, so this triangle is not right-angled
Triangle 2: the longest side is 145, so check 1452 = 1442 + 172
21025 compared with 20736 + 289 = 21025
21025 = 21025, so this triangle is right-angled

Area of a right-angled triangle

Area = (1/2) × a × b, where a and b are the two sides that meet at the right angle (the base and height of the triangle).
Worked exampleA right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of 8.2 cm and one other side of length 4.3 cm. Find the area, correct to 1 dp.
First find the missing side using the Pythagorean theorem:
other side = √(8.22 − 4.32) = √(67.24 − 18.49) = √48.75 ≈ 6.9821 cm
Area = (1/2) × 4.3 × 6.9821
Area ≈ 15.0 cm2 (1 dp)

Trigonometric ratios (SOH–CAH–TOA)

For an acute angle A in a right-angled triangle: sin A = Opposite / Hypotenuse, cos A = Adjacent / Hypotenuse, tan A = Opposite / Adjacent, where Opposite is the side facing angle A, Adjacent is the side next to angle A (not the hypotenuse), and Hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle.
Worked exampleIn right-angled triangle ABC, the right angle is at B. The side opposite angle A, BC, is 6 cm, and the hypotenuse AC is 10 cm. Find the size of angle A.
sin A = Opposite / Hypotenuse = 6 / 10 = 0.6
A = sin−1(0.6)
A ≈ 36.9° (3 sf)

Finding an unknown side using a trigonometric ratio

If one side and one acute angle of a right-angled triangle are known, an unknown side can be found by writing the correct ratio (sin, cos or tan) and solving the resulting equation.
Worked exampleIn right-angled triangle FGH, the right angle is at G, FH = 14 cm (the hypotenuse) and angle FHG = 52°. Find the length of GH, the side adjacent to angle FHG.
cos(FHG) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
cos 52° = GH / 14
GH = 14 × cos 52°
GH ≈ 14 × 0.6157
GH ≈ 8.62 cm (3 sf)

Solving a right-angled triangle in context

In an applied right-angled triangle problem (for example, a ladder leaning against a wall), first identify the hypotenuse, the known angle, and the sides labelled Opposite and Adjacent relative to that angle, then apply SOH–CAH–TOA to find each required length.
Worked exampleA ladder [KM] is 8.5 m long and leans against a wall so that the angle between the ladder and the ground, angle LKM, is 30°, with the right angle at L (the foot of the wall). Find the horizontal distance KL from the wall, find how far up the wall the ladder reaches (LM), and find the maximum height the ladder can reach if it must not lean at more than 55° to the ground.
KL is adjacent to the 30° angle: KL = KM × cos 30° = 8.5 × 0.8660 ≈ 7.36 m (3 sf)
LM is opposite the 30° angle: LM = KM × sin 30° = 8.5 × 0.5 = 4.25 m
Maximum height at 55°: LMmax = KM × sin 55° = 8.5 × 0.8192 ≈ 6.96 m (3 sf)
