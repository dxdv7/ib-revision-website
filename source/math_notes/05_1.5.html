The Pythagorean theorem

For a right-angled triangle, \(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\), where \(c\) is the length of the hypotenuse (the longest side, opposite the right angle) and \(a\) and \(b\) are the lengths of the other two sides.
Worked exampleIn right-angled triangle ABC, the right angle is at A, the hypotenuse \(BC = 15\) cm and \(AC = 7\) cm. Find the length of AB.
\[AB^2 + AC^2 = BC^2\]\[AB^2 = 15^2 - 7^2 = 225 - 49 = 176\]\[AB = \sqrt{176}\]\[\boxed{AB \approx 13.3 \text{ cm (3 sf)}}\]

The converse of the Pythagorean theorem

If a triangle has sides \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) (\(c\) the longest side) and \(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\), then the triangle must be right-angled, with the right angle opposite side \(c\).
Worked exampleDetermine whether a triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 9 cm is right-angled, and whether a triangle with sides 17 m, 144 m and 145 m is right-angled.
Triangle 1: the longest side is 9, so check \(9^2 = 6^2 + 8^2\)
\[81 \text{ compared with } 36 + 64 = 100\]\[81 \ne 100\], so this triangle is not right-angled
Triangle 2: the longest side is 145, so check \(145^2 = 144^2 + 17^2\)
\[21025 \text{ compared with } 20736 + 289 = 21025\]\[21025 = 21025\], so this triangle is right-angled

Area of a right-angled triangle

\(\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times a \times b\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are the two sides that meet at the right angle (the base and height of the triangle).
Worked exampleA right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of 8.2 cm and one other side of length 4.3 cm. Find the area, correct to 1 dp.
First find the missing side using the Pythagorean theorem:
\[\text{other side} = \sqrt{8.2^2 - 4.3^2} = \sqrt{67.24 - 18.49} = \sqrt{48.75} \approx 6.9821 \text{ cm}\]\[\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 4.3 \times 6.9821\]\[\boxed{\text{Area} \approx 15.0 \text{ cm}^2 \text{ (1 dp)}}\]

Trigonometric ratios (SOH–CAH–TOA)

For an acute angle \(A\) in a right-angled triangle: \(\sin A = \dfrac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}\), \(\cos A = \dfrac{\text{Adjacent}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}\), \(\tan A = \dfrac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Adjacent}}\), where Opposite is the side facing angle \(A\), Adjacent is the side next to angle \(A\) (not the hypotenuse), and Hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle.
Worked exampleIn right-angled triangle ABC, the right angle is at B. The side opposite angle A, BC, is 6 cm, and the hypotenuse AC is 10 cm. Find the size of angle A.
\[\sin A = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Hypotenuse}} = \frac{6}{10} = 0.6\]\[A = \sin^{-1}(0.6)\]\[\boxed{A \approx 36.9^{\circ} \text{ (3 sf)}}\]

Finding an unknown side using a trigonometric ratio

If one side and one acute angle of a right-angled triangle are known, an unknown side can be found by writing the correct ratio (sin, cos or tan) and solving the resulting equation.
Worked exampleIn right-angled triangle FGH, the right angle is at G, \(FH = 14\) cm (the hypotenuse) and angle \(FHG = 52^{\circ}\). Find the length of GH, the side adjacent to angle FHG.
\[\cos(FHG) = \frac{\text{Adjacent}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}\]\[\cos 52^{\circ} = \frac{GH}{14}\]\[GH = 14 \times \cos 52^{\circ}\]\[GH \approx 14 \times 0.6157\]\[\boxed{GH \approx 8.62 \text{ cm (3 sf)}}\]

Solving a right-angled triangle in context

In an applied right-angled triangle problem (for example, a ladder leaning against a wall), first identify the hypotenuse, the known angle, and the sides labelled Opposite and Adjacent relative to that angle, then apply SOH–CAH–TOA to find each required length.
Worked exampleA ladder [KM] is 8.5 m long and leans against a wall so that the angle between the ladder and the ground, angle LKM, is \(30^{\circ}\), with the right angle at L (the foot of the wall). Find the horizontal distance KL from the wall, find how far up the wall the ladder reaches (LM), and find the maximum height the ladder can reach if it must not lean at more than \(55^{\circ}\) to the ground.
KL is adjacent to the \(30^{\circ}\) angle:
\[KL = KM \times \cos 30^{\circ} = 8.5 \times 0.8660 \approx 7.36 \text{ m (3 sf)}\]LM is opposite the \(30^{\circ}\) angle:
\[LM = KM \times \sin 30^{\circ} = 8.5 \times 0.5 = 4.25 \text{ m}\]Maximum height at \(55^{\circ}\):
\[LM_{max} = KM \times \sin 55^{\circ} = 8.5 \times 0.8192 \approx 6.96 \text{ m (3 sf)}\]
