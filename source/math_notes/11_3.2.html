Simple random sampling: probability of selection

In a simple random sample of size \(n\) taken from a population of size \(N\), every member has an equal probability of selection:
\[P(\text{selected}) = \frac{n}{N}\]
Worked exampleA company has 500 employees. A simple random sample of 25 employees is selected using a random number generator. Find the probability that a particular employee is selected.
\[P(\text{selected}) = \frac{n}{N} = \frac{25}{500}\]\[\boxed{P(\text{selected}) = 0.05}\]Final answer: the probability is 0.05, or 5%.

Systematic sampling interval

sampling interval
\[k = \frac{N}{n}\]A random starting point between 1 and \(k\) is chosen, then every \(k\)th member after that is selected.
Worked exampleA company has 1200 customers on its mailing list and wants a systematic sample of 40 customers. Find the sampling interval.
\[k = \frac{N}{n} = \frac{1200}{40}\]\[\boxed{k = 30}\]Final answer: the sampling interval is 30, so a random start between 1 and 30 is chosen, then every 30th customer after that is selected.

Stratified sampling allocation

\[\text{sample size from a stratum} = \left(\frac{\text{stratum size}}{\text{population size}}\right) \times \text{total sample size}\]
Worked exampleA school has 450 students: 180 in Grade 9, 150 in Grade 10, and 120 in Grade 11. A stratified sample of 60 students is required. Find the number selected from each grade.
\[\text{Grade 9: } \frac{180}{450} \times 60 = 24\]\[\text{Grade 10: } \frac{150}{450} \times 60 = 20\]\[\text{Grade 11: } \frac{120}{450} \times 60 = 16\]Check: \(24 + 20 + 16 = 60\)
Final answer: 24 students from Grade 9, 20 from Grade 10, and 16 from Grade 11.

Stratified sampling with rounding

Each stratum allocation \(\left(\frac{\text{stratum size}}{\text{population size}}\right) \times \text{total sample size}\) is rounded to the nearest whole number. Because of rounding, the rounded allocations may not sum to exactly the intended total sample size.
Worked exampleA population of 100 is divided into three strata: A has 45, B has 35, and C has 20. A stratified sample of 32 is required. Find the number selected from each stratum.
\[\text{A: } \frac{45}{100} \times 32 = 14.4 \text{, rounds to } 14\]\[\text{B: } \frac{35}{100} \times 32 = 11.2 \text{, rounds to } 11\]\[\text{C: } \frac{20}{100} \times 32 = 6.4 \text{, rounds to } 6\]Total after rounding: \(14 + 11 + 6 = 31\)
Final answer: 14 from A, 11 from B, and 6 from C, giving a total of 31, one less than the intended 32 because each value was rounded down.

Quota sampling allocation

In quota sampling, quotas are set proportionally in the same way as stratified sampling,
\[\text{quota for a group} = \left(\frac{\text{group size}}{\text{population size}}\right) \times \text{total sample size}\]but the individuals within each quota are not chosen randomly, so the sample can be biased.
Worked exampleA school canteen has 250 boys and 300 girls (population 550). The canteen manager wants feedback from a quota sample of 55 students, interviewing whichever boys and girls arrive first until each quota is filled. Find the size of each quota.
\[\text{Boys: } \frac{250}{550} \times 55 = 25\]\[\text{Girls: } \frac{300}{550} \times 55 = 30\]Check: \(25 + 30 = 55\)
Final answer: a quota of 25 boys and 30 girls, but because the students are not chosen randomly this is not a stratified (random) sample and may be biased.
