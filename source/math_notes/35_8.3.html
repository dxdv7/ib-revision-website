Chi-squared goodness of fit test: hypotheses and expected frequencies

A goodness of fit test checks whether observed data for a single categorical variable matches a claimed distribution. H0: the data follows the claimed distribution. H1: the data does not follow the claimed distribution. The expected frequency for category i is Ei = n × pi, where n is the total number of observations and pi is the claimed probability of category i.
Worked exampleA standard six-sided die is rolled n = 120 times to test whether it is fair.
H0: the die is fair (each face has probability 1/6)
H1: the die is not fair
Under H0, expected frequency for each face = 120 × 1/6 = 20

Chi-squared test statistic and degrees of freedom

χ2 = ∑ [(Oi − Ei)2 / Ei], where Oi is the observed frequency and Ei is the expected frequency of category i, summed over all categories. Degrees of freedom v = k − 1, where k is the number of categories (not (rows − 1)(columns − 1), which applies only to a two-way test for independence).
Worked exampleContinuing the die example, the observed frequencies for the six faces are 18, 15, 24, 20, 25, 18 (n = 120), with expected frequency 20 for each face.
χ2 = (18−20)2/20 + (15−20)2/20 + (24−20)2/20 + (20−20)2/20 + (25−20)2/20 + (18−20)2/20
= 0.2 + 1.25 + 0.8 + 0 + 1.25 + 0.2
= 3.7
Degrees of freedom v = 6 − 1 = 5

Decision rule: critical value and p-value

If χ2calc > critical value (for the given significance level and degrees of freedom), reject H0. If χ2calc ≤ critical value, do not reject H0. Equivalently, reject H0 if the p-value is less than the significance level.
Worked exampleContinuing the die example, at the 5% significance level with v = 5, the critical value is 11.070.
χ2calc = 3.7, and 3.7 < 11.070, so do not reject H0.
Conclusion: there is insufficient evidence to suggest the die is unfair.

Goodness of fit test for a claimed binomial distribution

The same test applies to any claimed distribution (uniform, binomial, normal, etc.) — only the calculation of the expected frequencies Ei = n × pi changes, using the probabilities pi from the claimed distribution.
Worked exampleHagar tosses three fair coins n = 200 times and records the number of heads (0, 1, 2 or 3) each time. She tests, at the 5% significance level, whether the results follow a binomial distribution B(3, 0.5).
Under B(3, 0.5): P(0) = 0.125, P(1) = 0.375, P(2) = 0.375, P(3) = 0.125
Expected frequencies: E0 = 25, E1 = 75, E2 = 75, E3 = 25
Observed frequencies: O0 = 28, O1 = 67, O2 = 83, O3 = 22
χ2 = (28−25)2/25 + (67−75)2/75 + (83−75)2/75 + (22−25)2/25
= 0.36 + 0.8533... + 0.8533... + 0.36
= 2.4267 (4 dp)
Degrees of freedom v = 4 − 1 = 3, critical value at 5% is 7.815
Since 2.4267 < 7.815, do not reject H0: the data is consistent with a B(3, 0.5) distribution
