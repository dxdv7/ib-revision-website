Equation of the tangent to a curve at a point

The tangent to the curve y = f(x) at the point (x1, y1) has gradient m = f′(x1), and its equation is y − y1 = f′(x1)(x − x1), where y1 = f(x1).
Worked exampleFind the equation of the tangent to the curve f(x) = 2x2 − 4 at the point where x = 3.
y1 = f(3) = 2(3)2 − 4 = 18 − 4 = 14, so the point is (3, 14)
f′(x) = 4x, so f′(3) = 4(3) = 12
y − 14 = 12(x − 3)
y = 12x − 36 + 14 = 12x − 22

Equation of the normal to a curve at a point

The normal to the curve y = f(x) at the point (x1, y1) is perpendicular to the tangent there. Since perpendicular gradients multiply to give −1, the normal has gradient −1 / f′(x1) (provided f′(x1) ≠ 0), and its equation is y − y1 = [−1 / f′(x1)](x − x1).
Worked exampleFind the equation of the normal to the curve f(x) = 2x3 + 3x − 2 at the point where x = 1.
y1 = f(1) = 2(1)3 + 3(1) − 2 = 2 + 3 − 2 = 3, so the point is (1, 3)
f′(x) = 6x2 + 3, so f′(1) = 6(1)2 + 3 = 9
Gradient of normal = −1 / 9
y − 3 = (−1 / 9)(x − 1)
y = (−1 / 9)x + 1/9 + 3 = (−1 / 9)x + 28/9

Finding both the tangent and normal at the same point

At a point (x1, y1) on y = f(x), first find y1 = f(x1) and the gradient f′(x1). The tangent uses gradient f′(x1): y − y1 = f′(x1)(x − x1). The normal uses gradient −1 / f′(x1): y − y1 = [−1 / f′(x1)](x − x1).
Worked exampleFind the equations of the tangent and normal to the curve y = x4 − 6x + 3 at the point where x = 2.
y1 = (2)4 − 6(2) + 3 = 16 − 12 + 3 = 7, so the point is (2, 7)
dy/dx = 4x3 − 6, so at x = 2: 4(2)3 − 6 = 32 − 6 = 26
Tangent: y − 7 = 26(x − 2) ⇒ y = 26x − 45
Normal gradient = −1 / 26
Normal: y − 7 = (−1 / 26)(x − 2) ⇒ y = (−1 / 26)x + 1/13 + 7 = (−1 / 26)x + 92/13

Finding an unknown coefficient from a given tangent gradient

If the gradient of the tangent to f(x) at a given point is known, set f′(x1) equal to that gradient to form an equation in any unknown coefficients, then solve. Substitute x1 into f(x) to find any remaining unknown, such as the y-coordinate.
Worked exampleThe gradient of the tangent to the curve f(x) = ax2 + 3x − 1 at the point (2, b) is 7. Find the values of a and b.
f′(x) = 2ax + 3
f′(2) = 4a + 3 = 7 ⇒ 4a = 4 ⇒ a = 1
f(2) = (1)(2)2 + 3(2) − 1 = 4 + 6 − 1 = 9, so b = 9

Finding an unknown coefficient from a given normal gradient

If the gradient of the normal at a given point is known, the gradient of the tangent there is the negative reciprocal of it. Set f′(x1) equal to this tangent gradient to solve for any unknown coefficients, then substitute x1 into f(x) to find any remaining unknown.
Worked exampleThe gradient of the normal to the curve f(x) = kx3 − 2x + 1 at the point (1, b) is −1/4. Find the values of k and b.
Since the normal gradient is −1/4, the tangent gradient is 4
f′(x) = 3kx2 − 2
f′(1) = 3k − 2 = 4 ⇒ 3k = 6 ⇒ k = 2
f(1) = 2(1)3 − 2(1) + 1 = 2 − 2 + 1 = 1, so b = 1

Intersection point of two normal lines

To find where two normal lines to a curve meet, find the equation of each normal separately using y − y1 = [−1 / f′(x1)](x − x1) at each point, then solve the two linear equations simultaneously for x and y.
Worked exampleFind the point where the normals to f(x) = x2 at x = 2 and x = −2 meet.
At x = 2: f(2) = 4, f′(x) = 2x so f′(2) = 4; normal gradient = −1/4
Normal 1: y − 4 = (−1/4)(x − 2) ⇒ y = (−1/4)x + 4.5
At x = −2: f(−2) = 4, f′(−2) = −4; normal gradient = 1/4
Normal 2: y − 4 = (1/4)(x + 2) ⇒ y = (1/4)x + 4.5
By symmetry both lines meet where x = 0: y = 4.5
The normals meet at (0, 4.5)
