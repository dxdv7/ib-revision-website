Population variance and standard deviation

Variance = σ2 = [∑(x − mean)2] / n, where x is each data value, mean is the average of all values, and n is the number of values. Standard deviation = σ = √(variance).
Worked exampleFind the variance and standard deviation of: 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9.
n = 8, mean = (2+4+4+4+5+5+7+9) / 8 = 40 / 8 = 5
Deviations from the mean: −3, −1, −1, −1, 0, 0, 2, 4
Squared deviations: 9, 1, 1, 1, 0, 0, 4, 16, sum = 32
Variance = 32 / 8 = 4
Standard deviation = √4 = 2

Interquartile range (IQR)

IQR = Q3 − Q1, where Q1 is the lower quartile (median of the lower half of the ordered data) and Q3 is the upper quartile (median of the upper half). The IQR measures the spread of the middle 50% of the data.
Worked exampleFind the IQR of: 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17 (already ordered, n = 9).
Median (5th value) = 9
Lower half: 3, 5, 7, 8, so Q1 = (5 + 7) / 2 = 6
Upper half: 11, 13, 15, 17, so Q3 = (13 + 15) / 2 = 14
IQR = Q3 − Q1 = 14 − 6 = 8

Five-number summary and the box-and-whisker plot

The five-number summary is: minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum. A box-and-whisker plot draws a box from Q1 to Q3 (with a line at the median) and whiskers out to the minimum and maximum (non-outlier) values.
Worked exampleGive the five-number summary for: 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17.
Minimum = 3
Q1 = 6
Median = 9
Q3 = 14
Maximum = 17
Five-number summary: 3, 6, 9, 14, 17

Identifying outliers

A value is an outlier if it is below Q1 − 1.5 × IQR (lower boundary) or above Q3 + 1.5 × IQR (upper boundary).
Worked exampleTest the data 12, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19, 20, 21, 50 for outliers (n = 9, already ordered).
Median (5th value) = 18
Lower half: 12, 14, 15, 16, so Q1 = (14 + 15) / 2 = 14.5
Upper half: 19, 20, 21, 50, so Q3 = (20 + 21) / 2 = 20.5
IQR = 20.5 − 14.5 = 6
Lower boundary = 14.5 − 1.5 × 6 = 14.5 − 9 = 5.5
Upper boundary = 20.5 + 1.5 × 6 = 20.5 + 9 = 29.5
Since 50 > 29.5, the value 50 is an outlier.
