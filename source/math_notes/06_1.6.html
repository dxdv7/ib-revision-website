Angle of elevation

The angle of elevation, \(\theta\), is the angle above the horizontal from an observer up to an object. Using right-angled trigonometry, \(\tan \theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \dfrac{\text{height}}{\text{horizontal distance}}\).
Worked exampleAn observer stands 40 m from the base of a tree. The angle of elevation to the top of the tree is \(32^{\circ}\). Find the height of the tree.
\[\tan 32^{\circ} = \frac{\text{height}}{40}\]\[\text{height} = 40 \times \tan 32^{\circ}\]\[\text{height} = 40 \times 0.6249\]\[\boxed{\text{height} \approx 25.0 \text{ m (3 sf)}}\]

Angle of depression

The angle of depression, \(\theta\), is the angle below the horizontal from an observer down to an object. It is measured using the same right-angled trigonometry: \(\tan \theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \dfrac{\text{height (of the observer above the object)}}{\text{horizontal distance}}\). The angle of depression from the observer equals the angle of elevation from the object back to the observer (alternate angles between parallel horizontal lines).
Worked exampleFrom the top of a cliff, the angle of depression to a boat is \(25^{\circ}\). The boat is 200 m from the base of the cliff. Find the height of the cliff.
\[\tan 25^{\circ} = \frac{\text{height}}{200}\]\[\text{height} = 200 \times \tan 25^{\circ}\]\[\text{height} = 200 \times 0.4663\]\[\boxed{\text{height} \approx 93.3 \text{ m (3 sf)}}\]

Combining elevation and depression from one point

When an observer at height \(h\) above the ground sees the base of an object at an angle of depression \(\theta_1\) and the top of the object at an angle of elevation \(\theta_2\), first use \(\tan \theta_1 = \dfrac{h}{d}\) to find the horizontal distance \(d\), then use \(\tan \theta_2 = \dfrac{x}{d}\) to find the extra height \(x\) above the observer's eye level. The total height of the object is \(x + h\).
Worked exampleFrom a window 6.5 m above the ground, the angle of depression to the base of a building across the street is \(18^{\circ}\), and the angle of elevation to the top of the same building is \(55^{\circ}\). Find the height of the building.
Horizontal distance:
\[\tan 18^{\circ} = \frac{6.5}{d}\]\[d = \frac{6.5}{\tan 18^{\circ}} = 20.0049 \text{ m (unrounded)}\]Extra height above window:
\[\tan 55^{\circ} = \frac{x}{d}\]\[x = 20.0049 \times \tan 55^{\circ} = 28.57 \text{ m (2 dp)}\]\[\boxed{\text{Total height} = 28.57 + 6.5 = 35.07 \text{ m (2 dp)}}\] — keep the unrounded value of \(d\) in the second step to avoid losing accuracy
