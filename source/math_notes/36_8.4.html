Hypotheses for the t-test

The t-test compares the means, μ1 and μ2, of two data sets measuring the same quantity. The null hypothesis is always H0: μ1 = μ2. For a two-tailed test, H1: μ1 ≠ μ2. For a one-tailed test, H1: μ1 > μ2 or H1: μ1 < μ2.
Worked exampleMaya wants to test whether a new fertiliser increases the mean height of tomato plants compared with the standard fertiliser. Let μ1 be the mean height using the new fertiliser and μ2 the mean height using the standard fertiliser. Write down the null and alternative hypotheses.
Because the claim is directional (“increases”), this is a one-tailed test.
H0: μ1 = μ2
H1: μ1 > μ2

Degrees of freedom

For a two-sample t-test (pooled), the degrees of freedom are v = n1 + n2 − 2, where n1 and n2 are the sizes of the two samples.
Worked exampleA t-test compares a sample of 14 students from Class A with a sample of 12 students from Class B. Find the degrees of freedom.
v = n1 + n2 − 2 = 14 + 12 − 2 = 24
The test has 24 degrees of freedom.

The t-test statistic

The pooled two-sample t-statistic is t = (x-bar1 − x-bar2) / (sp × √(1/n1 + 1/n2)), where the pooled variance is sp2 = [(n1 − 1)s12 + (n2 − 1)s22] / (n1 + n2 − 2). In practice this is found directly using a GDC.
Worked exampleSample A: n1 = 8, mean x-bar1 = 72.5, standard deviation s1 = 6.2. Sample B: n2 = 10, mean x-bar2 = 68.1, standard deviation s2 = 7.4. Find the t-statistic.
sp2 = [(7)(6.22) + (9)(7.42)] / 16 = [7(38.44) + 9(54.76)] / 16 = [269.08 + 492.84] / 16 = 761.92 / 16 = 47.62
sp = √47.62 ≈ 6.90
t = (72.5 − 68.1) / (6.90 × √(1/8 + 1/10)) = 4.4 / (6.90 × 0.4743) = 4.4 / 3.273 ≈ 1.34
Degrees of freedom = 8 + 10 − 2 = 16

Using the p-value to reach a conclusion

Compare the p-value given by the GDC with the significance level (commonly 1%, 5% or 10%). If p > significance level, accept (do not reject) H0. If p ≤ significance level, reject H0 in favour of H1.
Worked exampleA t-test at the 5% significance level gives a p-value of 0.0392. State the conclusion.
0.0392 < 0.05, so the null hypothesis is rejected.
Conclusion: there is significant evidence that the two population means are different.
