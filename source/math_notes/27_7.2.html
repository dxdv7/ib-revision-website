Multiplying along the branches of a tree diagram

The probability of one specific combined outcome (one full path through a tree diagram) equals the product of the probabilities written on each branch followed along that path.
Worked exampleA bag contains 5 red and 3 blue counters. Two counters are drawn one after another without replacement. Find the probability that both counters drawn are red.
First branch: P(red) = 5/8
Second branch (one red already removed): P(red) = 4/7
Probability along this path = 5/8 × 4/7 = 20/56 = 5/14 = 0.357 (3 sf)

Adding branch-end probabilities for alternative routes

When an outcome can be reached by more than one path through a tree diagram, its overall probability equals the sum of the branch-end probabilities of every path that leads to that outcome.
Worked exampleUsing the same bag (5 red, 3 blue, drawn without replacement), find the probability that exactly one red and one blue counter are drawn (in either order).
Path red-then-blue: 5/8 × 3/7 = 15/56
Path blue-then-red: 3/8 × 5/7 = 15/56
Total probability = 15/56 + 15/56 = 30/56 = 15/28 = 0.536 (3 sf)

Reading probabilities directly from a Venn diagram

Each closed region of a Venn diagram holds a count (or probability) of outcomes. The probability of landing in any region, or combination of regions, is the total count in those regions divided by the count in the whole universal set, n(U): P(region) = (number of outcomes in the region) / n(U).
Worked exampleIn a class of 30 students, a Venn diagram shows 13 students studying French only, 7 studying Spanish only, 5 studying both, and the rest studying neither. Find the probability that a randomly selected student studies French or Spanish (or both), and the probability that a student studies neither.
Students studying neither = 30 − 13 − 7 − 5 = 5
Students studying French or Spanish = 13 + 7 + 5 = 25 (every region except "neither")
P(French or Spanish) = 25/30 = 5/6 = 0.833 (3 sf)
P(neither) = 5/30 = 1/6 = 0.167 (3 sf)

Reading a sample space diagram (two-way grid)

A sample space diagram lists every possible combined outcome in a grid. The probability of an event is the number of grid cells satisfying the event divided by the total number of cells in the grid.
Worked exampleTwo fair six-sided dice are thrown and their scores added. Using the 6 × 6 grid of equally likely outcomes, find the probability that the total is 8.
Total number of cells = 6 × 6 = 36
Cells giving a total of 8: (2,6), (3,5), (4,4), (5,3), (6,2), so 5 cells
P(total = 8) = 5/36 = 0.139 (3 sf)
