Area of a quadrilateral by splitting into two triangles

To find the area of a quadrilateral ABCD, draw a diagonal (for example AC) to split it into two triangles. Find the area of each triangle using Area = (1/2) × (side) × (side) × sin(included angle), then add the two areas together: Area of ABCD = Area of triangle ABC + Area of triangle ACD.
Worked exampleQuadrilateral ABCD has diagonal AC = 20 m. In triangle ABC, AB = 15 m and angle BAC = 40°. In triangle ACD, AD = 17 m and angle CAD = 32°. Find the total area of ABCD.
Area of ABC = (1/2) × 15 × 20 × sin40° = 150 × 0.6428 = 96.4 m²
Area of ACD = (1/2) × 17 × 20 × sin32° = 170 × 0.5299 = 90.1 m²
Total area = 96.4 + 90.1 = 186.5
Total area ≈ 187 m² (3 s.f.)

Area of a regular polygon using triangles from the centre

A regular polygon with n sides, inscribed in a circle of radius r, can be split into n congruent isosceles triangles from the centre, each with two sides of length r and an included central angle of 360° / n. Total area = n × (1/2) × r × r × sin(360° / n).
Worked exampleA regular hexagon is inscribed in a circle of radius 8 cm. Find the area of the hexagon.
The hexagon splits into 6 congruent triangles, each with central angle 360° / 6 = 60°.
Area of one triangle = (1/2) × 8 × 8 × sin60° = 32 × 0.8660 = 27.7 cm²
Total area = 6 × 27.7 = 166
Total area ≈ 166 cm² (3 s.f.)

Composite area: sine rule to find a side, then the area formula

When two angles and a side of a triangle are known (AAS), first use the sine rule to find the length of a second side, then substitute both sides and the angle between them into Area = (1/2) × (side) × (side) × sin(included angle).
Worked exampleAn isosceles roof truss forms triangle PQR, where PQ = RQ, angle P = 58° and PR = 14 m. Find the area of triangle PQR.
Since PQ = RQ, the angles opposite them are equal: angle R = angle P = 58°.
Angle Q = 180° − 58° − 58° = 64°
Use the sine rule to find PQ: PQ / sinR = PR / sinQ
PQ = 14 × sin58° / sin64° = 14 × 0.8480 / 0.8988 = 13.2 m
Area = (1/2) × PR × PQ × sinP = 0.5 × 14 × 13.2 × sin58°
Area = 92.4 × 0.8480
Area ≈ 78.4 m² (3 s.f.)

Composite area: cosine rule to find an angle, then the area formula

When all three sides of a triangle are known (SSS), first use the cosine rule to find one angle, then substitute the two adjacent sides and that angle into Area = (1/2) × (side) × (side) × sin(included angle).
Worked exampleA triangular plot has sides AB = 18 m, AC = 9 m and BC = 14 m. Find its area.
Use the cosine rule to find angle C (between sides AC and BC): cosC = (AC² + BC² − AB²) / (2 × AC × BC)
cosC = (9² + 14² − 18²) / (2 × 9 × 14) = −47 / 252 = −0.1865
C = cos−1(−0.1865) = 100.7°
Area = (1/2) × AC × BC × sinC = 0.5 × 9 × 14 × sin100.7°
Area = 63 × 0.9825
Area ≈ 61.9 m² (3 s.f.)

Composite area applied to a real-world quantity

In an applied problem, first find the triangle’s area (using the cosine or sine rule as needed, followed by the area formula), then use that area as one step in a further calculation, such as a proportion or rate.
Worked exampleA gardener has a triangular plot with AB = 22 m, AC = 16 m and BC = 27 m. Seeds cost 0.5 kg per 150 m² of coverage. Find the mass of seeds needed to cover the whole plot.
Use the cosine rule to find angle A: cosA = (AB² + AC² − BC²) / (2 × AB × AC)
cosA = (22² + 16² − 27²) / (2 × 22 × 16) = 11 / 704 = 0.01563
A = cos−1(0.01563) = 89.1°
Area = (1/2) × AB × AC × sinA = 0.5 × 22 × 16 × sin89.1° = 176 × 0.9999
Area ≈ 176 m² (3 s.f.)
Mass of seeds = (176 / 150) × 0.5 = 0.587 kg (3 s.f.)
