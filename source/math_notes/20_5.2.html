Equation of a linear model

A linear model has the form y = mx + c (or f(x) = mx + c), where m is the gradient (the constant rate of change of y with respect to x) and c is the y-intercept (the value of y when x = 0).
Worked exampleIn a chemistry experiment, a liquid is heated. At the start (t = 0 minutes) its temperature is 20°C, and it heats up at a constant rate of 30°C per minute. Find a linear model for T in terms of t.
Here m = 30 (the rate of change) and c = 20 (the initial temperature).
The linear model is T = 30t + 20.

Finding the gradient from two points

The gradient between two points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) on a line is m = (y2 − y1) / (x2 − x1).
Worked exampleA line representing the temperature model passes through (0, 20) and (2, 80). Find the gradient and the equation of the line.
m = (80 − 20) / (2 − 0) = 60 / 2 = 30
Since the line passes through (0, 20), c = 20.
The equation is T = 30t + 20.

Using a linear model to make predictions

To predict a value of y, substitute the known x-value into y = mx + c. To find the x-value that produces a given y, substitute y and solve the resulting linear equation for x.
Worked exampleUsing the model T = 30t + 20, predict the temperature after 5 minutes, and find the time at which the temperature reaches 200°C.
T(5) = 30(5) + 20 = 150 + 20 = 170°C
200 = 30t + 20
180 = 30t
t = 6 minutes

Direct variation (proportional linear models)

When y is directly proportional to x, the linear model has the form y = mx (the line passes through the origin, so c = 0), where m = y / x is the constant of proportionality.
Worked exampleA conversion graph shows that 20 pounds is equivalent to 9 kg. Find a model k(x) for the number of kilograms, x, given a mass in pounds, and use it to convert 30 pounds to kilograms.
m = 9 / 20 = 0.45
The model is k(x) = 0.45x.
k(30) = 0.45 × 30 = 13.5 kg

Domain and range of a linear model

Because linear models represent real-world quantities, their domain and range are often restricted to values that make sense in context (for example, time or an amount cannot be negative, and a process may stop once it reaches a maximum or minimum value).
Worked exampleA tank holds 1000 litres of water and drains at a constant rate of 20 litres per minute, modelled by W(t) = −20t + 1000, where t is the time in minutes. Find a suitable domain and range for this model.
The tank starts full at t = 0 and becomes empty when W(t) = 0:
0 = −20t + 1000
t = 50
The model is only valid for 0 ≤ t ≤ 50, with corresponding range 0 ≤ W ≤ 1000, since the tank cannot hold a negative amount of water and t cannot be negative.
