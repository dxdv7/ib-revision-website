The sine rule – finding a side

For any triangle ABC, where \(a\), \(b\), \(c\) are the side lengths opposite angles \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) respectively:
\[\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}\]Use this form when you know two angles and one side (AAS or ASA) and need to find another side.
Worked exampleIn triangle ABC, \(a = 10\) cm, \(A = 40^{\circ}\) and \(B = 60^{\circ}\). Find side \(b\).
\[\frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{a}{\sin A}\]\[b = \frac{a \times \sin B}{\sin A} = \frac{10 \times \sin 60^{\circ}}{\sin 40^{\circ}}\]\[b = \frac{10 \times 0.8660}{0.6428}\]\[\boxed{b = 13.5 \text{ cm (3 s.f.)}}\]

The sine rule – finding an angle

Rearranged for angles:
\[\frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{\sin B}{b} = \frac{\sin C}{c}\]Use this form when you know two sides and an angle opposite one of them (SSA) and need to find another angle.
Worked exampleIn triangle ABC, \(a = 12\) cm, \(b = 9\) cm and \(A = 55^{\circ}\), with \(a > b\). Find angle \(B\).
\[\sin B = \frac{b \times \sin A}{a} = \frac{9 \times \sin 55^{\circ}}{12}\]\[\sin B = \frac{9 \times 0.8192}{12} = 0.6144\]\[\boxed{B = \sin^{-1}(0.6144) = 37.9^{\circ} \text{ (3 s.f.)}}\]Since \(a > b\), \(B\) must be acute, so this is the only solution.

The ambiguous case of the sine rule

When using the sine rule to find an angle from two sides and a non-included angle (SSA), if the known side opposite the given angle is shorter than the other given side (specifically when \(b \sin A < a < b\)), two different triangles can be formed. Both the calculator value \(B\) and its supplement \((180^{\circ} - B)\) may be valid solutions, provided the resulting angle sum is still less than \(180^{\circ}\).
Worked exampleIn triangle ABC, \(a = 7\) cm, \(b = 10\) cm and \(A = 40^{\circ}\). Find all possible values of angle \(B\).
\[\sin B = \frac{b \times \sin A}{a} = \frac{10 \times \sin 40^{\circ}}{7} = 0.9184\]\[B_1 = \sin^{-1}(0.9184) = 66.7^{\circ} \text{ (3 s.f.)}\]\[B_2 = 180^{\circ} - 66.7^{\circ} = 113^{\circ} \text{ (3 s.f.)}\]Check: \(40^{\circ} + 113^{\circ} = 153^{\circ} < 180^{\circ}\), so both triangles are valid.
\[\boxed{B = 66.7^{\circ} \text{ or } B = 113^{\circ}}\]

The cosine rule – finding a side

For triangle ABC:
\[a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc \cos A\]and the equivalent forms for \(b^2\) and \(c^2\). Use this form when you know two sides and the included angle between them (SAS) and need to find the third side.
Worked exampleIn triangle ABC, \(b = 8\) cm, \(c = 10\) cm and \(A = 55^{\circ}\). Find side \(a\).
\[a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc \cos A\]\[a^2 = 8^2 + 10^2 - 2(8)(10) \cos 55^{\circ}\]\[a^2 = 64 + 100 - 160 \times 0.5736 = 72.2\]\[\boxed{a = \sqrt{72.2} = 8.50 \text{ cm (3 s.f.)}}\]

The cosine rule – finding an angle

Rearranged for angles:
\[\cos A = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc}\]Use this form when you know all three sides (SSS) and need to find any angle.
Worked exampleIn triangle ABC, \(a = 9\) cm, \(b = 7\) cm and \(c = 5\) cm. Find angle \(A\).
\[\cos A = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc} = \frac{7^2 + 5^2 - 9^2}{2 \times 7 \times 5}\]\[\cos A = \frac{49 + 25 - 81}{70} = \frac{-7}{70} = -0.100\]\[\boxed{A = \cos^{-1}(-0.100) = 95.7^{\circ} \text{ (3 s.f.)}}\]

Area of a triangle

The area of any triangle ABC is given by
\[\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}bc \sin A\]or equivalently \(\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ac \sin B\), or \(\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab \sin C\), where the angle used is always the one included between the two sides used. Use this formula when two sides and the included angle are known (SAS).
Worked exampleA triangular plot of land has sides \(a = 10\) m and \(b = 14\) m with an included angle \(C = 48^{\circ}\). Find its area.
\[\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab \sin C = 0.5 \times 10 \times 14 \times \sin 48^{\circ}\]\[\text{Area} = 70 \times 0.7431\]\[\boxed{\text{Area} = 52.0 \text{ m}^2 \text{ (3 s.f.)}}\]

Finding a side from the area of a triangle

If the area and one angle of a triangle are known together with one adjacent side, the area formula \(\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab \sin C\) can be rearranged to solve for the unknown side:
\[a = \frac{2 \times \text{Area}}{b \sin C}\]
Worked exampleThe area of triangle ABC is \(29.6 \text{ cm}^2\), \(B = 25^{\circ}\) and \(BC = 7\) cm. Find the length of side AB.
\[\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times BC \times AB \times \sin B\]\[29.6 = 0.5 \times 7 \times AB \times \sin 25^{\circ}\]\[AB = \frac{2 \times 29.6}{7 \times \sin 25^{\circ}} = \frac{59.2}{2.958}\]\[\boxed{AB = 20.0 \text{ cm (3 s.f.)}}\]
