The mean point and the line of best fit by eye

Mean point \((\bar{x}, \bar{y})\), where
\[\bar{x} = \frac{\Sigma x}{n} \qquad \bar{y} = \frac{\Sigma y}{n}\]with \(n\) the number of data pairs. A line of best fit drawn by eye should be drawn through the mean point, with roughly equal numbers of data points lying above the line and below it.
Worked exampleThe table shows the daily temperature \(x\) (°C) and the number of cold drinks sold \(y\) (in tens) at a kiosk over six days: \(x = 18, 20, 22, 24, 26, 28\); \(y = 18, 24, 24, 30, 32, 34\). Find the mean point.
\[\bar{x} = \frac{18 + 20 + 22 + 24 + 26 + 28}{6} = \frac{138}{6} = 23\]\[\bar{y} = \frac{18 + 24 + 24 + 30 + 32 + 34}{6} = \frac{162}{6} = 27\]\[\boxed{(\bar{x}, \bar{y}) = (23, 27)}\]A line of best fit for this data should be drawn through \((23, 27)\), balancing the points above and below it.

Residuals

\[\text{Residual} = y_{\text{observed}} - y_{\text{predicted}}\]where \(y_{\text{predicted}}\) is found by substituting the data point's \(x\)-value into the equation of a line of best fit. A positive residual means the data point lies above the line; a negative residual means it lies below the line.
Worked exampleUsing the kiosk data above, a line of best fit drawn through the mean point \((23, 27)\) has equation \(y = 1.5x - 7.5\). Find the residual for the day when \(x = 20\) and the observed value is \(y = 24\).
\[y_{\text{predicted}} = 1.5(20) - 7.5 = 30 - 7.5 = 22.5\]\[\text{Residual} = y_{\text{observed}} - y_{\text{predicted}} = 24 - 22.5 = \boxed{1.5}\]Since the residual is positive, this data point lies above the line of best fit.

The least-squares criterion

Sum of squares of the residuals,
\[SSR = \Sigma (y_{\text{observed}} - y_{\text{predicted}})^2\]for all data points. The least-squares criterion states that the best-fitting line for a set of data is the one that makes \(SSR\) as small as possible. Given two candidate lines of best fit for the same data, the line with the smaller \(SSR\) is the better fit.
Worked exampleTwo students each draw a line of best fit through the mean point \((23, 27)\) of the kiosk data (\(x = 18, 20, 22, 24, 26, 28\); \(y = 18, 24, 24, 30, 32, 34\)): Line A: \(y = 1.5x - 7.5\), and Line B: \(y = 1.3x - 2.9\). Use the least-squares criterion to decide which line fits the data better.
Line A predictions:
\[x=18 \rightarrow 19.5, \quad x=20 \rightarrow 22.5, \quad x=22 \rightarrow 25.5, \quad x=24 \rightarrow 28.5, \quad x=26 \rightarrow 31.5, \quad x=28 \rightarrow 34.5\]Line A residuals:
\[-1.5, \ 1.5, \ -1.5, \ 1.5, \ 0.5, \ -0.5\]Line A squared residuals:
\[2.25, \ 2.25, \ 2.25, \ 2.25, \ 0.25, \ 0.25\]\[\boxed{SSR(A) = 9.5}\]Line B predictions:
\[x=18 \rightarrow 20.5, \quad x=20 \rightarrow 23.1, \quad x=22 \rightarrow 25.7, \quad x=24 \rightarrow 28.3, \quad x=26 \rightarrow 30.9, \quad x=28 \rightarrow 33.5\]Line B residuals:
\[-2.5, \ 0.9, \ -1.7, \ 1.7, \ 1.1, \ 0.5\]Line B squared residuals:
\[6.25, \ 0.81, \ 2.89, \ 2.89, \ 1.21, \ 0.25\]\[\boxed{SSR(B) = 14.3}\]Since \(SSR(A) = 9.5\) is smaller than \(SSR(B) = 14.3\), Line A is the better fit for this data.
