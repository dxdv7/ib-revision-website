Pearson’s product-moment correlation coefficient (r)

r = Sxy / √(Sxx × Syy), where Sxy = sum of (x − x-bar)(y − y-bar), Sxx = sum of (x − x-bar)2, Syy = sum of (y − y-bar)2, and x-bar, y-bar are the means of x and y. The value of r lies between −1 and 1 and measures the strength and direction of the linear correlation between x and y.
Worked exampleFive students recorded the number of hours they revised (x) and their test score out of 10 (y): x = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5; y = 2, 4, 5, 4, 6. Find r.
x-bar = (1+2+3+4+5)/5 = 3. y-bar = (2+4+5+4+6)/5 = 4.2
Sxy = (−2)(−2.2) + (−1)(−0.2) + (0)(0.8) + (1)(−0.2) + (2)(1.8) = 4.4 + 0.2 + 0 − 0.2 + 3.6 = 8.0
Sxx = (−2)2 + (−1)2 + 02 + 12 + 22 = 4 + 1 + 0 + 1 + 4 = 10
Syy = (−2.2)2 + (−0.2)2 + 0.82 + (−0.2)2 + 1.82 = 4.84 + 0.04 + 0.64 + 0.04 + 3.24 = 8.8
r = 8.0 / √(10 × 8.8) = 8.0 / √88 = 8.0 / 9.381 ≈ 0.853
This is a strong, positive linear correlation between revision hours and test score.

Covariance

Covariance = Sxy / n = [sum of (x − x-bar)(y − y-bar)] / n, where n is the number of data pairs. A positive covariance means the two variables tend to increase together; a negative covariance means one tends to increase as the other decreases.
Worked exampleUsing the revision data above, where n = 5 and Sxy = 8.0, find the covariance.
Covariance = 8.0 / 5 = 1.6
The positive value confirms that test score tends to rise as revision hours rise.

Equation of the regression line (line of best fit)

The least-squares regression line of y on x is y = mx + c, where the gradient m = Sxy / Sxx and the intercept c = y-bar − m × x-bar. This line always passes through the point (x-bar, y-bar).
Worked exampleUsing the revision data (Sxy = 8.0, Sxx = 10, x-bar = 3, y-bar = 4.2), find the regression line.
m = Sxy / Sxx = 8.0 / 10 = 0.8
c = y-bar − m × x-bar = 4.2 − (0.8 × 3) = 4.2 − 2.4 = 1.8
The regression line is y = 0.8x + 1.8.

Using the regression line to make predictions

To predict a y-value, substitute the chosen x into the regression equation. The prediction is called interpolation if x lies within the range of the original data, and extrapolation if x lies outside that range; extrapolated predictions are less reliable.
Worked exampleThe revision data covered x from 1 to 5 hours, giving the line y = 0.8x + 1.8. Predict the score for a student who revises for 4 hours, and for a student who revises for 8 hours.
4 hours (interpolation, within the data range): y = 0.8(4) + 1.8 = 3.2 + 1.8 = 5
8 hours (extrapolation, outside the data range): y = 0.8(8) + 1.8 = 6.4 + 1.8 = 8.2
The prediction for 8 hours is less reliable because it extrapolates beyond the original data.
