The general cubic model

y = ax3 + bx2 + cx + d, where a, b, c, d are constants and a ≠ 0. Given a set of data points, technology (cubic regression) is used to find the values of a, b, c and d that best fit the data; the resulting model can then be used to estimate y for other values of x.
Worked exampleCubic regression on the data points (0, 5), (1, 8), (2, 17) and (3, 44) gives the model y = 2x3 − 3x2 + 4x + 5. Verify the model at x = 2, then use it to estimate y when x = 4.
Verify at x = 2: y = 2(2)3 − 3(2)2 + 4(2) + 5 = 2(8) − 3(4) + 8 + 5 = 16 − 12 + 8 + 5 = 17, which matches the data point (2, 17).
Estimate at x = 4: y = 2(4)3 − 3(4)2 + 4(4) + 5 = 2(64) − 3(16) + 16 + 5 = 128 − 48 + 16 + 5 = 101

Direct variation, y = kx

y varies directly with x: y = kx, where k is a non-zero constant of variation. Substitute one known pair of values to find k, then use the equation to find any other value of x or y.
Worked exampleThe cost C (in $) of fabric varies directly with the length L (in metres) purchased. 5 metres costs $37.50. Find the constant of variation, the cost of 12 metres, and the length that costs $60.
Find k: 37.50 = k(5), so k = 37.50 / 5 = 7.5. The equation is C = 7.5L.
Cost of 12 m: C = 7.5(12) = $90
Length for $60: 60 = 7.5L, so L = 60 / 7.5 = 8 metres

Direct variation with the square, y = kx2

y varies directly with the square of x: y = kx2, where k is a non-zero constant of variation. Substitute one known pair of values to find k, then use the equation to find any other value of x or y.
Worked exampleThe distance d (in metres) a ball has fallen varies directly with the square of the time t (in seconds) it has been falling. The ball falls 20 m in 2 seconds. Find the constant of variation, the distance fallen after 5 seconds, and the time to fall 80 m.
Find k: 20 = k(2)2 = 4k, so k = 20 / 4 = 5. The equation is d = 5t2.
Distance after 5 s: d = 5(5)2 = 5(25) = 125 m
Time to fall 80 m: 80 = 5t2, so t2 = 16, giving t = 4 s

Inverse variation, y = k / x

y varies inversely with x: y = k / x, where k is a non-zero constant of variation. Substitute one known pair of values to find k, then use the equation to find any other value of x or y.
Worked exampleThe time N (in hours) taken to build a wall varies inversely with the number of workers x available. When 4 workers are available, the wall takes 9 hours. Find the constant of variation, the time taken with 6 workers, and the number of workers needed to finish the wall in 3 hours.
Find k: 9 = k / 4, so k = 9 × 4 = 36. The equation is N = 36 / x.
Time with 6 workers: N = 36 / 6 = 6 hours
Workers needed for 3 hours: 3 = 36 / x, so x = 36 / 3 = 12 workers

Inverse variation with the square, y = k / x2

y varies inversely with the square of x: y = k / x2, where k is a non-zero constant of variation. Substitute one known pair of values to find k, then use the equation to find any other value of x or y.
Worked exampleThe intensity I (in lux) of light from a source varies inversely with the square of the distance x (in metres) from the source. At a distance of 3 m, the intensity is 80 lux. Find the constant of variation, the intensity at 6 m, and the distance at which the intensity is 5 lux.
Find k: 80 = k / 32 = k / 9, so k = 80 × 9 = 720. The equation is I = 720 / x2.
Intensity at 6 m: I = 720 / 62 = 720 / 36 = 20 lux
Distance for I = 5 lux: 5 = 720 / x2, so x2 = 720 / 5 = 144, giving x = 12 m
