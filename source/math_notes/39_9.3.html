The general cubic model

\(y = ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d\), where \(a\), \(b\), \(c\), \(d\) are constants and \(a \ne 0\). Given a set of data points, technology (cubic regression) is used to find the values of \(a\), \(b\), \(c\) and \(d\) that best fit the data; the resulting model can then be used to estimate \(y\) for other values of \(x\).
Worked exampleCubic regression on the data points \((0, 5)\), \((1, 8)\), \((2, 17)\) and \((3, 44)\) gives the model \(y = 2x^3 - 3x^2 + 4x + 5\). Verify the model at \(x = 2\), then use it to estimate \(y\) when \(x = 4\).
Verify at \(x = 2\):
\[y = 2(2)^3 - 3(2)^2 + 4(2) + 5\]\[y = 2(8) - 3(4) + 8 + 5\]\[\boxed{y = 16 - 12 + 8 + 5 = 17}\]which matches the data point \((2, 17)\).
Estimate at \(x = 4\):
\[y = 2(4)^3 - 3(4)^2 + 4(4) + 5\]\[y = 2(64) - 3(16) + 16 + 5\]\[\boxed{y = 128 - 48 + 16 + 5 = 101}\]

Direct variation, y = kx

\(y\) varies directly with \(x\): \(y = kx\), where \(k\) is a non-zero constant of variation. Substitute one known pair of values to find \(k\), then use the equation to find any other value of \(x\) or \(y\).
Worked exampleThe cost \(C\) (in $) of fabric varies directly with the length \(L\) (in metres) purchased. 5 metres costs $37.50. Find the constant of variation, the cost of 12 metres, and the length that costs $60.
Find \(k\):
\[37.50 = k(5)\]\[\boxed{k = \frac{37.50}{5} = 7.5}\]The equation is \(C = 7.5L\).
Cost of 12 m:
\[C = 7.5(12)\]\[\boxed{C = 90}\]i.e. a cost of $90.
Length for a cost of $60:
\[60 = 7.5L\]\[\boxed{L = \frac{60}{7.5} = 8}\]i.e. a length of 8 metres.

Direct variation with the square, y = kx2

\(y\) varies directly with the square of \(x\): \(y = kx^2\), where \(k\) is a non-zero constant of variation. Substitute one known pair of values to find \(k\), then use the equation to find any other value of \(x\) or \(y\).
Worked exampleThe distance \(d\) (in metres) a ball has fallen varies directly with the square of the time \(t\) (in seconds) it has been falling. The ball falls 20 m in 2 seconds. Find the constant of variation, the distance fallen after 5 seconds, and the time to fall 80 m.
Find \(k\):
\[20 = k(2)^2 = 4k\]\[\boxed{k = \frac{20}{4} = 5}\]The equation is \(d = 5t^2\).
Distance after 5 s:
\[d = 5(5)^2 = 5(25)\]\[\boxed{d = 125 \text{ m}}\]Time to fall 80 m:
\[80 = 5t^2\]\[t^2 = 16\]\[\boxed{t = 4 \text{ s}}\]

Inverse variation, y = k / x

\(y\) varies inversely with \(x\): \(y = \dfrac{k}{x}\), where \(k\) is a non-zero constant of variation. Substitute one known pair of values to find \(k\), then use the equation to find any other value of \(x\) or \(y\).
Worked exampleThe time \(N\) (in hours) taken to build a wall varies inversely with the number of workers \(x\) available. When 4 workers are available, the wall takes 9 hours. Find the constant of variation, the time taken with 6 workers, and the number of workers needed to finish the wall in 3 hours.
Find \(k\):
\[9 = \frac{k}{4}\]\[\boxed{k = 9 \times 4 = 36}\]The equation is \(N = \dfrac{36}{x}\).
Time with 6 workers:
\[N = \frac{36}{6}\]\[\boxed{N = 6 \text{ hours}}\]Workers needed for 3 hours:
\[3 = \frac{36}{x}\]\[\boxed{x = \frac{36}{3} = 12 \text{ workers}}\]

Inverse variation with the square, y = k / x2

\(y\) varies inversely with the square of \(x\): \(y = \dfrac{k}{x^2}\), where \(k\) is a non-zero constant of variation. Substitute one known pair of values to find \(k\), then use the equation to find any other value of \(x\) or \(y\).
Worked exampleThe intensity \(I\) (in lux) of light from a source varies inversely with the square of the distance \(x\) (in metres) from the source. At a distance of 3 m, the intensity is 80 lux. Find the constant of variation, the intensity at 6 m, and the distance at which the intensity is 5 lux.
Find \(k\):
\[80 = \frac{k}{3^2} = \frac{k}{9}\]\[\boxed{k = 80 \times 9 = 720}\]The equation is \(I = \dfrac{720}{x^2}\).
Intensity at 6 m:
\[I = \frac{720}{6^2} = \frac{720}{36}\]\[\boxed{I = 20 \text{ lux}}\]Distance for \(I = 5\) lux:
\[5 = \frac{720}{x^2}\]\[x^2 = \frac{720}{5} = 144\]\[\boxed{x = 12 \text{ m}}\]
