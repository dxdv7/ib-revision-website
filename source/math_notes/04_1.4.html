Standard form (scientific notation)

A number in standard form is written as a × 10n, where a is the coefficient (mantissa) with 1 ≤ a < 10, and n is an integer. To convert a decimal number to standard form, move the decimal point until only one non-zero digit remains before it; n is positive for numbers of size 10 or greater, and negative for numbers between 0 and 1, with |n| equal to the number of places the decimal point moved. To convert back to decimal form, move the decimal point n places to the right if n is positive, or |n| places to the left if n is negative.
Worked exampleWrite 704 000 000 and 0.0000068 in standard form. Then write 5.3 × 106 in decimal form.
704 000 000: move the decimal point 8 places left, giving 7.04 × 108
0.0000068: move the decimal point 6 places right, giving 6.8 × 10−6
5.3 × 106: move the decimal point 6 places right, giving 5 300 000

Multiplying numbers in standard form

(a × 10m) × (b × 10n) = (a × b) × 10m+n: multiply the coefficients and add the exponents. If a × b is not between 1 and 10, adjust the result by shifting the decimal point of the coefficient and changing the exponent accordingly.
Worked exampleA computer chip (a cuboid) is 2.44 × 10−6 m wide, 1.5 × 10−7 m long and 2.15 × 10−4 m high. Find its volume.
Volume = length × width × height = (2.44 × 10−6) × (1.5 × 10−7) × (2.15 × 10−4)
Coefficients: 2.44 × 1.5 × 2.15 = 7.869
Exponents: −6 + (−7) + (−4) = −17
Volume = 7.869 × 10−17 m3

Dividing numbers in standard form

(a × 10m) ÷ (b × 10n) = (a / b) × 10m−n: divide the coefficients and subtract the exponents. Adjust the result afterwards if the coefficient is not between 1 and 10.
Worked exampleThe image of a speck of dust seen through a microscope is 1.2 × 102 mm wide. The image is 5 × 102 times as large as the actual speck. Find the actual width of the speck of dust.
Actual width = image width / magnification = (1.2 × 102) / (5 × 102)
= (1.2 / 5) × 102−2 = 0.24 × 100
Since 0.24 is not between 1 and 10, rewrite it: 0.24 × 100 = 2.4 × 10−1
The actual width of the speck of dust is 2.4 × 10−1 mm (0.24 mm)
