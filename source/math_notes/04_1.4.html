Standard form (scientific notation)

A number in standard form is written as \(a \times 10^n\), where \(a\) is the coefficient (mantissa) with \(1 \le a < 10\), and \(n\) is an integer. To convert a decimal number to standard form, move the decimal point until only one non-zero digit remains before it; \(n\) is positive for numbers of size 10 or greater, and negative for numbers between 0 and 1, with \(|n|\) equal to the number of places the decimal point moved. To convert back to decimal form, move the decimal point \(n\) places to the right if \(n\) is positive, or \(|n|\) places to the left if \(n\) is negative.
Worked exampleWrite \(704\,000\,000\) and \(0.0000068\) in standard form. Then write \(5.3 \times 10^6\) in decimal form.
\(704\,000\,000\): move the decimal point 8 places left, giving \[\boxed{7.04 \times 10^8}\]\(0.0000068\): move the decimal point 6 places right, giving \[\boxed{6.8 \times 10^{-6}}\]\(5.3 \times 10^6\): move the decimal point 6 places right, giving \[\boxed{5\,300\,000}\]

Multiplying numbers in standard form

\((a \times 10^m) \times (b \times 10^n) = (a \times b) \times 10^{m+n}\): multiply the coefficients and add the exponents. If \(a \times b\) is not between 1 and 10, adjust the result by shifting the decimal point of the coefficient and changing the exponent accordingly.
Worked exampleA computer chip (a cuboid) is \(2.44 \times 10^{-6}\) m wide, \(1.5 \times 10^{-7}\) m long and \(2.15 \times 10^{-4}\) m high. Find its volume.
\[\text{Volume} = \text{length} \times \text{width} \times \text{height} = (2.44 \times 10^{-6}) \times (1.5 \times 10^{-7}) \times (2.15 \times 10^{-4})\]\[\text{Coefficients: } 2.44 \times 1.5 \times 2.15 = 7.869\]\[\text{Exponents: } -6 + (-7) + (-4) = -17\]\[\boxed{\text{Volume} = 7.869 \times 10^{-17} \text{ m}^3}\]

Dividing numbers in standard form

\((a \times 10^m) \div (b \times 10^n) = \left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right) \times 10^{m-n}\): divide the coefficients and subtract the exponents. Adjust the result afterwards if the coefficient is not between 1 and 10.
Worked exampleThe image of a speck of dust seen through a microscope is \(1.2 \times 10^2\) mm wide. The image is \(5 \times 10^2\) times as large as the actual speck. Find the actual width of the speck of dust.
\[\text{Actual width} = \frac{\text{image width}}{\text{magnification}} = \frac{1.2 \times 10^2}{5 \times 10^2}\]\[= \left(\frac{1.2}{5}\right) \times 10^{2-2} = 0.24 \times 10^0\]Since \(0.24\) is not between 1 and 10, rewrite it:
\[0.24 \times 10^0 = 2.4 \times 10^{-1}\]\[\boxed{\text{Actual width} = 2.4 \times 10^{-1} \text{ mm } (0.24 \text{ mm})}\]
