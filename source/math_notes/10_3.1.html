Mean of ungrouped data

Mean,
\[\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n}\]where \(\sum x\) is the total of all the data values and \(n\) is the number of values.
Worked exampleFind the mean of 4, 8, 6, 10, 12.
\[\sum x = 4 + 8 + 6 + 10 + 12 = 40\]\[n = 5\]\[\boxed{\text{Mean} = \frac{40}{5} = 8}\]

Median of ungrouped data

Arrange the data in ascending order. If \(n\) is odd, the median is the middle value, at position \(\frac{n+1}{2}\). If \(n\) is even, the median is the mean of the two middle values, at positions \(\frac{n}{2}\) and \(\frac{n}{2}+1\).
Worked exampleFind the median of 8, 3, 10, 5, 12 (odd \(n\)).
Sorted: 3, 5, 8, 10, 12
\(n = 5\), so the median is the 3rd value:
\[\boxed{\text{Median} = 8}\]Find the median of 20, 5, 15, 10 (even \(n\)).
Sorted: 5, 10, 15, 20
\(n = 4\), so the median is the mean of the 2nd and 3rd values:
\[\boxed{\text{Median} = \frac{10+15}{2} = 12.5}\]

Mode

The mode is the data value that occurs most frequently. A data set may have no mode, one mode, or more than one mode (bimodal/multimodal).
Worked exampleFind the mode of 12, 15, 12, 18, 12, 20.
12 occurs three times, more than any other value.
\[\boxed{\text{Mode} = 12}\]For the data set 4, 4, 6, 8, 8, 10, both 4 and 8 occur twice, so the data set is bimodal with modes 4 and 8.

Range

\[\text{Range} = \text{largest value} - \text{smallest value}\]
Worked exampleFind the range of 12, 18, 7, 25, 3, 30.
Largest value = 30
Smallest value = 3
\[\boxed{\text{Range} = 30 - 3 = 27}\]

Mean from a frequency table (discrete data)

Mean,
\[\bar{x} = \frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}\]where \(x\) is each distinct data value and \(f\) is its frequency.
Worked exampleThe number of siblings, \(x\), reported by a class of students has frequencies: \(x = 0\) (\(f = 5\)), \(x = 1\) (\(f = 8\)), \(x = 2\) (\(f = 4\)), \(x = 3\) (\(f = 3\)). Find the mean.
\[\sum fx = (0 \times 5) + (1 \times 8) + (2 \times 4) + (3 \times 3) = 0 + 8 + 8 + 9 = 25\]\[\sum f = 5 + 8 + 4 + 3 = 20\]\[\boxed{\text{Mean} = \frac{25}{20} = 1.25}\]

Mean of grouped data (using midpoints)

Mean,
\[\bar{x} \approx \frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}\]where \(x\) is the midpoint of each class interval and \(f\) is the frequency of that class. This value is only an estimate, since the exact original data values are unknown.
Worked exampleThe weights, in kg, of 20 dogs are grouped as: 40-49 (\(f = 3\), midpoint 44.5), 50-59 (\(f = 7\), midpoint 54.5), 60-69 (\(f = 5\), midpoint 64.5), 70-79 (\(f = 5\), midpoint 74.5). Estimate the mean weight.
\[\sum fx = 3(44.5) + 7(54.5) + 5(64.5) + 5(74.5) = 133.5 + 381.5 + 322.5 + 372.5 = 1210\]\[\sum f = 3 + 7 + 5 + 5 = 20\]\[\boxed{\text{Mean} \approx \frac{1210}{20} = 60.5 \text{ kg}}\]
