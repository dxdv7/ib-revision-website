Midpoint of a line segment (locating a Voronoi vertex candidate)

\[M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)\]where \(M\) is the midpoint of the segment joining sites \(A(x_1, y_1)\) and \(B(x_2, y_2)\).
Worked exampleTwo hospitals are sites \(A(1, 8)\) and \(B(8, 8)\). Find the midpoint of \([AB]\), which lies on the Voronoi boundary between them.
\[M = \left(\frac{1 + 8}{2}, \frac{8 + 8}{2}\right)\]\[M = \left(\frac{9}{2}, \frac{16}{2}\right)\]\[\boxed{M = (4.5, 8)}\]

Gradient of a perpendicular bisector

\(m_{AB} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\) is the gradient of \([AB]\). The perpendicular bisector of \([AB]\) has gradient \(m_{\perp} = -\frac{1}{m_{AB}}\) (the negative reciprocal), since the boundary between two Voronoi cells must be perpendicular to the segment joining the two sites.
Worked exampleSites \(A(1, 4)\) and \(C(4, 3)\). Find the gradient of the perpendicular bisector of \([AC]\).
\[m_{AC} = \frac{3 - 4}{4 - 1} = -\frac{1}{3}\]\[m_{\perp} = -\frac{1}{m_{AC}} = -\frac{1}{-\frac{1}{3}} = 3\]The perpendicular bisector of \([AC]\) has gradient \(\boxed{3}\).

Equation of a perpendicular bisector (Voronoi cell boundary)

To find the equation of the boundary between two sites \(A(x_1, y_1)\) and \(B(x_2, y_2)\): find the midpoint \(M\), find \(m_{AB}\), then find \(m_{\perp} = -\frac{1}{m_{AB}}\). The equation of the boundary is \(y - y_M = m_{\perp}(x - x_M)\).
Worked exampleA town has coffee shops at sites \(A(1, 6)\) and \(B(2, 2)\). Find the equation of the Voronoi boundary between them.
\[M = \left(\frac{1 + 2}{2}, \frac{6 + 2}{2}\right) = (1.5, 4)\]\[m_{AB} = \frac{2 - 6}{2 - 1} = -4\]\[m_{\perp} = -\frac{1}{-4} = 0.25\]\[y - 4 = 0.25(x - 1.5)\]\[y = 0.25x - 0.375 + 4\]\[\boxed{y = 0.25x + 3.625}\]

Nearest-neighbour interpolation

Nearest-neighbour interpolation estimates the value of a quantity at any point \(P\) as equal to the known value at the site closest to \(P\) (the site whose Voronoi cell contains \(P\)), found by comparing
\[d = \sqrt{(x - x_n)^2 + (y - y_n)^2}\]from \(P\) to each site.
Worked exampleRainfall is recorded at three weather stations: \(P_1(0, 0) = 40\) mm, \(P_2(10, 0) = 55\) mm, \(P_3(5, 8) = 30\) mm. Estimate the rainfall at the point \((4, 3)\) using nearest-neighbour interpolation.
\[\text{Distance to } P_1 = \sqrt{(4 - 0)^2 + (3 - 0)^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5\]\[\text{Distance to } P_2 = \sqrt{(4 - 10)^2 + (3 - 0)^2} = \sqrt{45} \approx 6.71\]\[\text{Distance to } P_3 = \sqrt{(4 - 5)^2 + (3 - 8)^2} = \sqrt{26} \approx 5.10\]\(P_1\) is closest (distance 5), so the estimated rainfall at \((4, 3)\) is \(\boxed{40 \text{ mm}}\).
