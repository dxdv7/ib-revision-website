Amplitude and principal axis (vertical shift) from maximum and minimum values

Amplitude a = (max − min) / 2, and the equation of the principal axis (vertical shift) is y = d, where d = (max + min) / 2.
Worked exampleOver a single day, the highest temperature recorded is 22°C and the lowest is 12°C. Find the amplitude and the vertical shift of a sinusoidal model for the temperature.
a = (22 − 12) / 2 = 10 / 2 = 5
d = (22 + 12) / 2 = 34 / 2 = 17

Period and the parameter b

For y = a sin(b(x − c)) + d or y = a cos(b(x − c)) + d, with x measured in degrees, the period is 360 / b, so b = 360 / period.
Worked exampleA Ferris wheel completes one full revolution every 8 minutes. Find the value of b for a sinusoidal model in degrees.
b = 360 / period = 360 / 8 = 45

The general sinusoidal model y = a sin(b(x − c)) + d

y = a sin(b(x − c)) + d, where a is the amplitude, b determines the period (period = 360 / b in degrees), c is the horizontal (phase) shift, and d is the vertical shift (principal axis y = d).
Worked exampleA Ferris wheel has minimum height 2 m (at t = 0 minutes, at the boarding platform) and maximum height 32 m, completing one revolution every 8 minutes. The height is modelled by h(t) = 15 sin(45(t − 2)) + 17, where t is in minutes.
Amplitude a = (32 − 2) / 2 = 15
Vertical shift d = (32 + 2) / 2 = 17
Period = 360 / 45 = 8 minutes (matches the 8-minute rotation)
Predict the height after 5 minutes:
h(5) = 15 sin(45(5 − 2)) + 17 = 15 sin(135°) + 17
sin(135°) = 0.7071
h(5) = 15(0.7071) + 17 = 10.6 + 17 = 27.6 m (3 sf)
