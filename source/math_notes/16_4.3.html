Gradient between two points

The gradient (slope) of the line through points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) is m = (y2 − y1) / (x2 − x1). This value of m is needed to build the equation of a line in any of its forms.
Worked exampleFind the gradient of the line through A(1, 3) and B(4, 9).
m = (y2 − y1) / (x2 − x1) = (9 − 3) / (4 − 1)
m = 6 / 3 = 2

Point-gradient (point-slope) form

y − y1 = m(x − x1), where m is the gradient of the line and (x1, y1) is a known point on the line. This form is used when a point and the gradient are known.
Worked exampleFind the equation of the line with gradient 3 that passes through (2, 5). Give the answer in the form y = mx + c.
y − y1 = m(x − x1)
y − 5 = 3(x − 2)
y − 5 = 3x − 6
y = 3x − 1

Finding the equation through two given points

If two points are known but not the gradient, first find m = (y2 − y1) / (x2 − x1), then substitute m and either point into the point-gradient form y − y1 = m(x − x1).
Worked exampleFind the equation of the line through A(1, 3) and B(4, 9).
m = (9 − 3) / (4 − 1) = 2
Using point A(1, 3): y − 3 = 2(x − 1)
y − 3 = 2x − 2
y = 2x + 1

Gradient-intercept (slope-intercept) form

y = mx + c, where m is the gradient of the line and c is the y-coordinate of the y-intercept (the value of y when x = 0). Equations in point-gradient or general form can always be rearranged into this form.
Worked exampleWrite the line 2x + 3y − 12 = 0 in the form y = mx + c.
3y = −2x + 12
Divide every term by 3:
y = −(2/3)x + 4
So m = −2/3 and c = 4

General (standard) form

ax + by + d = 0, where a, b and d are real numbers (usually taken to be integers with no common factor). Any equation in point-gradient or gradient-intercept form can be rearranged into this form by clearing fractions and moving all terms to one side.
Worked exampleWrite the line y = (2/3)x − 4 in the general form ax + by + d = 0, using integer coefficients.
Multiply every term by 3 to clear the fraction:
3y = 2x − 12
Rearrange so all terms are on one side:
2x − 3y − 12 = 0

Equations of horizontal and vertical lines

A horizontal line has equation y = k, where k is a constant (every point on the line has the same y-coordinate). A vertical line has equation x = h, where h is a constant (every point on the line has the same x-coordinate). A vertical line has no defined gradient, since x2 − x1 = 0.
Worked exampleFind the equation of the line through (5, 7) and (5, −3).
Both points have x-coordinate 5, so the line is vertical.
Equation: x = 5
Find the equation of the line through (−2, 4) and (6, 4).
Both points have y-coordinate 4, so the line is horizontal.
Equation: y = 4

Finding the x-intercept and y-intercept of a line

To find the y-intercept of a line, substitute x = 0 into its equation and solve for y. To find the x-intercept, substitute y = 0 and solve for x. The x-intercept has the form (x, 0) and the y-intercept has the form (0, y).
Worked exampleFind the x-intercept and y-intercept of the line 3x − 2y − 12 = 0.
y-intercept: set x = 0: −2y − 12 = 0, so y = −6. Point (0, −6).
x-intercept: set y = 0: 3x − 12 = 0, so x = 4. Point (4, 0).

Testing whether a point lies on a line

A point lies on a line if substituting its coordinates into the equation of the line makes the equation true (the left-hand side equals the right-hand side).
Worked exampleDoes the point (4, 5) lie on the line y = 2x − 3?
Substitute x = 4: y = 2(4) − 3 = 5
Since 5 = 5, the point (4, 5) DOES lie on the line.
Does the point (2, 0) lie on the same line?
Substitute x = 2: y = 2(2) − 3 = 1
Since 1 ≠ 0, the point (2, 0) does NOT lie on the line.
