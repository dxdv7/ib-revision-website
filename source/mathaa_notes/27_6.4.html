The sine rule

For a triangle with angles \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) and opposite sides \(a\), \(b\), \(c\) respectively:
\[\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}\]or, equivalently:
\[\frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{\sin B}{b} = \frac{\sin C}{c}\]The sine rule is used to find unknown sides or angles when two angles and one side are known (ASA or AAS).
Worked exampleIn triangle \(ABC\), \(A = 40^{\circ}\), \(B = 75^{\circ}\) and \(a = 9\text{ cm}\). Find angle \(C\) and the lengths of sides \(b\) and \(c\), correct to 3 significant figures.
\[C = 180^{\circ} - 40^{\circ} - 75^{\circ}\]\[\boxed{C = 65^{\circ}}\]\[\frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{a}{\sin A}\]\[b = \frac{9\sin 75^{\circ}}{\sin 40^{\circ}}\]\[\boxed{b \approx 13.5 \text{ cm}}\]\[c = \frac{9\sin 65^{\circ}}{\sin 40^{\circ}}\]\[\boxed{c \approx 12.7 \text{ cm}}\]

The ambiguous case of the sine rule (SSA)

When two sides and a non-included angle (SSA) are given, there may be no triangle, one triangle, or two triangles. If the given angle \(A\) is acute and sides \(a\) and \(b\) are known, with \(a\) opposite \(A\):
\[a < b\sin A \implies \text{no triangle}\]\[a = b\sin A \implies \text{one right-angled triangle}\]\[b\sin A < a < b \implies \text{two triangles}\]\[a \geq b \implies \text{one triangle}\]When two triangles are possible, the sine rule gives an acute angle \(B_1\) and an obtuse angle \(B_2 = 180^{\circ} - B_1\), both with the same sine.
Worked exampleIn triangle \(ABC\), \(a = 8\text{ cm}\), \(b = 11\text{ cm}\) and \(A = 28^{\circ}\). Show that two triangles are possible, and find both possible values of angle \(B\), correct to 3 significant figures.
\[b\sin A = 11\sin 28^{\circ} \approx 5.16 \text{ cm}\]Since \(5.16 < 8 < 11\), two triangles are possible.
\[\sin B = \frac{b\sin A}{a} = \frac{11\sin 28^{\circ}}{8}\]\[B_1 = \sin^{-1}(0.6455)\]\[\boxed{B_1 \approx 40.2^{\circ}}\]\[B_2 = 180^{\circ} - B_1\]\[\boxed{B_2 \approx 139.8^{\circ}}\]

The cosine rule: finding a side (SAS)

For a triangle with angles \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) and opposite sides \(a\), \(b\), \(c\):
\[a^{2} = b^{2} + c^{2} - 2bc\cos A\]and similarly for the other two sides. The cosine rule is used to find the side opposite a known angle when the other two sides and the included angle (SAS) are known. When the chosen angle is \(90^{\circ}\), the cosine rule reduces to Pythagoras' theorem, since \(\cos 90^{\circ} = 0\).
Worked exampleIn triangle \(ABC\), \(b = 14\text{ cm}\), \(c = 9\text{ cm}\) and the included angle \(A = 55^{\circ}\). Find the length of side \(a\), correct to 3 significant figures.
\[a^{2} = b^{2} + c^{2} - 2bc\cos A\]\[a^{2} = 14^{2} + 9^{2} - 2(14)(9)\cos 55^{\circ}\]\[a^{2} \approx 132.5\]\[a \approx \sqrt{132.5}\]\[\boxed{a \approx 11.5 \text{ cm}}\]

The cosine rule: finding an angle (SSS)

Rearranging the cosine rule to make the cosine of the angle the subject gives:
\[\cos A = \frac{b^{2}+c^{2}-a^{2}}{2bc}\]and similarly for angles \(B\) and \(C\). This form of the cosine rule is used to find any angle of a triangle when all three sides (SSS) are known. The largest angle of the triangle is always opposite the longest side.
Worked exampleA triangle has sides \(a = 7\text{ cm}\), \(b = 9\text{ cm}\) and \(c = 12\text{ cm}\). Find the size of angle \(C\), the largest angle, correct to 3 significant figures.
\[\cos C = \frac{a^{2}+b^{2}-c^{2}}{2ab}\]\[\cos C = \frac{7^{2}+9^{2}-12^{2}}{2(7)(9)}\]\[\cos C = \frac{-14}{126} \approx -0.1111\]\[C = \cos^{-1}(-0.1111)\]\[\boxed{C \approx 96.4^{\circ}}\]

Choosing between the sine rule and the cosine rule

The arrangement of known parts of a triangle determines which rule to use. Use the sine rule when two angles and a side (ASA or AAS) are known, or when two sides and a non-included angle (SSA) are known. Use the cosine rule when two sides and the included angle (SAS) are known, or when all three sides (SSS) are known, since the sine rule cannot be set up without at least one known angle-side opposite pair.
Worked exampleTown \(C\) is visible from towns \(A\) and \(B\), which are \(50\text{ km}\) apart. Angle \(CAB = 52^{\circ}\) and angle \(ABC = 71^{\circ}\). Find the distance from \(A\) to \(C\), correct to 3 significant figures.
Since two angles and the included side \(AB\) are known (ASA), the sine rule is used.
\[C = 180^{\circ} - 52^{\circ} - 71^{\circ}\]\[C = 57^{\circ}\]\[\frac{AC}{\sin B} = \frac{AB}{\sin C}\]\[AC = \frac{50\sin 71^{\circ}}{\sin 57^{\circ}}\]\[\boxed{AC \approx 56.4 \text{ km}}\]
