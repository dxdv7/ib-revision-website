Scatter diagrams and correlation

In bivariate data, each subject has two measurements, \(x\) and \(y\). The explanatory variable (independent variable) is plotted on the horizontal axis and the response variable (dependent variable) is plotted on the vertical axis. A scatter diagram plots each pair \((x_i, y_i)\) as a point, and is examined for three features: the form (linear or non-linear), the direction (positive or negative), and the strength (how closely the points cluster around a line).
Worked exampleA scatter diagram plots the number of hours of sunshine recorded in a town each day against the number of ice creams sold that day. As the hours of sunshine increase, the number of ice creams sold tends to increase, and the points lie close to a straight line. Describe the correlation.
The points rise from left to right, so the direction is positive.
The points lie close to a line, so the strength is strong.
\[\boxed{\text{strong positive linear correlation}}\]

The correlation coefficient

The (Pearson product-moment) correlation coefficient \(r\) measures the strength and direction of the linear relationship between two quantitative variables:
\[r = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i - \bar{x})^2} \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \bar{y})^2}}\]where \(\bar{x}\) and \(\bar{y}\) are the means of the two variables. In examinations, \(r\) is read from technology, not calculated by hand. It satisfies \(-1 \le r \le 1\): the sign of \(r\) gives the direction of the association, and the closer \(|r|\) is to \(1\), the stronger the linear association. A value of \(r\) close to \(0\) indicates a weak or negligible linear association. Correlation does not have units and is not a percentage, and a strong correlation does not by itself prove that one variable causes the other.
Worked exampleA calculator gives the correlation coefficient between the number of hours a group of students studied and their exam scores as \(r = 0.91\). A separate calculation gives the correlation coefficient between two other variables as \(r = -0.18\). Interpret each value.
Since \(r = 0.91\) is positive and close to \(1\):
\[\boxed{\text{strong positive correlation}}\]Since \(r = -0.18\) is negative and close to \(0\):
\[\boxed{\text{weak negative correlation}}\]

The least-squares regression line

The regression line of \(y\) on \(x\) is the line of best fit that minimises the sum of the squares of the vertical distances from the data points to the line (the least-squares line). It is written:
\[y = a + bx\]where \(b\) is the gradient, representing the average change in the response variable \(y\) for every increase of \(1\) unit in the explanatory variable \(x\), and \(a\) is the \(y\)-intercept. The regression line always passes through \((\bar{x}, \bar{y})\), so once \(b\) is known:
\[a = \bar{y} - b\bar{x}\]
Worked exampleFor a set of bivariate data, the mean number of hours studied is \(\bar{x} = 12\) and the mean exam score is \(\bar{y} = 68\). The regression line of \(y\) on \(x\) has gradient \(b = 3.2\). Find the equation of the regression line.
\[a = \bar{y} - b\bar{x}\]\[a = 68 - 3.2(12)\]\[a = 68 - 38.4\]\[\boxed{a = 29.6}\]\[\boxed{y = 3.2x + 29.6}\]

Using the regression line to make predictions

Once the equation of the regression line \(y = a + bx\) is known, it can be used to predict the value of the response variable for a given value of the explanatory variable, by substituting that value of \(x\) into the equation. A predicted value is an average (expected) value, not an exact value, since individual data points scatter around the line.
Worked exampleUsing the regression line \(y = 3.2x + 29.6\) found above, predict the exam score of a student who studies for \(15\) hours.
\[y = 3.2(15) + 29.6\]\[y = 48 + 29.6\]\[\boxed{y = 77.6}\]

Interpolation and extrapolation

Using the regression line to predict a value of \(y\) for a value of \(x\) that lies within the range of the \(x\)-values used to find the line is called interpolation. This is usually reasonable, especially when the correlation is strong. Using the regression line to predict a value of \(y\) for a value of \(x\) that lies outside this range is called extrapolation. Extrapolation is unreliable, since there is no evidence that the same linear relationship continues to hold outside the range of the observed data.
Worked exampleThe data used to find the regression line \(y = 3.2x + 29.6\) covered study times from \(x = 4\) to \(x = 20\) hours. (a) Classify the prediction at \(x = 15\) made in the previous example. (b) Predict the exam score for a student who studies for \(40\) hours, and comment on the reliability of this prediction.
(a) Since \(4 \le 15 \le 20\):
\[\boxed{\text{interpolation, relatively reliable}}\](b)
\[y = 3.2(40) + 29.6\]\[y = 128 + 29.6\]\[\boxed{y = 157.6}\]Since \(40\) lies outside the range \([4, 20]\), this is extrapolation, and the prediction is unreliable (it also exceeds a realistic maximum exam score).
