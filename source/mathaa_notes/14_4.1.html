Exponential functions and their graphs

For \(b>0\) and \(b \neq 1\), an exponential function with base \(b\) is defined by
\[f(x) = b^x\]The domain of \(f\) is all real numbers, \(x \in \mathbb{R}\), and the range is all positive real numbers, \(y > 0\). Every such graph passes through \((0,1)\) and has horizontal asymptote \(y=0\). If \(b>1\), \(f\) is an increasing exponential growth curve; if \(0
Worked exampleState the domain, range, y-intercept and horizontal asymptote of \(h(x) = \left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^x\), and state whether \(h\) is increasing or decreasing.
Since \(0 < \dfrac{1}{4} < 1\), the function is decreasing.
\[\boxed{\text{domain } x \in \mathbb{R}; \text{ range } y>0; \text{ y-intercept } (0,1); \text{ asymptote } y=0; \text{ decreasing}}\]

Transformations of exponential graphs

Worked exampleState the horizontal asymptote and range of \(g(x) = 3(2)^x - 5\).
The graph of \(2^x\) has asymptote \(y=0\); shifting down \(5\) units gives asymptote \(y=-5\). Since the stretch factor \(3\) is positive, the curve stays above its asymptote.
\[\boxed{\text{asymptote } y=-5; \text{ range } y>-5}\]

The exponential growth/decay model

An exponential model has the form
\[A(t) = A_0 b^t\]where \(A_0 \neq 0\) is the initial amount, since \(A(0) = A_0 b^0 = A_0\), \(t\) is time, and \(b>0\) is the growth or decay factor. If \(b>1\), \(A(t)\) models exponential growth; if \(0
Worked exampleA sample count of bacteria is doubling every hour. The estimated count at 15:00 was 12000. Taking \(t=0\) at 12:00, find \(A_0\) and write the growth function \(A(t)\).
The growth factor is \(b=2\), and \(t=3\) corresponds to 15:00.
\[12000 = A_0(2)^3\]\[12000 = 8A_0\]\[\boxed{A_0 = 1500}\]\[\boxed{A(t) = 1500(2)^t}\]

Percentage growth and decay

When a quantity grows at a constant percentage rate \(r\) (as a decimal) per time period, the growth factor is \(b = 1+r\):
\[A(t) = A_0(1+r)^t\]When a quantity decays at a constant percentage rate \(r\) per time period, the decay factor is \(b = 1-r\):
\[A(t) = A_0(1-r)^t\]
Worked exampleA new car is purchased for $22000 and depreciates at a rate of 15% per year. Find its approximate value after 4.5 years.
The decay factor is \(b = 1 - 0.15 = 0.85\).
\[A(t) = 22000(0.85)^t\]\[A(4.5) = 22000(0.85)^{4.5}\]\[\boxed{A(4.5) \approx \$10588}\]

Compound interest

An amount \(P\) invested at annual interest rate \(r\) (as a decimal), compounded \(n\) times per year, grows after \(t\) years to
\[A(t) = P\left(1+\frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}\]
Worked exampleFind the amount after 8 years if $1000 is invested at an annual rate of 5.25%, compounded quarterly.
\[A = 1000\left(1+\frac{0.0525}{4}\right)^{4(8)}\]\[A = 1000(1.013125)^{32}\]\[\boxed{A \approx \$1517.81}\]
