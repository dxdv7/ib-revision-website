Transformations of sine and cosine graphs

The graphs of \(y = a\sin(b(x-c))+d\) and \(y = a\cos(b(x-c))+d\) are transformations of \(y = \sin x\) and \(y = \cos x\):
\(|a|\) controls the amplitude (vertical stretch), \(b\) controls the period (horizontal stretch), \(c\) controls the phase shift (horizontal translation), and \(d\) controls the vertical shift (translation of the midline).
Worked exampleIdentify \(a\), \(b\), \(c\) and \(d\) for \(y = 3\sin\left(2\left(x - \frac{\pi}{4}\right)\right) + 1\), and describe the transformations applied to \(y = \sin x\).
\[a = 3, \qquad b = 2, \qquad c = \frac{\pi}{4}, \qquad d = 1\]This is a vertical stretch by factor \(3\), a horizontal shrink by factor \(\frac{1}{2}\), a shift of \(\frac{\pi}{4}\) to the right, and a shift of \(1\) up.
\[\boxed{a=3,\ b=2,\ c=\frac{\pi}{4},\ d=1}\]

Amplitude and the midline

For \(y = a\sin(b(x-c))+d\) or \(y = a\cos(b(x-c))+d\), the amplitude is \(|a|\) and the graph oscillates about the midline \(y = d\). The maximum value of the graph is \(d + |a|\) and the minimum value is \(d - |a|\).
Worked exampleA sinusoidal graph has a maximum value of \(9.7\) and a minimum value of \(5.1\). Find the amplitude \(a\) and the equation of the midline.
\[d = \frac{9.7 + 5.1}{2}\]\[\boxed{d = 7.4}\]\[a = \frac{9.7 - 5.1}{2}\]\[\boxed{a = 2.3}\]

Period

For \(y = a\sin(b(x-c))+d\) and \(y = a\cos(b(x-c))+d\) with \(b > 0\), the period is \(\frac{2\pi}{b}\). For \(y = a\tan(b(x-c))+d\) with \(b > 0\), the period is \(\frac{\pi}{b}\), since the tangent function already repeats every \(\pi\).
Worked exampleA sinusoidal function has a minimum point at \(t = 1\) and the next maximum point at \(t = 4\). Find the period \(T\) and the value of \(b\).
Consecutive minimum and maximum points are exactly half a period apart:
\[\frac{T}{2} = 4 - 1 = 3\]\[\boxed{T = 6}\]\[b = \frac{2\pi}{T} = \frac{2\pi}{6}\]\[\boxed{b = \frac{\pi}{3}}\]

Phase shift

In \(y = a\sin(b(x-c))+d\) or \(y = a\cos(b(x-c))+d\), the phase shift is \(c\): the graph of \(y = a\sin(bx)+d\) is translated \(c\) units to the right if \(c > 0\), and \(|c|\) units to the left if \(c < 0\).
Worked exampleWrite \(y = \sin\left(2x + \frac{\pi}{2}\right)\) in the form \(y = \sin(b(x-c))\), and state the phase shift.
\[\sin\left(2x + \frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \sin\left(2\left(x + \frac{\pi}{4}\right)\right)\]\[= \sin\left(2\left(x - \left(-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\right)\right)\]\[\boxed{c = -\frac{\pi}{4}}\]This is a phase shift of \(\frac{\pi}{4}\) to the left.

Reflections of trigonometric graphs

Replacing \(a\) with \(-a\) in \(y = a\sin(b(x-c))+d\) reflects the graph in its midline \(y = d\), which swaps the positions of the maximum and minimum points. Replacing \(x\) with \(-x\) reflects the graph in a vertical line, using the identities \(\sin(-x) = -\sin x\) and \(\cos(-x) = \cos x\).
Worked exampleState the maximum value of \(y = 3\cos x + 2\) and of \(y = -3\cos x + 2\), and the smallest non-negative value of \(x\) at which each maximum occurs.
For \(y = 3\cos x + 2\): since \(\cos 0 = 1\),
\[\boxed{\text{maximum} = 5 \text{ at } x = 0}\]For \(y = -3\cos x + 2\): since \(-3\cos \pi = -3(-1) = 3\),
\[\boxed{\text{maximum} = 5 \text{ at } x = \pi}\]Both graphs have the same maximum value, but \(y = -3\cos x + 2\) is the reflection of \(y = 3\cos x + 2\) in the midline \(y = 2\), so the maximum occurs half a period later.

Modelling periodic phenomena

To model a periodic real-world quantity with \(h(t) = a\sin(b(t-c))+d\) or \(h(t) = a\cos(b(t-c))+d\): let \(d\) be the midline value (often the average of the maximum and minimum), let \(|a|\) be the amplitude (half the difference between the maximum and minimum), let \(b = \frac{2\pi}{T}\) where \(T\) is the period, and choose the sine/cosine form and sign of \(a\) to match a known starting condition.
Worked exampleA Ferris wheel has diameter \(40\) m and its centre is \(22\) m above the ground. The wheel completes one full rotation every \(5\) minutes. A rider boards at the lowest point of the wheel. Find a model \(h(t) = a\cos(bt) + d\) for the rider's height above the ground \(t\) minutes after boarding, and find the height after \(2\) minutes, to 3 significant figures.
Radius \(= \frac{40}{2} = 20\), so the midline is \(d = 22\) and the maximum height is \(22 + 20 = 42\).
\[b = \frac{2\pi}{T} = \frac{2\pi}{5}\]At \(t = 0\) the rider is at the lowest point, \(h(0) = 2\), so \(a\cos(0) + 22 = 2\), giving \(a = -20\).
\[\boxed{h(t) = -20\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{5}t\right) + 22}\]\[h(2) = -20\cos\left(\frac{4\pi}{5}\right) + 22\]\[h(2) = -20(-0.809) + 22\]\[\boxed{h(2) \approx 38.2 \text{ m}}\]
