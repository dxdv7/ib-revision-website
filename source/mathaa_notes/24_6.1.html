The 3D distance formula

Extending the Pythagorean theorem to three dimensions, the distance \(d\) between the points \(P(x_1, y_1, z_1)\) and \(Q(x_2, y_2, z_2)\) is:
\[d = \sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2 + (y_2-y_1)^2 + (z_2-z_1)^2}\]This is the length of the diagonal of a rectangular box with edges \(|x_2-x_1|\), \(|y_2-y_1|\) and \(|z_2-z_1|\).
Worked exampleFind the distance between \(P(1, 2, -3)\) and \(Q(4, -2, 9)\).
\[d = \sqrt{(4-1)^2 + (-2-2)^2 + (9-(-3))^2}\]\[d = \sqrt{9 + 16 + 144}\]\[d = \sqrt{169}\]\[\boxed{d = 13}\]

The 3D midpoint formula

The midpoint \(M\) of the line segment with endpoints \(P(x_1, y_1, z_1)\) and \(Q(x_2, y_2, z_2)\) has coordinates equal to the average of each pair of coordinates:
\[M = \left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}, \frac{z_1+z_2}{2}\right)\]
Worked exampleFind the midpoint of \(P(1, 2, -3)\) and \(Q(4, -2, 9)\).
\[M = \left(\frac{1+4}{2}, \frac{2+(-2)}{2}, \frac{-3+9}{2}\right)\]\[M = \left(\frac{5}{2}, 0, 3\right)\]\[\boxed{M = \left(\frac{5}{2}, 0, 3\right)}\]

Testing collinearity with the distance formula

Three points \(A\), \(B\) and \(C\) are collinear (lie on the same straight line) if and only if, after labelling so that \(B\) lies between \(A\) and \(C\), the sum of the two shorter distances equals the longest distance:
\[AB + BC = AC\]
Worked exampleUse the distance formula to show that \(F(1, -1, 3)\), \(G(2, -4, 5)\) and \(H(5, -13, 11)\) are collinear.
\[FG = \sqrt{(1-2)^2 + (-1-(-4))^2 + (3-5)^2} = \sqrt{1+9+4} = \sqrt{14}\]\[GH = \sqrt{(2-5)^2 + (-4-(-13))^2 + (5-11)^2} = \sqrt{9+81+36} = \sqrt{126} = 3\sqrt{14}\]\[FH = \sqrt{(1-5)^2 + (-1-(-13))^2 + (3-11)^2} = \sqrt{16+144+64} = \sqrt{224} = 4\sqrt{14}\]Since \(FG + GH = \sqrt{14} + 3\sqrt{14} = 4\sqrt{14} = FH\):
\[\boxed{F, G \text{ and } H \text{ are collinear}}\]

Finding an unknown vertex with the midpoint formula

The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, so for parallelogram \(ABCD\), the midpoint of diagonal \([AC]\) is equal to the midpoint of diagonal \([BD]\). Setting these two midpoints equal gives a system of equations that can be solved for an unknown vertex.
Worked exampleThree vertices of parallelogram \(ABCD\) are \(A(3, -1, 2)\), \(B(1, 2, -4)\) and \(C(-1, 1, 2)\). Find the coordinates of vertex \(D(x, y, z)\).
\[\text{midpoint of } [AC] = \left(\frac{3+(-1)}{2}, \frac{-1+1}{2}, \frac{2+2}{2}\right) = (1, 0, 2)\]\[\text{midpoint of } [BD] = \left(\frac{1+x}{2}, \frac{2+y}{2}, \frac{-4+z}{2}\right)\]Setting the midpoints equal:
\[\frac{1+x}{2} = 1, \qquad \frac{2+y}{2} = 0, \qquad \frac{-4+z}{2} = 2\]\[\boxed{D = (1, -2, 8)}\]

Sphere centre and radius from a diameter

If \([PQ]\) is a diameter of a sphere, the centre of the sphere is the midpoint of \([PQ]\) and the radius is half the length of \([PQ]\):
\[\text{centre} = \left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}, \frac{z_1+z_2}{2}\right), \qquad r = \frac{PQ}{2}\]
Worked exampleA sphere has a diameter with endpoints \(P(2, -1, 4)\) and \(Q(-4, 5, 0)\). Find the centre and radius of the sphere.
\[\text{centre} = \left(\frac{2+(-4)}{2}, \frac{-1+5}{2}, \frac{4+0}{2}\right)\]\[\boxed{\text{centre} = (-1, 2, 2)}\]\[PQ = \sqrt{(2-(-4))^2 + (-1-5)^2 + (4-0)^2} = \sqrt{36+36+16} = \sqrt{88} = 2\sqrt{22}\]\[r = \frac{2\sqrt{22}}{2}\]\[\boxed{r = \sqrt{22}}\]

Classifying a triangle with the distance formula

To test whether a triangle with vertices \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) is right-angled, compute the square of each side length using the distance formula. If the sum of the two smaller squared lengths equals the largest squared length, the converse of the Pythagorean theorem guarantees a right angle opposite the longest side.
Worked exampleDetermine whether the triangle with vertices \(A(0, 0, 0)\), \(B(2, 2, 1)\) and \(C(4, 1, -1)\) is right-angled.
\[AB^2 = (2-0)^2 + (2-0)^2 + (1-0)^2 = 4+4+1 = 9\]\[BC^2 = (4-2)^2 + (1-2)^2 + (-1-1)^2 = 4+1+4 = 9\]\[AC^2 = (4-0)^2 + (1-0)^2 + (-1-0)^2 = 16+1+1 = 18\]Since \(AB^2 + BC^2 = 9 + 9 = 18 = AC^2\):
\[\boxed{\text{triangle } ABC \text{ is right-angled at } B}\]
