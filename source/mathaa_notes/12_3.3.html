Sigma notation and evaluating a sum

The sum of a sequence of terms \(a_k\) from \(k = m\) to \(k = n\) (where \(m \leq n\)) is written using sigma notation as:
\[\sum_{k=m}^{n} a_k = a_m + a_{m+1} + a_{m+2} + \cdots + a_n\]The variable \(k\) is the index of summation, \(m\) is the lower limit and \(n\) is the upper limit.
Worked exampleEvaluate \(\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{5} i^2\).
\[\sum_{i=1}^{5} i^2 = 1^2 + 2^2 + 3^2 + 4^2 + 5^2\]\[\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^{5} i^2 = 55}\]

Writing a sum in sigma notation

To write a given sum in sigma notation, identify the general term \(a_k\) as a function of the index \(k\), then determine the starting value \(m\) and ending value \(n\) of the index.
Worked exampleWrite the sum \(4 + 8 + 12 + 16 + 20\) using sigma notation.
The general term is \(4k\), starting at \(k = 1\) and ending at \(k = 5\).
\[\boxed{\sum_{k=1}^{5} 4k}\]

Sum of a constant term

If \(c\) is a constant that does not depend on the index \(k\), then summing it \(n\) times gives:
\[\sum_{k=1}^{n} c = nc\]
Worked exampleEvaluate \(\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{11} 6\).
\[\sum_{i=1}^{11} 6 = 11 \times 6\]\[\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^{11} 6 = 66}\]

Sigma notation for an arithmetic series

When the general term is linear in the index, such as \(a_k = pk + q\), the terms form an arithmetic sequence with common difference \(p\). The sum can be evaluated using the arithmetic series formula:
\[\sum_{k=1}^{n} (pk + q) = \frac{n}{2}\left(a_1 + a_n\right)\]where \(a_1\) and \(a_n\) are the first and last terms of the sum.
Worked exampleEvaluate \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{16} (3k + 2)\).
\[a_1 = 3(1) + 2 = 5, \quad a_{16} = 3(16) + 2 = 50\]\[\sum_{k=1}^{16} (3k + 2) = \frac{16}{2}(5 + 50)\]\[\boxed{\sum_{k=1}^{16} (3k + 2) = 440}\]

Sigma notation for a geometric series

When the general term is exponential in the index, such as \(a_k = c \cdot r^k\), the terms form a geometric sequence with common ratio \(r\). The sum can be evaluated using the geometric series formula:
\[\sum_{k=m}^{n} c \cdot r^k = \frac{a_1\left(1 - r^{\,n-m+1}\right)}{1 - r}\]where \(a_1 = c \cdot r^m\) is the first term of the sum and there are \(n - m + 1\) terms.
Worked exampleEvaluate \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{5} 3^k\).
\[a_1 = 3^1 = 3, \quad r = 3, \quad \text{5 terms}\]\[\sum_{k=1}^{5} 3^k = \frac{3\left(1 - 3^5\right)}{1 - 3}\]\[\sum_{k=1}^{5} 3^k = \frac{3(1 - 243)}{-2}\]\[\boxed{\sum_{k=1}^{5} 3^k = 363}\]
