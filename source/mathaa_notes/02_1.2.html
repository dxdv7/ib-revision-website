Parallel lines

Two distinct non-vertical lines are parallel if and only if their gradients are equal:
\[m_1 = m_2\]If two lines have the same gradient but different \(y\)-intercepts, they never meet.
Worked exampleFind, in slope-intercept form, the equation of the line parallel to \(y = 2x + 5\) that passes through \((3, 1)\).
Since the lines are parallel:
\[m = 2\]\[y - 1 = 2(x - 3)\]\[y - 1 = 2x - 6\]\[\boxed{y = 2x - 5}\]

Perpendicular lines

Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular if and only if the product of their gradients is \(-1\):
\[m_1 \times m_2 = -1\]Equivalently, the gradient of one line is the negative reciprocal of the other: \(m_2 = -\dfrac{1}{m_1}\).
Worked exampleFind, in slope-intercept form, the equation of the line perpendicular to \(y = 2x + 5\) that passes through \((4, -1)\).
\[m_2 = -\frac{1}{2}\]\[y - (-1) = -\frac{1}{2}(x - 4)\]\[y + 1 = -\frac{1}{2}x + 2\]\[\boxed{y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 1}\]

Determining whether two lines are parallel or perpendicular

To compare two lines given in general form, rearrange each into slope-intercept form to find its gradient. If the gradients are equal, the lines are parallel. If the product of the gradients is \(-1\), the lines are perpendicular. Otherwise, the lines are neither.
Worked exampleDetermine whether the lines \(2x - 3y + 6 = 0\) and \(4x - 6y - 9 = 0\) are parallel, perpendicular, or neither.
\[2x - 3y + 6 = 0 \Rightarrow y = \frac{2}{3}x + 2\]\[4x - 6y - 9 = 0 \Rightarrow y = \frac{2}{3}x - \frac{3}{2}\]Both lines have gradient \(\dfrac{2}{3}\).
These lines are parallel.

Equation of a line parallel to a given line in general form

A line parallel to \(ax + by + c = 0\) has the same gradient, \(-\dfrac{a}{b}\). Once the gradient and a point are known, the point-slope form gives the equation, which can then be rearranged back into general form.
Worked exampleFind, in general form, the equation of the line parallel to \(5x - 2y + 4 = 0\) that passes through \((-2, 3)\).
\[5x - 2y + 4 = 0 \Rightarrow y = \frac{5}{2}x + 2\]\[y - 3 = \frac{5}{2}(x - (-2))\]\[y - 3 = \frac{5}{2}x + 5\]\[y = \frac{5}{2}x + 8\]\[\boxed{5x - 2y + 16 = 0}\]

Equation of a line perpendicular to a given line in general form

A line perpendicular to \(ax + by + c = 0\) has gradient equal to the negative reciprocal of \(-\dfrac{a}{b}\). Find this gradient first, then use the point-slope form with the given point and rearrange into the required form.
Worked exampleFind, in general form, the equation of the line perpendicular to \(3x + 2y - 6 = 0\) that passes through \((1, 5)\).
\[3x + 2y - 6 = 0 \Rightarrow y = -\frac{3}{2}x + 3\]\[m_2 = \frac{2}{3}\]\[y - 5 = \frac{2}{3}(x - 1)\]\[y - 5 = \frac{2}{3}x - \frac{2}{3}\]\[y = \frac{2}{3}x + \frac{13}{3}\]\[\boxed{2x - 3y + 13 = 0}\]
