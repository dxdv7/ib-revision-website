Volume and surface area of prisms

For a right prism with a uniform polygonal cross-section of area \(A\) and height \(h\):
\[V = Ah\]\[S = 2A + \sum A_{\text{lateral}}\]where \(\sum A_{\text{lateral}}\) is the sum of the areas of the \(n\) rectangular lateral faces. For a cuboid (rectangular prism) with length \(l\), width \(w\) and height \(h\), every face is a rectangle, so:
\[V = lwh\]\[S = 2(lw + lh + wh)\]
Worked exampleA triangular right prism has a right-angled triangular base with legs \(3\text{ cm}\) and \(4\text{ cm}\), and the prism has a height (length) of \(10\text{ cm}\). Find the volume and the total surface area of the prism.
The hypotenuse of the base triangle is \(\sqrt{3^{2}+4^{2}} = 5\text{ cm}\), so the base area is:
\[A = \frac{1}{2}(3)(4) = 6 \text{ cm}^{2}\]\[V = Ah\]\[V = 6 \times 10\]\[\boxed{V = 60 \text{ cm}^{3}}\]The perimeter of the base is \(3 + 4 + 5 = 12\text{ cm}\), so the lateral surface area is \(12 \times 10 = 120\text{ cm}^{2}\):
\[S = 2A + 120\]\[S = 2(6) + 120\]\[\boxed{S = 132 \text{ cm}^{2}}\]

Volume and surface area of cylinders

For a cylinder of radius \(r\) and height \(h\):
\[V = \pi r^{2}h\]\[S = 2\pi r^{2} + 2\pi rh = 2\pi r(r + h)\]where the two terms of the surface area formula are the areas of the two circular bases and the curved lateral surface, respectively.
Worked exampleA cylinder has radius \(4\text{ cm}\) and height \(10\text{ cm}\). Find the exact volume and the exact surface area of the cylinder.
\[V = \pi r^{2}h\]\[V = \pi (4)^{2}(10)\]\[\boxed{V = 160\pi \text{ cm}^{3} \approx 503 \text{ cm}^{3}}\]\[S = 2\pi r(r+h)\]\[S = 2\pi (4)(4+10)\]\[\boxed{S = 112\pi \text{ cm}^{2} \approx 352 \text{ cm}^{2}}\]

Volume and surface area of pyramids

For a pyramid with a polygonal base of area \(A_{\text{base}}\) and height \(h\) (the perpendicular distance from the apex to the base):
\[V = \frac{1}{3}A_{\text{base}}h\]\[S = A_{\text{base}} + \sum A_{\text{lateral}}\]where \(\sum A_{\text{lateral}}\) is the sum of the areas of the \(n\) triangular lateral faces. A pyramid and a prism with the same base and the same height are related by a factor of \(\frac{1}{3}\) in volume.
Worked exampleA right pyramid has a square base of side \(6\text{ cm}\) and height \(9\text{ cm}\). Find the volume and the total surface area of the pyramid.
\[V = \frac{1}{3}A_{\text{base}}h\]\[V = \frac{1}{3}(6^{2})(9)\]\[\boxed{V = 108 \text{ cm}^{3}}\]The slant height of each triangular face is \(l = \sqrt{h^{2} + \left(\frac{6}{2}\right)^{2}} = \sqrt{81+9} = 3\sqrt{10}\text{ cm}\), so each triangular face has area \(\frac{1}{2}(6)(3\sqrt{10}) = 9\sqrt{10}\text{ cm}^{2}\):
\[S = 6^{2} + 4(9\sqrt{10})\]\[S = 36 + 36\sqrt{10}\]\[\boxed{S \approx 150 \text{ cm}^{2}}\]

Volume and surface area of cones

For a cone of radius \(r\) and height \(h\), with slant height \(l = \sqrt{r^{2}+h^{2}}\):
\[V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^{2}h\]\[S = \pi r^{2} + \pi rl\]where the two terms of the surface area formula are the area of the circular base and the curved lateral surface, respectively.
Worked exampleA cone has radius \(3\text{ cm}\) and height \(4\text{ cm}\). Find the exact volume and the exact surface area of the cone.
\[l = \sqrt{r^{2}+h^{2}}\]\[l = \sqrt{3^{2}+4^{2}} = 5 \text{ cm}\]\[V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^{2}h\]\[V = \frac{1}{3}\pi (3)^{2}(4)\]\[\boxed{V = 12\pi \text{ cm}^{3} \approx 37.7 \text{ cm}^{3}}\]\[S = \pi r^{2} + \pi rl\]\[S = \pi (3)^{2} + \pi (3)(5)\]\[\boxed{S = 24\pi \text{ cm}^{2} \approx 75.4 \text{ cm}^{2}}\]

Volume and surface area of spheres

For a sphere of radius \(r\):
\[V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}\]\[S = 4\pi r^{2}\]
Worked exampleA sphere has radius \(6\text{ cm}\). Find the exact volume and the exact surface area of the sphere.
\[V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}\]\[V = \frac{4}{3}\pi (6)^{3}\]\[\boxed{V = 288\pi \text{ cm}^{3} \approx 905 \text{ cm}^{3}}\]\[S = 4\pi r^{2}\]\[S = 4\pi (6)^{2}\]\[\boxed{S = 144\pi \text{ cm}^{2} \approx 452 \text{ cm}^{2}}\]

Volume and surface area of composite solids

A solid formed by combining two or more basic solids (such as a cone, cylinder, pyramid, or hemisphere) has a volume equal to the sum (or difference) of the volumes of its individual parts. Its surface area is found by adding only the faces that form the outer surface of the composite solid, excluding any internal faces where the parts join.
Worked exampleA grain silo consists of a cylinder of radius \(5\text{ m}\) and height \(12\text{ m}\), topped with a cone of the same radius and height \(4\text{ m}\). The base of the silo is not included in its surface. Find the volume and surface area of the silo, correct to 3 significant figures.
The slant height of the cone is \(l = \sqrt{5^{2}+4^{2}} = \sqrt{41}\text{ m}\).
\[V = \pi r^{2}h_{\text{cyl}} + \frac{1}{3}\pi r^{2}h_{\text{cone}}\]\[V = \pi (5)^{2}(12) + \frac{1}{3}\pi (5)^{2}(4)\]\[V = 300\pi + \frac{100}{3}\pi = \frac{1000}{3}\pi\]\[\boxed{V \approx 1050 \text{ m}^{3}}\]The outer surface consists of the curved cylinder surface and the curved cone surface only (no base, and no join circle since the cone sits directly on the cylinder):
\[S = 2\pi rh_{\text{cyl}} + \pi rl\]\[S = 2\pi (5)(12) + \pi (5)\sqrt{41}\]\[\boxed{S \approx 478 \text{ m}^{2}}\]
