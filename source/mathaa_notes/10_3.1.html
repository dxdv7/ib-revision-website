Arithmetic sequences and the common difference

A sequence \(a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots\) is an arithmetic sequence if the difference between every pair of consecutive terms is a constant \(d\), called the common difference:
\[d = a_{n+1} - a_n\]Equivalently, each term is generated from the previous one by the recursive rule \(a_{n+1} = a_n + d\).
Worked exampleFind the common difference of the arithmetic sequence \(4, 15, 26, 37, \ldots\)
\[d = 15 - 4\]\[\boxed{d = 11}\]

The nth term formula

The nth term of an arithmetic sequence with first term \(a_1\) and common difference \(d\) is given by the explicit formula:
\[a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d\]
Worked exampleAn arithmetic sequence has first term \(a_1 = 6\) and common difference \(d = 5\). Find an explicit formula for the nth term.
\[a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d\]\[a_n = 6 + (n-1)(5)\]\[\boxed{a_n = 5n + 1}\]

Finding a particular term

To find a particular term of an arithmetic sequence, substitute the term number \(n\), the first term \(a_1\), and the common difference \(d\) into the nth term formula \(a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d\).
Worked exampleFind the 16th term of the arithmetic sequence with first term \(9\) and common difference \(7\).
\[a_{16} = a_1 + (n-1)d\]\[a_{16} = 9 + (16-1)(7)\]\[\boxed{a_{16} = 114}\]

Sum of a finite arithmetic series

The sum of the first \(n\) terms of an arithmetic series, denoted \(S_n\), can be found using either of the equivalent formulas:
\[S_n = \frac{n}{2}\left(2a_1 + (n-1)d\right)\]\[S_n = \frac{n}{2}\left(a_1 + a_n\right)\]where \(a_n\) is the last term being summed.
Worked exampleFind the sum of the first 20 terms of the arithmetic series with first term \(3\) and common difference \(5\).
\[S_{20} = \frac{20}{2}\left(2(3) + (20-1)(5)\right)\]\[S_{20} = 10(6 + 95)\]\[\boxed{S_{20} = 1010}\]

Solving for n given the sum

If the sum \(S_n\) of an arithmetic series is known, an equation in \(n\) can be formed from \(S_n = \frac{n}{2}\left(2a_1 + (n-1)d\right)\). This rearranges into a quadratic in \(n\); since \(n\) must be a positive integer, only the positive root of the quadratic gives a valid solution.
Worked exampleThe sum of the first \(n\) terms of an arithmetic series with first term \(5\) and common difference \(3\) is \(343\). Find \(n\).
\[343 = \frac{n}{2}\left(2(5) + (n-1)(3)\right)\]\[686 = n(3n + 7)\]\[3n^2 + 7n - 686 = 0\]\[n = \frac{-7 + \sqrt{49 + 8232}}{6} = \frac{-7 + 91}{6}\]\[\boxed{n = 14}\]
