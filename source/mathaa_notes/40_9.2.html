The power rule

For any rational number \(n\), the derivative of a power of \(x\) is found by multiplying by the exponent and then reducing the exponent by \(1\):
\[\frac{d}{dx}(x^n) = nx^{n-1}\]This single rule applies to positive, negative, and fractional exponents alike.
Worked exampleDifferentiate \(f(x) = x^7\).
\[f'(x) = 7x^{7-1}\]\[\boxed{f'(x) = 7x^6}\]

The derivative of a constant, and the sum and difference rule

The derivative of a constant term is always zero, since a constant function has a horizontal graph with gradient \(0\):
\[\frac{d}{dx}(c) = 0, \quad c \in \mathbb{R}\]To differentiate a sum or difference of terms, differentiate each term separately and multiply each derivative by its constant coefficient:
\[\frac{d}{dx}\big[a\,g(x) + b\,h(x)\big] = a\,g'(x) + b\,h'(x)\]
Worked exampleDifferentiate \(f(x) = 4x^3 - 5x^2 + 7x - 9\).
\[f'(x) = 4(3x^2) - 5(2x) + 7(1) - 0\]\[\boxed{f'(x) = 12x^2 - 10x + 7}\]

Differentiating terms with negative and fractional exponents

Before applying the power rule, rewrite every term in the form \(x^n\). A term in a denominator can be rewritten using a negative exponent, \(\dfrac{1}{x^n} = x^{-n}\), and a root can be rewritten using a fractional exponent, \(\sqrt[n]{x^m} = x^{\frac{m}{n}}\).
Worked exampleDifferentiate \(f(x) = \dfrac{3}{x^2} + 4\sqrt{x}\).
Rewrite each term as a power of \(x\):
\[f(x) = 3x^{-2} + 4x^{\frac{1}{2}}\]Differentiate term by term:
\[f'(x) = 3(-2x^{-3}) + 4\left(\frac{1}{2}x^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\]\[\boxed{f'(x) = -\frac{6}{x^3} + \frac{2}{\sqrt{x}}}\]

The derivative of sine

Provided \(x\) is measured in radians:
\[\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x) = \cos x\]
Worked exampleDifferentiate \(f(x) = 5\sin x - x^2 + 3\).
\[f'(x) = 5\cos x - 2x + 0\]\[\boxed{f'(x) = 5\cos x - 2x}\]

The derivative of cosine

Provided \(x\) is measured in radians:
\[\frac{d}{dx}(\cos x) = -\sin x\]
Worked exampleDifferentiate \(f(x) = 2\cos x + 7x^3\).
\[f'(x) = 2(-\sin x) + 7(3x^2)\]\[\boxed{f'(x) = -2\sin x + 21x^2}\]
