The concept of a limit

The statement
\[\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L\]means that the values of \(f(x)\) can be made arbitrarily close to the number \(L\) by choosing \(x\) sufficiently close to (but not equal to) \(a\). The function does not need to be defined at \(x = a\) itself for this limit to exist.
Worked exampleInvestigate \(\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\sin x}{x}\) (with \(x\) in radians) by evaluating \(\dfrac{\sin x}{x}\) for values of \(x\) close to \(0\).
\[x = 0.1: \quad \frac{\sin(0.1)}{0.1} \approx 0.99833\]\[x = 0.01: \quad \frac{\sin(0.01)}{0.01} \approx 0.99998\]\[x = 0.001: \quad \frac{\sin(0.001)}{0.001} \approx 0.9999998\]As \(x\) gets closer to \(0\), the value gets closer to \(1\):
\[\boxed{\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1}\]

Evaluating limits by direct substitution

If \(f\) is defined at \(x = a\) and its graph has no break, jump or hole there, the limit can be evaluated simply by substituting \(x = a\) into the function:
\[\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)\]
Worked exampleFind \(\lim_{x \to 2} (3x^2 - 5x + 1)\).
\[\lim_{x \to 2} (3x^2 - 5x + 1) = 3(2)^2 - 5(2) + 1\]\[\lim_{x \to 2} (3x^2 - 5x + 1) = 12 - 10 + 1\]\[\boxed{\lim_{x \to 2} (3x^2 - 5x + 1) = 3}\]

Limits that require algebraic simplification

If direct substitution produces the indeterminate form \(\dfrac{0}{0}\), the function can often be rewritten (for example by factoring) so that a common factor cancels, producing a new expression that agrees with the original everywhere except at \(x = a\) and that can be evaluated directly at \(a\).
Worked exampleFind \(\lim_{x \to 3} \dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3}\).
Direct substitution gives \(\dfrac{0}{0}\), so factor the numerator:
\[\frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3} = \frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3} = x + 3, \quad x \ne 3\]\[\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3} = \lim_{x \to 3} (x + 3)\]\[\boxed{\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3} = 6}\]

From secant to tangent: the difference quotient

For a curve \(y = f(x)\), let \(P(x, f(x))\) and \(Q(x+h, f(x+h))\) be two points on the curve. The gradient of the secant line \(PQ\) is the difference quotient:
\[m_{PQ} = \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}\]As \(h \to 0\), point \(Q\) slides along the curve towards \(P\), and the secant line \(PQ\) approaches the tangent line to the curve at \(P\).
Worked exampleFor \(f(x) = x^2 + 1\), find the gradient of the secant line through the points where \(x = 1\) and \(x = 1 + h\), then evaluate it for \(h = 0.1\) and \(h = 0.01\) to estimate the gradient of the tangent at \(x = 1\).
\[m_{PQ} = \frac{f(1+h) - f(1)}{h} = \frac{[(1+h)^2 + 1] - 2}{h}\]\[m_{PQ} = \frac{2h + h^2}{h} = 2 + h\]\[h = 0.1: \quad m_{PQ} = 2.1 \qquad h = 0.01: \quad m_{PQ} = 2.01\]As \(h \to 0\), \(m_{PQ} \to 2\):
\[\boxed{\text{gradient of the tangent at } x=1 \text{ is } 2}\]

The derivative as the limit of the difference quotient

The derivative of \(f\) at \(x\), denoted \(f'(x)\) or \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\), is defined as:
\[f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}\]provided this limit exists. Geometrically, \(f'(x)\) is the gradient of the tangent to the graph of \(y = f(x)\) at the point \((x, f(x))\).
Worked exampleUse the definition of the derivative to find \(f'(x)\) for \(f(x) = x^2\).
\[f(x+h) - f(x) = (x+h)^2 - x^2 = 2xh + h^2\]\[\frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} = \frac{2xh + h^2}{h} = 2x + h\]\[f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} (2x + h)\]\[\boxed{f'(x) = 2x}\]

The derivative as an instantaneous rate of change

The gradient of a secant line is an average rate of change of \(y\) with respect to \(x\) over an interval. The derivative \(f'(x)\) is the limit of this average rate of change as the interval shrinks to a single point, so \(f'(x)\) is the instantaneous rate of change of \(y\) with respect to \(x\). If \(s(t)\) gives the position of an object at time \(t\), then \(s'(t)\) is its instantaneous velocity.
Worked exampleThe position of an object at time \(t\) is \(s(t) = t^2 + 3t\). Use the definition of the derivative to find \(s'(t)\), then find the instantaneous velocity at \(t = 2\).
\[s(t+h) - s(t) = [(t+h)^2 + 3(t+h)] - [t^2 + 3t]\]\[s(t+h) - s(t) = 2th + h^2 + 3h\]\[\frac{s(t+h) - s(t)}{h} = 2t + h + 3\]\[s'(t) = \lim_{h \to 0} (2t + h + 3)\]\[s'(t) = 2t + 3\]\[\boxed{s'(2) = 2(2) + 3 = 7}\]
