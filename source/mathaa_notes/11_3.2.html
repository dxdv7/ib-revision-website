Geometric sequences and the common ratio

A sequence \(a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots\) is a geometric sequence if the ratio between every pair of consecutive terms is a constant \(r\), called the common ratio:
\[r = \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\]Equivalently, each term is generated from the previous one by the recursive rule \(a_{n+1} = a_n \cdot r\).
Worked exampleFind the common ratio of the geometric sequence \(5, -15, 45, -135, \ldots\)
\[r = \frac{-15}{5}\]\[\boxed{r = -3}\]

The nth term formula

The nth term of a geometric sequence with first term \(a_1\) and common ratio \(r\) is given by the explicit formula:
\[a_n = a_1 \cdot r^{\,n-1}\]
Worked exampleA geometric sequence has first term \(a_1 = 5\) and common ratio \(r = -2\). Find an explicit formula for the nth term.
\[a_n = a_1 \cdot r^{\,n-1}\]\[\boxed{a_n = 5 \cdot (-2)^{\,n-1}}\]

Finding a particular term

To find a particular term of a geometric sequence, substitute the term number \(n\), the first term \(a_1\), and the common ratio \(r\) into the nth term formula \(a_n = a_1 \cdot r^{\,n-1}\).
Worked exampleFind the 6th term of the geometric sequence with first term \(4\) and common ratio \(3\).
\[a_6 = a_1 \cdot r^{\,n-1}\]\[a_6 = 4 \cdot 3^5\]\[\boxed{a_6 = 972}\]

Sum of a finite geometric series

For \(r \neq 1\), the sum of the first \(n\) terms of a geometric series, denoted \(S_n\), is given by:
\[S_n = \frac{a_1\left(1 - r^n\right)}{1 - r}\]
Worked exampleFind the sum of the first 6 terms of the geometric series with first term \(3\) and common ratio \(2\).
\[S_6 = \frac{3\left(1 - 2^6\right)}{1 - 2}\]\[S_6 = \frac{3(1 - 64)}{-1}\]\[\boxed{S_6 = 189}\]

Sum to infinity

When \(|r| < 1\), the terms of a geometric series shrink toward zero and the sum of infinitely many terms converges to a finite value, the sum to infinity:
\[S_\infty = \frac{a_1}{1 - r}\]When \(|r| \geq 1\), the sum to infinity does not exist.
Worked exampleFind the sum to infinity of the geometric series with first term \(6\) and common ratio \(\dfrac{1}{3}\).
\[S_\infty = \frac{6}{1 - \frac{1}{3}}\]\[S_\infty = \frac{6}{\frac{2}{3}}\]\[\boxed{S_\infty = 9}\]
