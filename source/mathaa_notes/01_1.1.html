The gradient of a line

The gradient (or slope) \(m\) of a line through two points \(A(x_1, y_1)\) and \(B(x_2, y_2)\) measures how steeply the line rises or falls, found by dividing the vertical change by the horizontal change:
\[m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\]where \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) are any two distinct points on the line.
Worked exampleFind the gradient of the line through \(A(-1, 2)\) and \(B(5, 20)\).
\[m = \frac{20 - 2}{5 - (-1)}\]\[m = \frac{18}{6}\]\[\boxed{m = 3}\]

The slope-intercept form of a line

A linear equation written as \(y = mx + c\) is in slope-intercept form, where \(m\) is the gradient of the line and \((0, c)\) is the point at which the line crosses the \(y\)-axis, the \(y\)-intercept.
Worked exampleFind, in slope-intercept form, the equation of the line with gradient \(4\) that passes through the point \((2, 5)\).
\[y = mx + c\]\[5 = 4(2) + c\]\[5 = 8 + c\]\[c = -3\]\[\boxed{y = 4x - 3}\]

The point-slope form of a line

A linear equation can be written in point-slope form as:
\[y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\]where \(m\) is the gradient of the line and \((x_1, y_1)\) is any known point on the line. This form is useful for writing down the equation of a line directly when a point and the gradient are known.
Worked exampleFind, in slope-intercept form, the equation of the line through \((3, -2)\) with gradient \(-\dfrac{1}{2}\).
\[y - (-2) = -\frac{1}{2}(x - 3)\]\[y + 2 = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{3}{2}\]\[y = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{3}{2} - 2\]\[\boxed{y = -\frac{1}{2}x - \frac{1}{2}}\]

Finding the equation of a line through two points

To find the equation of a line through two known points \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\): first find the gradient \(m\) using the gradient formula, then substitute \(m\) and either point into the point-slope form to obtain the equation.
Worked exampleFind, in slope-intercept form, the equation of the line through \((-3, 4)\) and \((1, -8)\).
\[m = \frac{-8 - 4}{1 - (-3)}\]\[m = \frac{-12}{4} = -3\]\[y - 4 = -3(x - (-3))\]\[y - 4 = -3x - 9\]\[\boxed{y = -3x - 5}\]

The general form of a line

Every line can be written in general form:
\[ax + by + c = 0\]where \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) are constants and \(a\) and \(b\) are not both zero. To find the gradient and \(y\)-intercept of a line given in general form, rearrange it into slope-intercept form by solving for \(y\).
Worked exampleWrite the line \(3x + 4y - 8 = 0\) in slope-intercept form, and state its gradient and \(y\)-intercept.
\[4y = -3x + 8\]\[\boxed{y = -\frac{3}{4}x + 2}\]The gradient is \(m = -\dfrac{3}{4}\) and the \(y\)-intercept is \((0, 2)\).

Horizontal and vertical lines

A horizontal line has equation \(y = c\), with gradient \(m = 0\). A vertical line has equation \(x = c\), with undefined gradient, since the horizontal change between any two points on it is zero.
Worked exampleFind the equations of the horizontal and vertical lines that pass through the point \((5, -3)\).
Horizontal line:
\[\boxed{y = -3}\]Vertical line:
\[\boxed{x = 5}\]
