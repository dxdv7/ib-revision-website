Converting between degrees and radians

Since a straight angle measures \(180^{\circ}\) and \(\pi\) radians:
\[180^{\circ} = \pi \text{ radians}\]\[1^{\circ} = \frac{\pi}{180}\text{ radians} \qquad 1 \text{ radian} = \frac{180^{\circ}}{\pi}\]To convert degrees to radians, multiply by \(\frac{\pi}{180^{\circ}}\); to convert radians to degrees, multiply by \(\frac{180^{\circ}}{\pi}\).
Worked exampleConvert (a) \(210^{\circ}\) to exact radian measure, and (b) \(\frac{7\pi}{4}\) to degrees.
(a)
\[210^{\circ} \times \frac{\pi}{180^{\circ}} = \frac{210\pi}{180}\]\[\boxed{= \frac{7\pi}{6}}\]
(b)
\[\frac{7\pi}{4} \times \frac{180^{\circ}}{\pi} = \frac{7 \times 180^{\circ}}{4}\]\[\boxed{= 315^{\circ}}\]

The radian measure of an angle

For a central angle \(\theta\) of a circle of radius \(r\) subtending an arc of length \(s\), the radian measure of \(\theta\) is the ratio of arc length to radius:
\[\theta = \frac{s}{r}\]This ratio is independent of the size of the circle. One radian is the measure of a central angle whose subtended arc length equals the radius, so that \(\theta = 1 \implies s = r\).
Worked exampleAn arc of length \(18\) cm is subtended by a central angle on a circle of radius \(8\) cm. Find the radian measure of the angle.
\[\theta = \frac{s}{r}\]\[\theta = \frac{18}{8}\]\[\boxed{\theta = \frac{9}{4} \text{ radians} = 2.25 \text{ radians}}\]

Common angle equivalences

Many common angles are convenient multiples of \(30^{\circ} = \frac{\pi}{6}\) or \(45^{\circ} = \frac{\pi}{4}\). Recognising these multiples allows quick mental conversion between degrees and radians.
Worked exampleConvert (a) \(300^{\circ}\) to radians, and (b) \(\frac{11\pi}{6}\) to degrees, using known multiples of \(30^{\circ}\).
(a)
\[300^{\circ} = 10 \times 30^{\circ} = 10 \times \frac{\pi}{6}\]\[\boxed{= \frac{5\pi}{3}}\]
(b)
\[\frac{11\pi}{6} = 11 \times \frac{\pi}{6} = 11 \times 30^{\circ}\]\[\boxed{= 330^{\circ}}\]

Arc length

For a circle of radius \(r\), a central angle \(\theta\), measured in radians, subtends an arc of length:
\[s = r\theta\]This formula is true only when \(\theta\) is measured in radians.
Worked exampleA circle has radius \(12\) cm. Find the exact length of the arc subtended by a central angle of \(75^{\circ}\), and give the answer to 4 significant figures.
\[75^{\circ} \times \frac{\pi}{180^{\circ}} = \frac{5\pi}{12}\]\[s = r\theta\]\[s = 12 \times \frac{5\pi}{12}\]\[s = 5\pi\]\[\boxed{s = 5\pi \approx 15.71 \text{ cm}}\]

Finding an angle from the arc length formula

Rearranging \(s = r\theta\) gives \(\theta = \frac{s}{r}\), which can be used to find the central angle (in radians) when the arc length and radius are known. The result can then be converted to degrees if required.
Worked exampleA sector has radius \(14\) cm and arc length \(21\) cm. Find the central angle in degrees, to 3 significant figures.
\[\theta = \frac{s}{r}\]\[\theta = \frac{21}{14}\]\[\theta = 1.5 \text{ radians}\]\[1.5 \times \frac{180^{\circ}}{\pi}\]\[\boxed{\theta \approx 85.9^{\circ}}\]

Sector area

The area of a sector of a circle with radius \(r\) and central angle \(\theta\) (in radians) is:
\[A = \frac{1}{2}r^{2}\theta\]This formula is true only when \(\theta\) is measured in radians.
Worked exampleA circle has radius \(8\) cm and a sector with central angle \(\frac{\pi}{3}\). Find the exact arc length and the exact area of the sector.
\[s = r\theta\]\[s = 8 \times \frac{\pi}{3}\]\[\boxed{s = \frac{8\pi}{3} \text{ cm}}\]\[A = \frac{1}{2}r^{2}\theta\]\[A = \frac{1}{2}(8)^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)\]\[\boxed{A = \frac{32\pi}{3} \text{ cm}^{2}}\]
