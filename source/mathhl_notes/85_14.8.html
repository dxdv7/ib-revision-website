Volumes of revolution about the x-axis

If the region bounded by \(y=f(x)\), the \(x\)-axis, and the lines \(x=a\) and \(x=b\) is rotated \(360^\circ\) about the \(x\)-axis, the cross-section perpendicular to the \(x\)-axis at any point is a circular disk of radius \(f(x)\), with area \(\pi[f(x)]^2\). Integrating this area over the interval gives the volume of the resulting solid:
\[V = \pi\int_a^b [f(x)]^2\,dx\]
Worked exampleFind the volume generated when the region under \(y=\sqrt{x}\) between \(x=0\) and \(x=4\) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis.
Here \([f(x)]^2 = x\), so:
\[V = \pi\int_0^4 x\,dx = \pi\left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^4 = \pi(8-0)\]\[\boxed{V = 8\pi \text{ cubic units}}\]

A full worked example: deriving the volume of a cone

A cone of base radius \(r\) and height \(h\) is generated by rotating the line segment from the origin to \((h,r)\) about the \(x\)-axis. This line has equation \(y=\frac{r}{h}x\), so the disk method can be used to derive the familiar cone volume formula directly.
Worked exampleRotate \(y=\dfrac{r}{h}x\) about the \(x\)-axis between \(x=0\) and \(x=h\), and find the resulting volume.
Here \([f(x)]^2 = \dfrac{r^2}{h^2}x^2\), so:
\[V = \pi\int_0^h \frac{r^2}{h^2}x^2\,dx = \frac{\pi r^2}{h^2}\left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^h = \frac{\pi r^2}{h^2}\cdot\frac{h^3}{3}\]\[\boxed{V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h}\]This matches the standard geometric formula for the volume of a cone, confirming the method.

Volumes of revolution about the y-axis

If a region bounded by \(x=g(y)\), the \(y\)-axis, and the lines \(y=c\) and \(y=d\) is rotated about the \(y\)-axis, the roles of \(x\) and \(y\) are simply interchanged in the disk method: each disk now has radius \(g(y)\) and thickness \(dy\):
\[V = \pi\int_c^d [g(y)]^2\,dy\]
Worked exampleFind the volume generated when the region bounded by \(x=y^2\), the \(y\)-axis, and \(y=2\) is rotated about the \(y\)-axis.
Here \([g(y)]^2 = (y^2)^2=y^4\), so:
\[V = \pi\int_0^2 y^4\,dy = \pi\left[\frac{y^5}{5}\right]_0^2 = \pi\cdot\frac{32}{5}\]\[\boxed{V = \frac{32\pi}{5} \text{ cubic units}}\]

A full worked example: rotation about the y-axis requiring rearrangement

A curve is often given as \(y=f(x)\) even when the rotation is about the \(y\)-axis. Before the \(y\)-axis volume formula can be used, the equation must first be rearranged to express \(x\) as a function of \(y\), i.e. \(x=g(y)\), and the bounds must also be expressed as \(y\)-values.
Worked exampleFind the volume generated when the region bounded by \(y=2x\), the \(y\)-axis, and \(y=4\) is rotated about the \(y\)-axis.
Rearranging \(y=2x\) gives \(x=\dfrac{y}{2}\), so \([g(y)]^2=\dfrac{y^2}{4}\), with \(y\) ranging from \(0\) to \(4\):
\[V = \pi\int_0^4 \frac{y^2}{4}\,dy = \frac{\pi}{4}\left[\frac{y^3}{3}\right]_0^4 = \frac{\pi}{4}\cdot\frac{64}{3}\]\[\boxed{V = \frac{16\pi}{3} \text{ cubic units}}\]

Volumes between two curves: the washer method

If a region between two curves \(y=f(x)\) and \(y=g(x)\), with \(f(x)\geq g(x)\geq 0\) on \([a,b]\), is rotated about the \(x\)-axis, each cross-section is a washer (an annulus): a disk of outer radius \(f(x)\) with a smaller disk of inner radius \(g(x)\) removed. Its area is \(\pi\big([f(x)]^2-[g(x)]^2\big)\), giving a volume equal to the outer solid's volume minus the inner solid's volume:
\[V = \pi\int_a^b \Big([f(x)]^2-[g(x)]^2\Big)\,dx\]
Worked exampleThe region between \(f(x)=4\) and \(g(x)=x^2\) (where \(f(x)\geq g(x)\)) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis. Find the volume generated, using the interval where the curves intersect.
Setting \(4=x^2\) gives \(x=\pm 2\), so the interval is \([-2,2]\). Then:
\[V = \pi\int_{-2}^{2}\Big(16-x^4\Big)\,dx = \pi\left[16x-\frac{x^5}{5}\right]_{-2}^{2}\]At \(x=2\): \(32-\frac{32}{5}=\frac{128}{5}\). At \(x=-2\): \(-32+\frac{32}{5}=-\frac{128}{5}\).
\[V = \pi\left(\frac{128}{5}-\left(-\frac{128}{5}\right)\right)\]\[\boxed{V = \frac{256\pi}{5} \text{ cubic units}}\]

The washer method about the y-axis

The washer method applies equally when rotating a region between two curves \(x=f(y)\) and \(x=g(y)\) about the \(y\)-axis, with \(f(y)\geq g(y)\geq 0\) on \([c,d]\):
\[V = \pi\int_c^d \Big([f(y)]^2-[g(y)]^2\Big)\,dy\]
Worked exampleThe region between \(x=y\) and \(x=y^2\), for \(0\leq y\leq 1\) (where \(y\geq y^2\) on this interval), is rotated about the \(y\)-axis. Find the volume generated.
Here \([f(y)]^2-[g(y)]^2 = y^2-y^4\), so:
\[V = \pi\int_0^1 \Big(y^2-y^4\Big)\,dy = \pi\left[\frac{y^3}{3}-\frac{y^5}{5}\right]_0^1 = \pi\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{5}\right)\]\[\boxed{V = \frac{2\pi}{15} \text{ cubic units}}\]
