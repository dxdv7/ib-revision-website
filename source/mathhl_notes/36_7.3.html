Trigonometric ratios in a right-angled triangle

For an acute angle \(\theta\) in a right-angled triangle:
\[\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\]
Worked exampleIn triangle \(ABC\), the right angle is at \(C\), angle \(A = 30^\circ\), and the hypotenuse \(c = 8.76\) cm. Find the lengths of \(a\) and \(b\).
\[\sin 30^\circ = \frac{a}{8.76} \implies a = 8.76 \sin 30^\circ = 8.76\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\]\[\boxed{a = 4.38 \text{ cm}}\]\[\cos 30^\circ = \frac{b}{8.76} \implies b = 8.76\cos 30^\circ = 8.76\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)\]\[\boxed{b \approx 7.59 \text{ cm}}\]

Exact trigonometric values of 30, 45, and 60 degrees

\[\sin 30^\circ = \frac{1}{2}, \quad \cos 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \quad \tan 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\]\[\sin 45^\circ = \cos 45^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \quad \tan 45^\circ = 1\]\[\sin 60^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \quad \cos 60^\circ = \frac{1}{2}, \quad \tan 60^\circ = \sqrt{3}\]
Worked exampleFind the exact value of \(\cos 30^\circ \times \tan 60^\circ\).
\[\cos 30^\circ \times \tan 60^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \times \sqrt{3}\]\[\boxed{= \frac{3}{2}}\]

Finding an angle using inverse trigonometric ratios

If the lengths of two sides of a right-angled triangle are known, an unknown acute angle can be found by using the inverse sine, inverse cosine, or inverse tangent function.
Worked exampleA right-angled triangle has legs of length 3 cm and 4 cm meeting at the right angle. Find the angle \(\theta\) opposite the side of length 3 cm, to 3 significant figures.
\[\tan\theta = \frac{3}{4}\]\[\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)\]\[\boxed{\theta \approx 36.9^\circ}\]

Angles of elevation and depression

The angle of elevation of a point \(P\) above an observer \(O\) is the angle between the horizontal line through \(O\) and the line of sight \(OP\). The angle of depression of a point below the observer is defined similarly, measured below the horizontal. The angle of elevation from \(O\) to \(P\) equals the angle of depression from \(P\) to \(O\), since the horizontal lines through \(O\) and \(P\) are parallel.
Worked exampleThe angle of elevation of the top of a cliff from a boat increases from \(10^\circ\) to \(15^\circ\) as the boat sails a distance of 50 m directly towards the base of the cliff. Find the height of the cliff.
Let \(h\) be the height of the cliff and \(x\) the distance from the base of the cliff at the later position.
\[\tan 15^\circ = \frac{h}{x} \implies h = x\tan 15^\circ\]\[\tan 10^\circ = \frac{h}{x + 50} \implies h = (x + 50)\tan 10^\circ\]\[x\tan 15^\circ = (x + 50)\tan 10^\circ\]\[x(\tan 15^\circ - \tan 10^\circ) = 50\tan 10^\circ\]\[x = \frac{50\tan 10^\circ}{\tan 15^\circ - \tan 10^\circ} \approx 96.225\]\[h = x\tan 15^\circ \approx 96.225 \tan 15^\circ\]\[\boxed{h \approx 25.8 \text{ m}}\]

Angle between two lines using gradients

Given two non-vertical lines with gradients \(m_1\) and \(m_2\), each line makes an angle \(\tan^{-1}(m_1)\) or \(\tan^{-1}(m_2)\) with the positive direction of the \(x\)-axis. The acute angle \(\theta\) between the two lines is:
\[\theta = \left|\tan^{-1}(m_1) - \tan^{-1}(m_2)\right|\]
Worked exampleFind the acute angle between the lines \(y = 5x - 2\) and \(y = \dfrac{1}{3}x - 1\).
\[\tan^{-1}(5) \approx 78.690^\circ, \qquad \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right) \approx 18.435^\circ\]\[\theta \approx 78.690^\circ - 18.435^\circ\]\[\boxed{\theta \approx 60.3^\circ}\]

Angle between two lines in three dimensions

In a 3D solid, the angle between two intersecting edges is usually found by identifying or constructing a right-angled triangle that contains the required angle, often by dropping a perpendicular from a vertex to the midpoint of an edge, and then applying trigonometric ratios within that triangle.
Worked exampleA right pyramid has a square base \(EFGH\) with side length 2 cm and apex \(A\) directly above the centre \(C\) of the base, with height \(AC = 2\) cm. Let \(M\) be the midpoint of edge \([GF]\). Find the angle \(A\hat{G}F\) between edge \([GA]\) and edge \([GF]\).
Since \(M\) is the midpoint of \([GF]\) and \(C\) is the centre of the square base, \(CM = 1\) cm, and \([AM]\) is perpendicular to \([GF]\).
\[AM = \sqrt{AC^2 + CM^2} = \sqrt{2^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{5}\]In right triangle \(AMG\), which has a right angle at \(M\) and \(GM = 1\) cm:
\[\tan(A\hat{G}M) = \frac{AM}{GM} = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{1}\]\[A\hat{G}F = \tan^{-1}\left(\sqrt{5}\right)\]\[\boxed{A\hat{G}F \approx 65.9^\circ}\]
