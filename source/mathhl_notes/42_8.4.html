Multiplying complex numbers in polar form

For \(z_1 = r_1(\cos\theta_1+i\sin\theta_1)\) and \(z_2 = r_2(\cos\theta_2+i\sin\theta_2)\):
\[z_1 z_2 = r_1 r_2\big[\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) + i\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)\big] = r_1 r_2 \operatorname{cis}(\theta_1+\theta_2)\]To multiply two complex numbers in polar form, multiply the moduli and add the arguments.
Worked exampleLet \(z_1 = 2+2\sqrt{3}i\) and \(z_2 = -1-\sqrt{3}i\). Write each in polar form and find \(z_1 z_2\).
\[r_1 = \sqrt{2^2+(2\sqrt{3})^2} = \sqrt{4+12} = 4, \qquad \theta_1 = \frac{\pi}{3}\]\[r_2 = \sqrt{(-1)^2+(-\sqrt{3})^2} = \sqrt{1+3} = 2, \qquad \theta_2 = \pi+\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{4\pi}{3}\]\[z_1 = 4\operatorname{cis}\frac{\pi}{3}, \qquad z_2 = 2\operatorname{cis}\frac{4\pi}{3}\]\[z_1 z_2 = 4(2)\operatorname{cis}\left(\frac{\pi}{3}+\frac{4\pi}{3}\right) = 8\operatorname{cis}\frac{5\pi}{3}\]\[\boxed{z_1 z_2 = 8\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}i\right) = 4 - 4\sqrt{3}i}\]

Dividing complex numbers in polar form

For \(z_1 = r_1(\cos\theta_1+i\sin\theta_1)\) and \(z_2 = r_2(\cos\theta_2+i\sin\theta_2)\) with \(z_2 \ne 0\):
\[\frac{z_1}{z_2} = \frac{r_1}{r_2}\big[\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2) + i\sin(\theta_1-\theta_2)\big] = \frac{r_1}{r_2}\operatorname{cis}(\theta_1-\theta_2)\]To divide two complex numbers in polar form, divide the moduli and subtract the arguments.
Worked exampleLet \(z_1 = 1+i\) and \(z_2 = \sqrt{3}-i\). Write each in polar form and find \(\dfrac{z_1}{z_2}\).
\[z_1 = \sqrt{2}\operatorname{cis}\frac{\pi}{4}, \qquad z_2 = 2\operatorname{cis}\left(-\frac{\pi}{6}\right)\]\[\frac{z_1}{z_2} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\operatorname{cis}\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-\left(-\frac{\pi}{6}\right)\right) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\operatorname{cis}\left(\frac{3\pi}{12}+\frac{2\pi}{12}\right)\]\[\boxed{\frac{z_1}{z_2} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\operatorname{cis}\frac{5\pi}{12}}\]

Multiplying and dividing in Euler form

Writing \(z_1 = r_1 e^{i\theta_1}\) and \(z_2 = r_2 e^{i\theta_2}\), the laws of exponents give the same rules as in polar form:
\[z_1 z_2 = r_1 r_2\, e^{i(\theta_1+\theta_2)}, \qquad \frac{z_1}{z_2} = \frac{r_1}{r_2}\, e^{i(\theta_1-\theta_2)}\]
Worked exampleLet \(z_1 = 2e^{i\pi/3}\) and \(z_2 = 4e^{i\pi/6}\). Find \(z_1 z_2\) and \(\dfrac{z_1}{z_2}\) in Cartesian form.
\[z_1 z_2 = 2(4)e^{i(\pi/3+\pi/6)} = 8e^{i\pi/2} = 8\left(\cos\frac{\pi}{2}+i\sin\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\]\[\boxed{z_1 z_2 = 8i}\]\[\frac{z_1}{z_2} = \frac{2}{4}e^{i(\pi/3-\pi/6)} = \frac{1}{2}e^{i\pi/6} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}i\right)\]\[\boxed{\frac{z_1}{z_2} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} + \frac{1}{4}i}\]

Geometric interpretation: rotation and scaling

Multiplying a complex number \(z\) by \(w = s\operatorname{cis}\varphi\) scales \(|z|\) by the factor \(s\) and rotates \(z\) about the origin by angle \(\varphi\) (counterclockwise if \(\varphi>0\)). Dividing \(z\) by \(w\) scales \(|z|\) by \(\dfrac{1}{s}\) and rotates it by \(-\varphi\).
Worked exampleLet \(z = 3+4i\). Describe and find \(iz\).
Since \(i = 1\operatorname{cis}\dfrac{\pi}{2}\), multiplying by \(i\) leaves the modulus unchanged and rotates \(z\) by \(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\) (90 degrees) counterclockwise.
\[iz = i(3+4i) = 3i+4i^2 = -4+3i\]\[\boxed{iz = -4+3i, \qquad |iz| = \sqrt{(-4)^2+3^2} = 5 = |z|}\]

The reciprocal of a complex number in polar form

Taking \(z_1 = 1 = 1\operatorname{cis}0\) in the division rule gives the reciprocal of \(z = r\operatorname{cis}\theta\) (for \(z \ne 0\)):
\[z^{-1} = \frac{1}{z} = \frac{1}{r}\operatorname{cis}(-\theta) = \frac{1}{r}(\cos\theta - i\sin\theta)\]
Worked exampleFind \(\dfrac{1}{z}\) for \(z = 4\operatorname{cis}\dfrac{\pi}{3}\).
\[\frac{1}{z} = \frac{1}{4}\operatorname{cis}\left(-\frac{\pi}{3}\right)\]\[\boxed{\frac{1}{z} = \frac{1}{4}\left(\cos\frac{\pi}{3} - i\sin\frac{\pi}{3}\right) = \frac{1}{8} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{8}i}\]
