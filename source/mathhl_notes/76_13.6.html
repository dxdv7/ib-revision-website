Setting up an optimization problem

Many problems ask for the maximum or minimum value of some quantity \(Q\), which initially depends on two (or more) variables linked by a constraint equation. The method is: (1) identify the quantity \(Q\) to be optimized and write an expression for it, (2) identify the constraint relating the variables, (3) use the constraint to eliminate one variable, writing \(Q\) as a function of a single variable, together with a sensible domain for that variable.
Worked exampleA rectangle has a perimeter of \(40\text{ cm}\). Let \(x\) be its width. Express its area \(A\) as a function of \(x\) alone, stating a suitable domain.
Let \(y\) be the height. The constraint is \(2x+2y=40\), so \(y=20-x\). The area is \(A=xy\), so:

Finding critical points

Once the quantity to be optimized has been written as a function of one variable, \(Q(x)\), its maximum or minimum on the domain occurs either at a critical point, where \(Q'(x)=0\), or at an endpoint of the domain. Critical points are found by differentiating and solving:
\[Q'(x)=0\]
Worked exampleFind the critical point of \(A(x)=20x-x^2\) on \(0\[A'(x)=20-2x\]Setting \(A'(x)=0\):
\[20-2x=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x=10\]\[\boxed{x=10 \text{ is the critical point, with } y=20-10=10}\]

The second derivative test

At a critical point \(x=c\) where \(Q'(c)=0\), the sign of the second derivative classifies the point:
\[Q''(c)>0 \Rightarrow \text{local minimum}, \qquad Q''(c)<0 \Rightarrow \text{local maximum}, \qquad Q''(c)=0 \Rightarrow \text{test inconclusive}\]
Worked exampleConfirm that \(x=10\) gives a maximum of \(A(x)=20x-x^2\), and find the maximum area.
\[A''(x)=-2\]Since \(A''(10)=-2<0\), the critical point gives a local maximum. Since this is the only critical point in the domain, it is the overall maximum. The maximum area is:
\[A(10)=20(10)-10^2=200-100=100\]\[\boxed{\text{Maximum area }=100\text{ cm}^2\text{, when the rectangle is a }10\text{ cm}\times10\text{ cm square}}\]

Classic problem: minimizing the surface area of a box with fixed volume

For an open-topped box with a square base of side \(x\) and height \(h\), the volume is \(V=x^2h\) (the constraint) and the surface area (base plus four sides, no lid) is \(S=x^2+4xh\) (the quantity to minimize). Using the constraint to write \(h=\dfrac{V}{x^2}\) and substituting gives \(S\) as a function of \(x\) alone.
Worked exampleAn open-topped box with a square base is to have a volume of \(500\text{ cm}^3\). Find the dimensions that minimize the surface area, and find that minimum area.
Constraint: \(x^2h=500\), so \(h=\dfrac{500}{x^2}\). Surface area:
\[S(x)=x^2+4xh=x^2+4x\cdot\frac{500}{x^2}=x^2+\frac{2000}{x}, \quad x>0\]Differentiating:
\[S'(x)=2x-\frac{2000}{x^2}\]Setting \(S'(x)=0\):
\[2x=\frac{2000}{x^2} \quad\Rightarrow\quad 2x^3=2000 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x^3=1000 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x=10\]Checking with the second derivative: \(S''(x)=2+\dfrac{4000}{x^3}\), so \(S''(10)=2+4=6>0\), confirming a minimum. Then \(h=\dfrac{500}{10^2}=5\), and:
\[S(10)=10^2+\frac{2000}{10}=100+200=300\]\[\boxed{x=10\text{ cm}, \ h=5\text{ cm}, \ \text{minimum surface area}=300\text{ cm}^2}\]

Optimization with a boundary constraint

Some optimization problems involve an existing boundary (such as a wall or edge) that requires no extra material along that side. The constraint equation then only accounts for the sides that need to be constructed, which changes how the single variable is eliminated.
Worked exampleA farmer has \(80\text{ m}\) of fencing to enclose a rectangular field against a long, straight wall, which forms one side of the field (so no fencing is needed there). Let \(x\) be the width of the field, perpendicular to the wall, and \(y\) the length parallel to the wall. Find the dimensions that maximize the enclosed area, and find that maximum area.
The fencing is used for two widths and one length: \(2x+y=80\), so \(y=80-2x\). The area is:
\[A(x)=xy=x(80-2x)=80x-2x^2, \quad 0\[A'(x)=80-4x\]Setting \(A'(x)=0\):
\[80-4x=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x=20\]Since \(A''(x)=-4<0\) for all \(x\), this critical point is a maximum. Then \(y=80-2(20)=40\), and:
\[A(20)=20(40)=800\]\[\boxed{x=20\text{ m}, \ y=40\text{ m}, \ \text{maximum area}=800\text{ m}^2}\]
