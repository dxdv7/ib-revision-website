Vector equation of a plane

A plane through the point with position vector \(\mathbf{a}\), parallel to two non-parallel vectors \(\mathbf{b}\) and \(\mathbf{c}\), has vector equation:
\[\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{a} + \lambda\mathbf{b} + \mu\mathbf{c}\]where \(\mathbf{r}\) is the position vector of a general point on the plane, and \(\lambda\), \(\mu\) are independent scalar parameters. This equation is not unique, since any point in the plane and any two non-parallel direction vectors in the plane may be used.
Worked exampleWrite a vector equation of the plane through \(A(2,-1,3)\), parallel to \(\mathbf{b} = (1,2,-1)\) and \(\mathbf{c} = (2,-1,3)\).
\[\boxed{\mathbf{r} = (2,-1,3) + \lambda(1,2,-1) + \mu(2,-1,3)}\]

Finding a normal vector to a plane

A vector normal (perpendicular) to a plane with direction vectors \(\mathbf{b}\) and \(\mathbf{c}\) can be found using the vector product:
\[\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}\]Any nonzero scalar multiple of \(\mathbf{n}\) is also a normal to the same plane.
Worked exampleFind a normal vector to the plane from the previous example, with \(\mathbf{b} = (1,2,-1)\) and \(\mathbf{c} = (2,-1,3)\).
\[b_2c_3-b_3c_2 = (2)(3)-(-1)(-1) = 6-1 = 5\]\[b_3c_1-b_1c_3 = (-1)(2)-(1)(3) = -2-3 = -5\]\[b_1c_2-b_2c_1 = (1)(-1)-(2)(2) = -1-4 = -5\]\[\mathbf{n} = (5,-5,-5) = 5(1,-1,-1)\]\[\boxed{\text{A normal vector is } \mathbf{n} = (1,-1,-1)}\]

Normal form of a plane

If a plane has normal vector \(\mathbf{n}\) and passes through the point with position vector \(\mathbf{a}\), then every point \(\mathbf{r}\) on the plane satisfies:
\[\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{n}\]This is often found by first computing \(\mathbf{n}\) as the vector product of the plane's two direction vectors.
Worked exampleFind the normal form of the plane through \(A(2,-1,3)\) with normal \(\mathbf{n} = (1,-1,-1)\).
\[\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{n} = (2)(1)+(-1)(-1)+(3)(-1) = 2+1-3 = 0\]\[\boxed{\mathbf{r}\cdot(1,-1,-1) = 0}\]

Cartesian equation of a plane

Writing the normal as \(\mathbf{n} = (a,b,c)\) and \(\mathbf{r} = (x,y,z)\), expanding \(\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{n}\) gives the Cartesian equation of the plane:
\[ax+by+cz = d\]where \(d = \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{n}\) is a constant.
Worked exampleFind the Cartesian equation of the plane with normal form \(\mathbf{r}\cdot(1,-1,-1) = 0\).
\[(x,y,z)\cdot(1,-1,-1) = x - y - z\]\[\boxed{x - y - z = 0}\]

Equation of a plane through three points

Given three non-collinear points \(P\), \(Q\), and \(R\) on a plane, form two direction vectors \(\overrightarrow{PQ}\) and \(\overrightarrow{PR}\). Their vector product gives a normal:
\[\mathbf{n} = \overrightarrow{PQ}\times\overrightarrow{PR}\]and the Cartesian equation then follows from \(\mathbf{n}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{p}) = 0\), using the position vector \(\mathbf{p}\) of \(P\).
Worked exampleFind the Cartesian equation of the plane through \(P(1,2,0)\), \(Q(3,-1,4)\), and \(R(0,1,-2)\).
\[\overrightarrow{PQ} = (2,-3,4), \qquad \overrightarrow{PR} = (-1,-1,-2)\]\[(-3)(-2)-(4)(-1) = 6+4 = 10\]\[(4)(-1)-(2)(-2) = -4+4 = 0\]\[(2)(-1)-(-3)(-1) = -2-3 = -5\]\[\mathbf{n} = (10,0,-5) = 5(2,0,-1)\]Using the normal \((2,0,-1)\) and point \(P(1,2,0)\):
\[2(x-1) + 0(y-2) - 1(z-0) = 0\]\[\boxed{2x - z = 2}\]

Converting between Cartesian and vector form

Given a Cartesian equation \(ax+by+cz=d\), a vector equation can be recovered by solving for one variable in terms of the other two, which are then used as the two parameters. The resulting direction vectors will automatically be perpendicular to the normal \((a,b,c)\).
Worked exampleWrite the plane \(2x - z = 2\) (from the previous example) in vector form.
\[z = 2x - 2, \qquad \text{let } x = \lambda, \ y = \mu\]\[\boxed{\mathbf{r} = (0,0,-2) + \lambda(1,0,2) + \mu(0,1,0)}\]Checking: \((1,0,2)\cdot(2,0,-1) = 2+0-2 = 0\) and \((0,1,0)\cdot(2,0,-1) = 0\), so both direction vectors are indeed perpendicular to the normal.
