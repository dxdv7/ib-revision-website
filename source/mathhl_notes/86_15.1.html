Discrete random variables and their probability distribution

A discrete random variable \(X\) is a variable that takes isolated numerical values \(x_i\), each occurring with some probability, as the outcome of a random experiment. Its probability distribution lists every possible value alongside its probability, often as a table of \(x_i\) and \(P(X=x_i)\). Since the values are mutually exclusive and exhaustive:
\[\sum_i P(X=x_i) = 1\]with \(0 \leq P(X=x_i) \leq 1\) for every \(x_i\).
Worked exampleTwo fair coins are tossed and \(X\) is the number of heads obtained. Show that the probability distribution of \(X\) is valid.
The possible outcomes give \(X=0\) with probability \(\tfrac14\), \(X=1\) with probability \(\tfrac12\), and \(X=2\) with probability \(\tfrac14\):
\[\begin{array}{c|ccc}x & 0 & 1 & 2\\\hline P(X=x) & 0.25 & 0.5 & 0.25\end{array}\]Each probability lies between 0 and 1, and summing gives \(0.25+0.5+0.25=1\).
\[\boxed{\text{This is a valid probability distribution, since } \textstyle\sum P(X=x)=1}\]

Expected value of a discrete random variable

The expected value (mean) of a discrete random variable \(X\) with probability distribution \(P(X=x_i)\) is:
\[E(X) = \mu = \sum_i x_i P(X=x_i)\]This is the long-run average value of \(X\) if the experiment were repeated an infinite number of times; it need not be a value \(X\) can actually take.
Worked exampleThe number of pets owned by a household, \(X\), has distribution:
\[\begin{array}{c|cccc}x & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3\\\hline P(X=x) & 0.1 & 0.4 & 0.3 & 0.2\end{array}\]Find \(E(X)\).
\[E(X) = 0(0.1) + 1(0.4) + 2(0.3) + 3(0.2)\]\[E(X) = 0 + 0.4 + 0.6 + 0.6\]\[\boxed{E(X) = 1.6}\]

Finding an unknown probability

Since the probabilities in a discrete distribution must sum to 1, any single unknown probability can be found by subtracting the sum of the known probabilities from 1:
\[P(X=x_{\text{unknown}}) = 1 - \sum_{\text{known}} P(X=x_i)\]
Worked exampleA random variable \(X\) has distribution:
\[\begin{array}{c|cccc}x & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4\\\hline P(X=x) & 0.2 & 0.35 & k & 0.15\end{array}\]Find the value of \(k\).
\[0.2 + 0.35 + k + 0.15 = 1\]\[0.7 + k = 1\]\[\boxed{k = 0.3}\]

Variance and standard deviation of a discrete random variable

The variance of a discrete random variable \(X\) is:
\[\text{Var}(X) = E(X^2) - [E(X)]^2\]where \(E(X^2) = \sum_i x_i^2 P(X=x_i)\). The standard deviation is the positive square root:
\[\sigma = \sqrt{\text{Var}(X)}\]
Worked exampleUsing the pets distribution above, where \(E(X)=1.6\), find \(\text{Var}(X)\) and the standard deviation.
\[E(X^2) = 0^2(0.1) + 1^2(0.4) + 2^2(0.3) + 3^2(0.2)\]\[E(X^2) = 0 + 0.4 + 1.2 + 1.8 = 3.4\]\[\text{Var}(X) = 3.4 - 1.6^2 = 3.4 - 2.56 = 0.84\]\[\boxed{\text{Var}(X) = 0.84, \quad \sigma = \sqrt{0.84} \approx 0.917}\]

Worked example: expected value and variance for a game of chance

For a game with random monetary payout \(X\), the expected payout \(E(X)\) represents the average amount a player wins per game over many repetitions, and is used to judge whether a fixed entry fee gives the house (or the player) a long-run advantage.
Worked exampleA fair six-sided die is rolled once. A player wins €20 if a 6 is rolled, €8 if a 4 or 5 is rolled, and €0 otherwise. Let \(X\) be the payout. Find \(E(X)\) and \(\text{Var}(X)\).
The distribution is:
\[\begin{array}{c|ccc}x & 20 & 8 & 0\\\hline P(X=x) & \tfrac16 & \tfrac26 & \tfrac36\end{array}\]\[E(X) = 20\left(\tfrac16\right) + 8\left(\tfrac26\right) + 0\left(\tfrac36\right) = \tfrac{20}{6}+\tfrac{16}{6} = \tfrac{36}{6} = 6\]\[E(X^2) = 20^2\left(\tfrac16\right) + 8^2\left(\tfrac26\right) + 0^2\left(\tfrac36\right) = \tfrac{400}{6}+\tfrac{128}{6} = \tfrac{528}{6} = 88\]\[\text{Var}(X) = 88 - 6^2 = 88 - 36 = 52\]\[\boxed{E(X) = 6, \quad \text{Var}(X) = 52, \quad \sigma = \sqrt{52} \approx 7.21}\]
