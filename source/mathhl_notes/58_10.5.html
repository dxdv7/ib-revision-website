Cumulative frequency tables

The cumulative frequency of a class is the total number of data values less than its upper boundary: it is found by adding the frequency of that class to the sum of the frequencies of all the classes before it. A cumulative frequency table lists, for each class, the cumulative frequency up to its upper boundary.
Worked exampleConstruct a cumulative frequency table for the bus-stop waiting time data (frequencies \(4, 9, 14, 8, 5\) for the classes \(0\le t<5\), \(5\le t<10\), \(10\le t<15\), \(15\le t<20\), \(20\le t<25\)).
Upper boundary \(5\): cumulative frequency \(4\)
Upper boundary \(10\): cumulative frequency \(4+9=13\)
Upper boundary \(15\): cumulative frequency \(13+14=27\)
Upper boundary \(20\): cumulative frequency \(27+8=35\)
Upper boundary \(25\): cumulative frequency \(35+5=40\)
\[\boxed{\text{final cumulative frequency}=40, \text{ which equals the total number of passengers}}\]

Cumulative frequency graphs (ogives)

A cumulative frequency graph, or ogive, is drawn by plotting each upper class boundary against its cumulative frequency, and joining the points with a smooth curve (or straight line segments). Because cumulative frequency only ever increases, an ogive is always non-decreasing, rising from \(0\) at the lower boundary of the first class up to \(n\) at the upper boundary of the last class. The graph can be used to estimate, for any given value, how many data values lie below it, by interpolating along the segment between two plotted points.
Worked exampleUsing the ogive through the points \((5,4), (10,13), (15,27), (20,35), (25,40)\) for the bus-stop data, estimate the number of passengers who waited less than \(18\) minutes.
The value \(18\) lies between the plotted points \((15,27)\) and \((20,35)\). Interpolating along the segment between them:
\[\text{fraction of the way} = \frac{18-15}{20-15} = \frac{3}{5}\]\[\text{estimate} = 27 + \frac{3}{5}\times(35-27)\]\[\text{estimate} = 27+0.6\times8 = 27+4.8\]\[\boxed{\text{approximately } 32 \text{ passengers waited less than } 18 \text{ minutes}}\]

Linear interpolation for grouped data

To estimate the value corresponding to a given cumulative frequency \(k\) within a class, assume the data values are spread evenly across that class, and interpolate linearly:
\[\text{value} = L + \left(\frac{k-F}{f}\right)\times c\]where \(L\) is the lower boundary of the class containing the \(k\)th value, \(F\) is the cumulative frequency up to (but not including) that class, \(f\) is the frequency of that class, and \(c\) is its class width. This formula gives the same result as reading the ogive, but is more precise.
Worked exampleUsing the bus-stop cumulative frequency table, estimate the waiting time below which exactly \(25\) of the \(40\) passengers fall.
The cumulative frequency reaches \(13\) at \(t=10\) and \(27\) at \(t=15\), so the \(25\)th value lies in the class \(10\le t<15\), with \(L=10\), \(F=13\), \(f=14\), \(c=5\).
\[\text{value} = 10+\left(\frac{25-13}{14}\right)\times5\]\[\text{value} = 10+\left(\frac{12}{14}\right)\times5 \approx 10+4.29\]\[\boxed{\text{value}\approx14.3 \text{ minutes}}\]

Estimating the median from cumulative frequency

For grouped data with total frequency \(n\), the median is estimated using the interpolation formula with \(k=\frac{n}{2}\): it is the value corresponding to the point halfway up the cumulative frequency scale.
Worked exampleEstimate the median waiting time for the \(40\) passengers.
\[k=\frac{n}{2}=\frac{40}{2}=20\]The cumulative frequency reaches \(13\) at \(t=10\) and \(27\) at \(t=15\), so the median lies in the class \(10\le t<15\), with \(L=10\), \(F=13\), \(f=14\), \(c=5\).
\[\text{median} = 10+\left(\frac{20-13}{14}\right)\times5\]\[\text{median} = 10+\left(\frac{7}{14}\right)\times5 = 10+2.5\]\[\boxed{\text{median}=12.5 \text{ minutes}}\]

Estimating quartiles and percentiles from cumulative frequency

The lower quartile \(Q_1\) and upper quartile \(Q_3\) are estimated using the same interpolation formula, with \(k=\frac{n}{4}\) and \(k=\frac{3n}{4}\) respectively. More generally, the \(p\)th percentile is found using \(k=\frac{p}{100}\times n\).
Worked exampleFor the bus-stop waiting time data, estimate \(Q_1\), \(Q_3\), and the \(90\)th percentile.
(a) \(Q_1\): \(k=\frac{40}{4}=10\). This lies in the class \(5\le t<10\) (cumulative frequency \(4\) at \(t=5\), \(13\) at \(t=10\)), with \(L=5\), \(F=4\), \(f=9\), \(c=5\).
\[Q_1 = 5+\left(\frac{10-4}{9}\right)\times5\]\[\boxed{Q_1\approx8.33 \text{ minutes}}\]
(b) \(Q_3\): \(k=\frac{3\times40}{4}=30\). This lies in the class \(15\le t<20\) (cumulative frequency \(27\) at \(t=15\), \(35\) at \(t=20\)), with \(L=15\), \(F=27\), \(f=8\), \(c=5\).
\[Q_3 = 15+\left(\frac{30-27}{8}\right)\times5\]\[\boxed{Q_3=16.875 \text{ minutes}}\]
(c) \(90\)th percentile: \(k=\frac{90}{100}\times40=36\). This lies in the class \(20\le t<25\) (cumulative frequency \(35\) at \(t=20\), \(40\) at \(t=25\)), with \(L=20\), \(F=35\), \(f=5\), \(c=5\).
\[P_{90} = 20+\left(\frac{36-35}{5}\right)\times5\]\[\boxed{P_{90}=21 \text{ minutes}}\]

The interquartile range

The interquartile range measures the spread of the middle \(50\%\) of the data:
\[IQR = Q_3-Q_1\]Like the quartiles themselves, it can be estimated from a cumulative frequency table or graph, and unlike the range, it is not affected by extreme values in the top or bottom quarter of the data.
Worked exampleUsing the quartiles found above (\(Q_1\approx8.33\) minutes, \(Q_3=16.875\) minutes) for the bus-stop waiting time data, find the interquartile range.
\[IQR = Q_3-Q_1 = 16.875-8.33\]\[\boxed{IQR\approx8.54 \text{ minutes}}\]
