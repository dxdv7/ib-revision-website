Gradient of the tangent at a point

The gradient of the tangent to the curve \(y=f(x)\) at the point where \(x=a\) is given by the derivative evaluated at that point:
\[m_{\text{tangent}} = f'(a)\]
Worked exampleFind the gradient of the tangent to \(f(x)=x^3-2x+1\) at the point where \(x=2\).
\[f'(x)=3x^2-2\]
\[\boxed{f'(2)=3(4)-2=10}\]

Equation of the tangent line

The tangent to \(y=f(x)\) at the point \((a,f(a))\) has gradient \(f'(a)\), so its equation is:
\[y-f(a)=f'(a)(x-a)\]
Worked exampleFind the equation of the tangent to \(f(x)=x^3-2x+1\) at \(x=2\).
\[f(2)=8-4+1=5, \qquad f'(2)=10\]\[y-5=10(x-2)\]
\[\boxed{y=10x-15}\]

Gradient of the normal line

The normal to a curve at a point is the line perpendicular to the tangent there. Since perpendicular gradients multiply to \(-1\), provided \(f'(a)\neq0\):
\[m_{\text{normal}} = -\frac{1}{f'(a)}\]
Worked exampleFind the gradient of the normal to \(f(x)=x^3-2x+1\) at \(x=2\).
Since \(f'(2)=10\):
\[\boxed{m_{\text{normal}} = -\frac{1}{10}}\]

Equation of the normal line

The normal to \(y=f(x)\) at \((a,f(a))\) has gradient \(-\frac{1}{f'(a)}\), so its equation is:
\[y-f(a)=-\frac{1}{f'(a)}(x-a)\]
Worked exampleFind the equation of the normal to \(f(x)=x^3-2x+1\) at \(x=2\).
\[y-5=-\frac{1}{10}(x-2)\]\[y=-\frac{1}{10}x+\frac{1}{5}+5\]
\[\boxed{y=-\frac{1}{10}x+\frac{26}{5}}\]

Finding a point where the tangent has a given gradient

To find the point(s) on \(y=f(x)\) where the tangent is parallel to a line of gradient \(m\), solve \(f'(x)=m\) and substitute back into \(f(x)\) to find the corresponding \(y\)-coordinate.
Worked exampleFind the point on the curve \(f(x)=x^2-4x+5\) at which the tangent is parallel to the line \(y=2x-3\).
The line has gradient \(2\), so solve \(f'(x)=2\):
\[f'(x)=2x-4=2 \ \Rightarrow\ x=3\]\[f(3)=9-12+5=2\]Equation of this tangent: \(y-2=2(x-3)\)
\[\boxed{\text{Point } (3,2); \text{ tangent: } y=2x-4}\]

Special case: a horizontal tangent and a vertical normal

If \(f'(a)=0\), the tangent at \(x=a\) is the horizontal line \(y=f(a)\), and since no finite gradient is perpendicular to a horizontal line in the usual sense, the normal is the vertical line \(x=a\).
Worked exampleFind the equations of the tangent and normal to \(f(x)=x^3-3x+2\) at \(x=1\).
\[f'(x)=3x^2-3, \qquad f'(1)=3-3=0\]\[f(1)=1-3+2=0\]Since \(f'(1)=0\), the tangent is horizontal and the normal is vertical.
\[\boxed{\text{Tangent: } y=0; \qquad \text{Normal: } x=1}\]
