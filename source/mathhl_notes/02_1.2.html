Parallel lines

Two distinct non-vertical lines are parallel if and only if their gradients are equal:
\[m_1 = m_2\]If two lines have the same gradient but different \(y\)-intercepts, they never meet.
Worked exampleFind, in slope-intercept form, the equation of the line parallel to \(y = 3x - 4\) that passes through \((2, 7)\).
\[m = 3\]\[y - 7 = 3(x - 2)\]\[y - 7 = 3x - 6\]\[\boxed{y = 3x + 1}\]

Perpendicular lines

Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular if and only if the product of their gradients is \(-1\):
\[m_1 \times m_2 = -1\]Equivalently, the gradient of one line is the negative reciprocal of the other: \(m_2 = -\dfrac{1}{m_1}\).
Worked exampleFind, in slope-intercept form, the equation of the line perpendicular to \(y = 3x - 4\) that passes through \((6, 1)\).
\[m_2 = -\frac{1}{3}\]\[y - 1 = -\frac{1}{3}(x - 6)\]\[y - 1 = -\frac{1}{3}x + 2\]\[\boxed{y = -\frac{1}{3}x + 3}\]

Determining whether two lines are parallel, perpendicular, or neither

To compare two lines given in general form, rearrange each into slope-intercept form to find its gradient. If the gradients are equal, the lines are parallel. If the product of the gradients is \(-1\), the lines are perpendicular. Otherwise, the lines are neither.
Worked exampleDetermine whether the lines \(4x - 2y + 5 = 0\) and \(x + 2y - 7 = 0\) are parallel, perpendicular, or neither.
\[4x - 2y + 5 = 0 \Rightarrow y = 2x + \frac{5}{2}\]\[x + 2y - 7 = 0 \Rightarrow y = -\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{7}{2}\]\[m_1 \times m_2 = 2 \times \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = -1\]These lines are perpendicular.

Equation of a line parallel to a given line in general form

A line parallel to \(ax + by + c = 0\) has the same gradient, \(-\dfrac{a}{b}\). Find the gradient, use the point-slope form with the given point, then rearrange back into general form.
Worked exampleFind, in general form, the equation of the line parallel to \(3x - 4y + 8 = 0\) that passes through \((-4, 1)\).
\[3x - 4y + 8 = 0 \Rightarrow y = \frac{3}{4}x + 2\]\[y - 1 = \frac{3}{4}(x - (-4))\]\[y - 1 = \frac{3}{4}x + 3\]\[y = \frac{3}{4}x + 4\]\[\boxed{3x - 4y + 16 = 0}\]

Equation of a line perpendicular to a given line in general form

A line perpendicular to \(ax + by + c = 0\) has gradient equal to the negative reciprocal of \(-\dfrac{a}{b}\). Find this gradient, then use the point-slope form with the given point and rearrange into the required form.
Worked exampleFind, in general form, the equation of the line perpendicular to \(2x + 5y - 10 = 0\) that passes through \((3, -4)\).
\[2x + 5y - 10 = 0 \Rightarrow y = -\frac{2}{5}x + 2\]\[m_2 = \frac{5}{2}\]\[y - (-4) = \frac{5}{2}(x - 3)\]\[y + 4 = \frac{5}{2}x - \frac{15}{2}\]\[y = \frac{5}{2}x - \frac{23}{2}\]\[\boxed{5x - 2y - 23 = 0}\]

Parallel and perpendicular lines involving vertical and horizontal lines

Any two vertical lines, \(x = c_1\) and \(x = c_2\), are parallel to each other, and any two horizontal lines are parallel to each other. A vertical line and a horizontal line are always perpendicular to each other, even though the gradient of a vertical line is undefined and the condition \(m_1 \times m_2 = -1\) does not apply directly.
Worked exampleState the equation of the line through \((5, -2)\) that is parallel to \(x = 7\), and the equation of the line through \((5, -2)\) that is perpendicular to \(x = 7\).
Parallel line (also vertical):
\[\boxed{x = 5}\]Perpendicular line (horizontal):
\[\boxed{y = -2}\]
