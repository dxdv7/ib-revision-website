Stationary points and the first derivative test

A stationary point of a differentiable function \(f\) occurs where \(f'(x)=0\), so the tangent to the graph is horizontal there. Having already used the sign of \(f'(x)\) to decide where \(f\) is increasing or decreasing, the first derivative test classifies a stationary point \(x=c\) by comparing the sign of \(f'(x)\) just to the left and just to the right of \(c\):
\[f'(x) \text{ changes } + \to - \text{ across } c \ \Rightarrow\ \text{local maximum at } c\]\[f'(x) \text{ changes } - \to + \text{ across } c \ \Rightarrow\ \text{local minimum at } c\]\[f'(x) \text{ does not change sign across } c \ \Rightarrow\ \text{neither a maximum nor a minimum}\]
Worked exampleFind and classify the stationary points of \(f(x)=2x^3+3x^2-12x-4\).
\[f'(x)=6x^2+6x-12=6(x+2)(x-1)\]Setting \(f'(x)=0\) gives \(x=-2\) or \(x=1\). Testing the sign of \(f'(x)\) in each interval:
\[f'(-3)=6(-1)(-4)=24>0, \qquad f'(0)=6(2)(-1)=-12<0, \qquad f'(2)=6(4)(1)=24>0\]So \(f'(x)\) changes from \(+\) to \(-\) at \(x=-2\), and from \(-\) to \(+\) at \(x=1\). Since \(f(-2)=16\) and \(f(1)=-11\):
\[\boxed{(-2,16) \text{ is a local maximum}, \qquad (1,-11) \text{ is a local minimum}}\]

The second derivative test

If \(f'(c)=0\), the sign of \(f''(c)\) classifies the stationary point directly:
\[f''(c)>0 \ \Rightarrow\ \text{local minimum at } x=c\]\[f''(c)<0 \ \Rightarrow\ \text{local maximum at } x=c\]\[f''(c)=0 \ \Rightarrow\ \text{test is inconclusive; use the first derivative test instead}\]
Worked exampleUse the second derivative test to classify the stationary points of \(f(x)=x^3-6x^2+9x+1\).
\[f'(x)=3x^2-12x+9=3(x-1)(x-3)=0 \ \Rightarrow\ x=1 \text{ or } x=3\]\[f''(x)=6x-12\]\[f''(1)=6-12=-6<0, \qquad f''(3)=18-12=6>0\]Since \(f(1)=5\) and \(f(3)=1\):
\[\boxed{(1,5) \text{ is a local maximum}, \qquad (3,1) \text{ is a local minimum}}\]

Concavity and the sign of f''(x)

The sign of the second derivative describes the curvature of the graph:
\[f''(x)>0 \text{ on an interval} \ \Rightarrow\ \text{graph is concave up (curving upwards) there}\]\[f''(x)<0 \text{ on an interval} \ \Rightarrow\ \text{graph is concave down (curving downwards) there}\]
Worked exampleDetermine the concavity of \(f(x)=x^3-6x^2+9x+1\) (as above).
\[f''(x)=6x-12=0 \ \Rightarrow\ x=2\]\[f''(0)=-12<0, \qquad f''(3)=6>0\]
\[\boxed{f \text{ is concave down on } (-\infty,2) \text{ and concave up on } (2,\infty)}\]

Points of inflection

A point of inflection is a point where the concavity of the graph changes, from concave up to concave down or vice versa. This can only happen where \(f''(x)=0\), so inflection points are found among the solutions of \(f''(x)=0\) — but \(f''(x)=0\) alone does not guarantee an inflection point: the sign of \(f''(x)\) must actually change across that value.
Worked exampleConfirm that \(x=2\) is a point of inflection of \(f(x)=x^3-6x^2+9x+1\), and find its coordinates.
From above, \(f''(x)=6x-12\) changes sign from negative to positive at \(x=2\), so the concavity genuinely changes there. Since \(f(2)=8-24+18+1=3\):
\[\boxed{(2,3) \text{ is a point of inflection}}\]

When the second derivative test is inconclusive

If \(f'(c)=0\) and \(f''(c)=0\), the second derivative test gives no information: the stationary point could be a maximum, a minimum, or neither (a stationary point of inflection). The first derivative test must then be used instead.
Worked exampleClassify the stationary point at \(x=0\) for (a) \(f(x)=x^4\) and (b) \(g(x)=x^3\).
(a) \(f'(x)=4x^3=0\) at \(x=0\); \(f''(x)=12x^2\) so \(f''(0)=0\) (inconclusive). First derivative test: \(f'(x)<0\) for \(x<0\) and \(f'(x)>0\) for \(x>0\), so \(f'\) changes \(-\) to \(+\).
(b) \(g'(x)=3x^2=0\) at \(x=0\); \(g''(x)=6x\) so \(g''(0)=0\) (inconclusive). First derivative test: \(g'(x)=3x^2>0\) on both sides of \(x=0\), so \(g'\) does not change sign.
\[\boxed{x=0 \text{ is a local minimum for } f(x)=x^4, \text{ but a stationary point of inflection (neither) for } g(x)=x^3}\]

Finding and classifying all stationary points of a polynomial

To fully analyse a polynomial: solve \(f'(x)=0\) for all stationary points, then classify each using the second derivative test (falling back to the first derivative test wherever \(f''(x)=0\)).
Worked exampleFind and classify all stationary points of \(f(x)=x^4-8x^2+5\).
\[f'(x)=4x^3-16x=4x(x-2)(x+2)=0 \ \Rightarrow\ x=-2,0,2\]\[f''(x)=12x^2-16\]\[f''(-2)=48-16=32>0, \qquad f''(0)=-16<0, \qquad f''(2)=32>0\]Since \(f(-2)=16-32+5=-11\), \(f(0)=5\), and \(f(2)=16-32+5=-11\):
\[\boxed{(-2,-11) \text{ and } (2,-11) \text{ are local minima}, \qquad (0,5) \text{ is a local maximum}}\]
