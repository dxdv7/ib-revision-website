The quadratic formula

For a quadratic equation \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) with \(a \neq 0\), the solutions for \(x\) are given by the quadratic formula:
\[x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\]where \(a\), \(b\), \(c\) are the coefficients of \(x^2\), \(x\) and the constant term respectively, and \(\pm\) indicates the two solutions, one using \(+\) and one using \(-\).
Worked exampleSolve \(2x^2 + 5x - 3 = 0\) using the quadratic formula.
\[x = \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{5^2 - 4(2)(-3)}}{2(2)}\]\[x = \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{25 + 24}}{4}\]\[x = \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{49}}{4}\]\[x = \frac{-5 \pm 7}{4}\]\[\boxed{x = \frac{1}{2} \text{ or } x = -3}\]

The discriminant and the nature of the roots

The discriminant of \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) is the expression under the square root in the quadratic formula:
\[\Delta = b^2 - 4ac\]If \(\Delta > 0\) the equation has two distinct real roots; if \(\Delta = 0\) it has one repeated real root; if \(\Delta < 0\) it has no real roots. When \(a\), \(b\), \(c\) are integers and \(\Delta\) is a perfect square, the roots are rational.
Worked exampleUse the discriminant to determine the number of real roots of \(4x^2 - 12x + 9 = 0\), then solve the equation.
\[\Delta = (-12)^2 - 4(4)(9)\]\[\Delta = 144 - 144\]\[\Delta = 0\]Since \(\Delta = 0\), the equation has exactly one repeated real root, given by \(x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}\):
\[x = -\frac{-12}{2(4)}\]\[x = \frac{12}{8}\]\[\boxed{x = \frac{3}{2}}\]

Solving quadratic inequalities by sign analysis

To solve a quadratic inequality, first move all terms to one side so that zero is on the other side, then factorise to find the critical values (the roots of the corresponding equation). These values divide the number line into intervals. Use a sign chart, together with the shape of the parabola (upward if \(a > 0\), downward if \(a < 0\)), to determine which intervals satisfy the inequality: an upward parabola is positive outside its roots and negative between them, while a downward parabola is the opposite.
Worked exampleSolve the inequality \(x^2 > x\).
\[x^2 - x > 0\]\[x(x-1) > 0\]The critical values are \(x = 0\) and \(x = 1\). Since \(a = 1 > 0\), the graph of \(y = x(x-1)\) is an upward parabola, positive outside its roots.
\[\boxed{x < 0 \text{ or } x > 1}\]

Using the discriminant with an unknown parameter

When a quadratic equation contains an unknown parameter, the condition for two distinct real roots (\(\Delta > 0\)), one repeated real root (\(\Delta = 0\)), or no real roots (\(\Delta < 0\)) becomes an inequality or equation in that parameter, which is then solved using the quadratic formula and sign analysis, just as for any quadratic equation or inequality.
Worked exampleFor \(4x^2 + 4kx + 9 = 0\), find the value(s) of \(k\) for which the equation has (a) one real zero, (b) two distinct real zeros, (c) no real zeros.
\[\Delta = (4k)^2 - 4(4)(9) = 16k^2 - 144\](a) One real zero requires \(\Delta = 0\):
\[16k^2 - 144 = 0 \implies k^2 = 9 \implies \boxed{k = \pm 3}\](b) Two distinct real zeros requires \(\Delta > 0\):
\[16k^2 - 144 > 0 \implies k^2 > 9 \implies \boxed{k < -3 \text{ or } k > 3}\](c) No real zeros requires \(\Delta < 0\):
\[16k^2 - 144 < 0 \implies k^2 < 9 \implies \boxed{-3 < k < 3}\]

Discriminant conditions for an inequality to hold for all real x

A quadratic expression \(ax^2 + bx + c\) is positive for all real \(x\) exactly when its graph never touches or crosses the \(x\)-axis while opening upward, that is, when \(a > 0\) and \(\Delta < 0\). Similarly, it is negative for all real \(x\) when \(a < 0\) and \(\Delta < 0\).
Worked exampleFind the range of values of \(k\) such that \(3x^2 - 12x + k > 0\) for all real values of \(x\).
Since \(a = 3 > 0\), this holds exactly when \(\Delta < 0\):
\[(-12)^2 - 4(3)(k) < 0\]\[144 - 12k < 0\]\[144 < 12k\]\[\boxed{k > 12}\]
