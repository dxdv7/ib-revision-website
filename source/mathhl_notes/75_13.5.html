What implicit differentiation is

Some equations relating \(x\) and \(y\), such as \(x^2+y^2=25\), define \(y\) implicitly as a function (or functions) of \(x\) without being solved explicitly for \(y\). To find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\), differentiate both sides of the equation with respect to \(x\), treating \(y\) as a function of \(x\) and applying the chain rule to any term involving \(y\). In particular:
\[\frac{d}{dx}(y^n)=ny^{n-1}\frac{dy}{dx}\]
Worked exampleDifferentiate \(x^2+y^2=25\) implicitly with respect to \(x\).
\[\frac{d}{dx}(x^2)+\frac{d}{dx}(y^2)=\frac{d}{dx}(25)\]\[2x+2y\frac{dy}{dx}=0\]\[\boxed{2x+2y\frac{dy}{dx}=0}\]

Solving for dy/dx

After differentiating both sides, collect the terms containing \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) on one side, factor it out, and divide to isolate \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) in terms of \(x\) and \(y\).
Worked exampleContinuing from \(2x+2y\dfrac{dy}{dx}=0\), solve for \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\).
\[2y\frac{dy}{dx}=-2x\]\[\boxed{\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{x}{y}}\]

Implicit differentiation with mixed terms

When an equation contains a term with both \(x\) and \(y\) multiplied together, such as \(xy\), apply the product rule, remembering that \(\dfrac{d}{dx}(y)=\dfrac{dy}{dx}\):
\[\frac{d}{dx}(xy)=x\frac{dy}{dx}+y\]
Worked exampleFind \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) given \(x^2y+y^3=10\).
Differentiating term by term, using the product rule on \(x^2y\):
\[\left(2xy+x^2\frac{dy}{dx}\right)+3y^2\frac{dy}{dx}=0\]\[x^2\frac{dy}{dx}+3y^2\frac{dy}{dx}=-2xy\]\[\frac{dy}{dx}(x^2+3y^2)=-2xy\]\[\boxed{\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{-2xy}{x^2+3y^2}}\]

Gradient of the tangent to an implicit curve at a point

To find the gradient of the tangent to an implicitly defined curve at a specific point \((x_0,y_0)\) on the curve, find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) by implicit differentiation, then substitute both coordinates \(x_0\) and \(y_0\) into the resulting expression.
Worked exampleFind the gradient of the tangent to \(x^2+y^2=25\) at the point \((3,4)\).
From above, \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac{x}{y}\). Substituting \(x=3\), \(y=4\):
\[\boxed{\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{3}{4}}\]

Related rates: the chain rule linking rates of change

When two or more quantities are related by an equation, and each changes with time \(t\), the rates of change are linked by differentiating the equation with respect to \(t\), using the chain rule. For example, if a quantity \(V\) depends on \(r\), which in turn depends on \(t\):
\[\frac{dV}{dt}=\frac{dV}{dr}\cdot\frac{dr}{dt}\]A typical approach: (1) write an equation relating the quantities, (2) differentiate both sides with respect to \(t\), (3) substitute the known values and known rate, (4) solve for the required rate.
Worked exampleA spherical balloon is inflated so that its volume increases at a constant rate of \(\dfrac{dV}{dt}=100\text{ cm}^3\text{s}^{-1}\). Find the rate at which the radius is increasing at the instant when \(r=5\text{ cm}\). (Volume of a sphere: \(V=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3\).)
Differentiating \(V=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3\) with respect to \(t\):
\[\frac{dV}{dt}=4\pi r^2\frac{dr}{dt}\]Substituting \(\dfrac{dV}{dt}=100\) and \(r=5\):
\[100=4\pi(5)^2\frac{dr}{dt}=100\pi\frac{dr}{dt}\]\[\boxed{\frac{dr}{dt}=\frac{1}{\pi}\approx0.318\text{ cm s}^{-1}}\]

A geometric related rates problem: the sliding ladder

A classic related rates setup: a ladder of fixed length \(L\) leans against a vertical wall, with its foot a horizontal distance \(x\) from the wall and its top a height \(y\) up the wall, so that \(x^2+y^2=L^2\) (Pythagoras) at every instant. Differentiating with respect to \(t\):
\[2x\frac{dx}{dt}+2y\frac{dy}{dt}=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x\frac{dx}{dt}+y\frac{dy}{dt}=0\]
Worked exampleA \(5\text{ m}\) ladder leans against a vertical wall. The foot of the ladder is pulled away from the wall at a constant rate of \(\dfrac{dx}{dt}=0.6\text{ m s}^{-1}\). Find the rate at which the top of the ladder is sliding down the wall at the instant when the foot is \(3\text{ m}\) from the wall.
Since \(x^2+y^2=25\), when \(x=3\): \(y^2=25-9=16\), so \(y=4\).
From \(x\dfrac{dx}{dt}+y\dfrac{dy}{dt}=0\):
\[3(0.6)+4\frac{dy}{dt}=0\]\[4\frac{dy}{dt}=-1.8\]\[\boxed{\frac{dy}{dt}=-0.45\text{ m s}^{-1}\ \text{(the top is sliding down at }0.45\text{ m s}^{-1}\text{)}}\]
