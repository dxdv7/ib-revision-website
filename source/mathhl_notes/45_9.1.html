Vectors as displacements in the plane and in space

A vector \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) represents a displacement from an initial point \(A\) to a terminal point \(B\): it has a magnitude, equal to the distance from \(A\) to \(B\), and a direction, from \(A\) towards \(B\). Two displacement vectors are equal (they represent the same free vector) if they have the same magnitude and the same direction, regardless of their initial point.
Worked examplePoints \(A(2,-1)\) and \(B(5,3)\) lie in the plane. Describe the displacement \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) in terms of horizontal and vertical movement.
Moving from \(A\) to \(B\), the \(x\)-coordinate changes by \(5-2=3\) and the \(y\)-coordinate changes by \(3-(-1)=4\).
\[\boxed{\overrightarrow{AB} \text{ is a displacement of } 3 \text{ units in the positive } x\text{-direction and } 4 \text{ units in the positive } y\text{-direction}}\]

Column vector notation

A vector \(\mathbf{v}\) is written as a column of its components. In 2D:
\[\mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix}v_1\\v_2\end{pmatrix}\]and in 3D:
\[\mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix}v_1\\v_2\\v_3\end{pmatrix}\]For a displacement from \(A(x_1,y_1,z_1)\) to \(B(x_2,y_2,z_2)\):
\[\overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix}x_2-x_1\\y_2-y_1\\z_2-z_1\end{pmatrix}\]
Worked exampleFind the column vector representing the displacement from \(A(1,2,-3)\) to \(B(4,-1,5)\) in 3D space.
\[\overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix}4-1\\-1-2\\5-(-3)\end{pmatrix}\]\[\boxed{\overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix}3\\-3\\8\end{pmatrix}}\]

Base vectors i, j, and k

The base vectors are the unit vectors along the coordinate axes:
\[\mathbf{i} = \begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{j} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{k} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}\]Every vector in 3-space can be written uniquely in terms of them:
\[\mathbf{v} = \begin{pmatrix}v_1\\v_2\\v_3\end{pmatrix} = v_1\mathbf{i} + v_2\mathbf{j} + v_3\mathbf{k}\]
Worked exampleWrite the vector \(\overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix}3\\-3\\8\end{pmatrix}\) found above in terms of the base vectors \(\mathbf{i}\), \(\mathbf{j}\), and \(\mathbf{k}\).
Here \(v_1 = 3\), \(v_2 = -3\), and \(v_3 = 8\), so:
\[\boxed{\overrightarrow{AB} = 3\mathbf{i} - 3\mathbf{j} + 8\mathbf{k}}\]

Position vectors

If the initial point of a vector is the origin \(O\), the vector is said to be in standard position, and it is called the position vector of its terminal point. For a point \(A\), the position vector of \(A\) is:
\[\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}\]Its components are simply the coordinates of \(A\). For two points \(A\) and \(B\) with position vectors \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\):
\[\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OB} - \overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}\]
Worked examplePoints \(A(1,2,-3)\) and \(B(4,-1,5)\) have position vectors \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\). Use position vectors to find \(\overrightarrow{AB}\), and check this agrees with the column vector found earlier.
\[\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix}1\\2\\-3\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix}4\\-1\\5\end{pmatrix}\]\[\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix}4-1\\-1-2\\5-(-3)\end{pmatrix}\]\[\boxed{\overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix}3\\-3\\8\end{pmatrix}, \text{ which agrees with the earlier result}}\]
