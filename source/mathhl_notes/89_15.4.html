The normal distribution

A continuous random variable \(X\) follows a normal distribution with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\), written:
\[X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)\]Its probability density function is:
\[f(x) = \dfrac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}}, \qquad x \in \mathbb{R}\]The graph of \(f\) is a symmetric, bell-shaped curve centred on \(x=\mu\), with the total area underneath equal to \(1\). The distribution is completely determined by the two parameters \(\mu\) and \(\sigma\).
Worked exampleThe volume of liquid, \(X\) ml, dispensed by a filling machine into bottles is modelled by \(X \sim N(500, 16)\). State the mean and standard deviation of \(X\).
Here \(\mu = 500\) directly, and since the variance is \(\sigma^2 = 16\):
\[\sigma = \sqrt{16} = 4\]\[\boxed{\mu = 500 \text{ ml}, \qquad \sigma = 4 \text{ ml}}\]

The standard normal distribution

The standard normal distribution is the special case with mean \(0\) and variance \(1\):
\[Z \sim N(0,1)\]Its curve has exactly the same bell shape and properties as any normal curve, but is symmetric about \(z=0\). By symmetry:
\[P(Z<-z) = P(Z>z), \qquad P(Z<0)=P(Z>0)=0.5\]Every normal distribution can be related back to this one standard curve through standardization, so a GDC (or a standard normal table) only ever needs to store probabilities for \(Z \sim N(0,1)\).
Worked exampleGiven that \(P(Z<1.5) = 0.933\) (from a GDC), find \(P(Z>1.5)\) and \(P(Z<-1.5)\).
Since the total area is 1:
\[P(Z>1.5) = 1 - 0.933 = 0.067\]By symmetry of the standard normal curve about 0:
\[P(Z<-1.5) = P(Z>1.5)\]\[\boxed{P(Z>1.5) = 0.067, \qquad P(Z<-1.5) = 0.067}\]

Standardization

Any normal variable \(X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)\) can be converted into a standard normal variable \(Z \sim N(0,1)\) using the standardization formula:
\[Z = \dfrac{X-\mu}{\sigma}\]The resulting value \(z\) (also called the \(z\)-score) measures how many standard deviations a value of \(X\) lies above or below the mean \(\mu\).
Worked exampleFor \(X \sim N(500, 16)\) (so \(\mu=500\), \(\sigma=4\)), find the \(z\)-score corresponding to \(x = 508\).
\[z = \dfrac{508-500}{4} = \dfrac{8}{4}\]\[\boxed{z = 2}\]
