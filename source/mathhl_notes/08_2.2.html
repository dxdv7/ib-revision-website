General form of a quadratic function

A quadratic function can be written in general form as
\[y = ax^2 + bx + c\]where \(a\), \(b\), \(c\) are real constants and \(a \neq 0\). The \(y\)-intercept is found by setting \(x = 0\), giving the point \((0, c)\). If \(a > 0\) the parabola opens upward (concave up) and has a minimum point; if \(a < 0\) it opens downward (concave down) and has a maximum point.
Worked exampleFor \(y = -3x^2 + 12x - 7\), state the \(y\)-intercept and whether the vertex is a minimum or a maximum.
\[y\text{-intercept: } (0, -7)\]Since \(a = -3 < 0\):
\[\boxed{y\text{-intercept } (0,-7); \text{ the graph has a maximum point}}\]

The vertex formula

For a quadratic in general form \(y = ax^2 + bx + c\), the axis of symmetry is the vertical line
\[x = -\frac{b}{2a}\]which passes through the vertex. The \(y\)-coordinate of the vertex is found by substituting this \(x\)-value back into the function.
Worked exampleFind the vertex of \(y = -3x^2 + 12x - 7\).
\[x = -\frac{12}{2(-3)} = -\frac{12}{-6} = 2\]\[y = -3(2)^2 + 12(2) - 7 = -12 + 24 - 7\]\[\boxed{\text{Vertex} = (2, 5)}\]

Vertex form

A quadratic function can also be written in vertex form
\[y = a(x - h)^2 + k\]where the vertex is at \((h, k)\) and the axis of symmetry is \(x = h\). The constant \(a\) is the same leading coefficient as in general form.
Worked exampleState the vertex and axis of symmetry of \(y = -3(x - 2)^2 + 5\).
\[\boxed{\text{Vertex} = (2, 5); \text{ axis of symmetry: } x = 2}\]

Completing the square

A quadratic in general form can be converted to vertex form by completing the square. Factor \(a\) out of the first two terms,
\[y = ax^2 + bx + c = a\left(x^2 + \frac{b}{a}x\right) + c\]then add and subtract the square of half the coefficient of \(x\) inside the brackets:
\[y = a\left[\left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{b}{2a}\right)^2\right] + c\]
Worked exampleConvert \(g(x) = -2x^2 - 12x + 7\) to vertex form and state the vertex.
\[g(x) = -2(x^2 + 6x) + 7\]\[g(x) = -2[(x+3)^2 - 9] + 7\]\[g(x) = -2(x+3)^2 + 18 + 7\]\[\boxed{g(x) = -2(x+3)^2 + 25, \text{ vertex } (-3, 25)}\]

Factorised form and x-intercepts

If a quadratic function is written in factorised form \(f(x) = a(x - p)(x - q)\), its graph has \(x\)-intercepts at \((p, 0)\) and \((q, 0)\), since \(f(p) = f(q) = 0\). Because a parabola is symmetric, the axis of symmetry passes through the midpoint of the intercepts,
\[x = \frac{p+q}{2}\]
Worked exampleA quadratic function has \(x\)-intercepts \(-3\) and \(1\), and \(y\)-intercept \(6\). Find the function in the form \(f(x) = a(x-p)(x-q)\) and in general form.
\[f(x) = a(x+3)(x-1)\]\[6 = a(0+3)(0-1)\]\[6 = -3a \implies a = -2\]\[\boxed{f(x) = -2(x+3)(x-1) = -2x^2 - 4x + 6}\]
