The vector (cross) product

For \(\mathbf{u} = (u_1,u_2,u_3)\) and \(\mathbf{v} = (v_1,v_2,v_3)\), the vector (cross) product \(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}\) is defined by the determinant:
\[\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v} = \begin{vmatrix}\mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k}\\u_1 & u_2 & u_3\\v_1 & v_2 & v_3\end{vmatrix}\]which expands to the component formula:
\[\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v} = (u_2v_3-u_3v_2)\mathbf{i} + (u_3v_1-u_1v_3)\mathbf{j} + (u_1v_2-u_2v_1)\mathbf{k}\]Unlike the scalar product, the result is a vector, not a number.
Worked exampleFind \(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}\) for \(\mathbf{u} = (3,-1,2)\) and \(\mathbf{v} = (1,2,-1)\).
\[u_2v_3-u_3v_2 = (-1)(-1)-(2)(2) = 1-4 = -3\]\[u_3v_1-u_1v_3 = (2)(1)-(3)(-1) = 2+3 = 5\]\[u_1v_2-u_2v_1 = (3)(2)-(-1)(1) = 6+1 = 7\]\[\boxed{\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v} = (-3,5,7)}\]

Geometric interpretation and magnitude

The vector \(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}\) is perpendicular to both \(\mathbf{u}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\), with its direction given by the right-hand rule. Its magnitude is:
\[|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}| = |\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|\sin\theta\]where \(\theta\) is the angle between \(\mathbf{u}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\) (with \(0^\circ \le \theta \le 180^\circ\)).
Worked exampleVectors \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\) have \(|\mathbf{a}| = 4\), \(|\mathbf{b}| = 5\), and the angle between them is \(150^\circ\). Find \(|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}|\).
\[|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}| = |\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\sin\theta = 4 \times 5 \times \sin150^\circ\]\[\sin150^\circ = \frac{1}{2}\]\[\boxed{|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}| = 4 \times 5 \times \frac{1}{2} = 10}\]

Properties of the vector product

Key properties of the vector product:
\[\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v} = -(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{u})\]\[\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{0} \text{ for every vector } \mathbf{u}\]\[\text{for nonzero } \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}: \quad \mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0} \iff \mathbf{u} \text{ and } \mathbf{v} \text{ are parallel}\]\[\mathbf{u}\times(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{w}) = \mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{w}\]
Worked exampleShow that \(\mathbf{u} = (2,-1,3)\) and \(\mathbf{v} = (-4,2,-6)\) are parallel by computing \(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}\).
\[u_2v_3-u_3v_2 = (-1)(-6)-(3)(2) = 6-6 = 0\]\[u_3v_1-u_1v_3 = (3)(-4)-(2)(-6) = -12+12 = 0\]\[u_1v_2-u_2v_1 = (2)(2)-(-1)(-4) = 4-4 = 0\]\[\boxed{\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v} = (0,0,0), \text{ confirming that } \mathbf{u} \text{ and } \mathbf{v} \text{ are parallel}}\]

Finding a vector perpendicular to two vectors

Since \(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}\) is perpendicular to both \(\mathbf{u}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\), a unit vector perpendicular to both is found by normalising it:
\[\mathbf{n} = \frac{\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}|}\]There are always two such unit vectors, \(\mathbf{n}\) and \(-\mathbf{n}\), pointing in opposite directions.
Worked exampleFind a unit vector perpendicular to \(\mathbf{u} = (3,-1,2)\) and \(\mathbf{v} = (1,2,-1)\).
From the earlier example, \(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v} = (-3,5,7)\).
\[|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{(-3)^2+5^2+7^2} = \sqrt{9+25+49} = \sqrt{83}\]\[\boxed{\mathbf{n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{83}}(-3,5,7)}\]

Area of a parallelogram

If a parallelogram has adjacent sides represented by vectors \(\mathbf{u}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\), its area is:
\[\text{Area} = |\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}|\]This follows directly from \(|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}| = |\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|\sin\theta\), which is exactly base times height.
Worked exampleFind the area of the parallelogram with adjacent sides \(\mathbf{p} = (2,0,1)\) and \(\mathbf{q} = (1,3,-2)\).
\[p_2q_3-p_3q_2 = (0)(-2)-(1)(3) = -3\]\[p_3q_1-p_1q_3 = (1)(1)-(2)(-2) = 1+4 = 5\]\[p_1q_2-p_2q_1 = (2)(3)-(0)(1) = 6\]\[\mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{q} = (-3,5,6)\]\[\boxed{\text{Area} = \sqrt{(-3)^2+5^2+6^2} = \sqrt{9+25+36} = \sqrt{70}}\]

Area of a triangle

If a triangle has vertices \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\), its area is half the area of the parallelogram with sides \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) and \(\overrightarrow{AC}\):
\[\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}|\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}|\]
Worked exampleFind the area of the triangle with vertices \(A(1,0,2)\), \(B(3,-1,4)\), and \(C(0,2,1)\).
\[\overrightarrow{AB} = (2,-1,2), \qquad \overrightarrow{AC} = (-1,2,-1)\]\[(-1)(-1)-(2)(2) = 1-4 = -3\]\[(2)(-1)-(2)(-1) = -2+2 = 0\]\[(2)(2)-(-1)(-1) = 4-1 = 3\]\[\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC} = (-3,0,3)\]\[|\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}| = \sqrt{(-3)^2+0^2+3^2} = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}\]\[\boxed{\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(3\sqrt{2}) = \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}}\]
