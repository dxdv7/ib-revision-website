The gradient formula

The gradient (or slope) \(m\) of a line through two points \(A(x_1, y_1)\) and \(B(x_2, y_2)\) is given by
\[m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\]where \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) are any two distinct points on the line. Since division by zero is undefined, the gradient of a vertical line is undefined.
Worked exampleFind the gradient of the line through \(A(2, -3)\) and \(B(6, 9)\).
\[m = \frac{9 - (-3)}{6 - 2}\]\[m = \frac{12}{4}\]\[\boxed{m = 3}\]

The slope-intercept form of a line

A linear equation written as \(y = mx + c\) is in slope-intercept form, where \(m\) is the gradient and \((0, c)\) is the \(y\)-intercept, the point at which the line crosses the \(y\)-axis.
Worked exampleFind, in slope-intercept form, the equation of the line with gradient \(12\) that passes through the point \((3, 31)\).
\[y = mx + c\]\[31 = 12(3) + c\]\[31 = 36 + c\]\[c = -5\]\[\boxed{y = 12x - 5}\]

The point-slope form of a line

A linear equation can be written in point-slope form as
\[y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\]where \(m\) is the gradient and \((x_1, y_1)\) is any known point on the line.
Worked exampleFind, in slope-intercept form, the equation of the line through \((4, -6)\) with gradient \(-2\).
\[y - (-6) = -2(x - 4)\]\[y + 6 = -2x + 8\]\[\boxed{y = -2x + 2}\]

Finding the equation of a line through two points

To find the equation of a line through two known points \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\): first find the gradient \(m\) using the gradient formula, then substitute \(m\) and either point into the point-slope form to obtain the equation.
Worked exampleA linear relationship between temperature in degrees Celsius, \(C\), and degrees Fahrenheit, \(F\), passes through the points \((C, F) = (10, 50)\) and \((100, 212)\). Find \(F\) in terms of \(C\).
\[m = \frac{212 - 50}{100 - 10}\]\[m = \frac{162}{90} = \frac{9}{5}\]\[F - 50 = \frac{9}{5}(C - 10)\]\[F = \frac{9}{5}C - 18 + 50\]\[\boxed{F = \frac{9}{5}C + 32}\]

The general form of a line

Every line can be written in general form:
\[ax + by + c = 0\]where \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) are constants and \(a\) and \(b\) are not both zero. To find the gradient and \(y\)-intercept of a line given in general form, rearrange it into slope-intercept form by solving for \(y\).
Worked exampleWrite the line \(5x + 3y - 6 = 0\) in slope-intercept form, and state its gradient and \(y\)-intercept.
\[3y = -5x + 6\]\[\boxed{y = -\frac{5}{3}x + 2}\]The gradient is \(m = -\dfrac{5}{3}\) and the \(y\)-intercept is \((0, 2)\).

Horizontal and vertical lines

A horizontal line has equation \(y = c\), with gradient \(m = 0\). A vertical line has equation \(x = c\), with undefined gradient, since the horizontal change between any two points on it is zero.
Worked exampleWrite the equations of the horizontal and vertical lines described by \(y - 4 = 0\) and \(x + 3 = 0\).
Horizontal line:
\[y - 4 = 0\]\[\boxed{y = 4}\]Vertical line:
\[x + 3 = 0\]\[\boxed{x = -3}\]
