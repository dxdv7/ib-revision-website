Logic basics 
The most vital notion in mathematics is that of proof. In mathematics, it is the 
process of establishing the validity of a statement, especially by derivation from 
other statements in accordance with principles of reasoning. 
Mathematicians use deductive reasoning to develop and extend a theory by 
drawing conclusions based on statements accepted as true. Proofs are essential 
in mathematical reasoning because they demonstrate that the conclusions are 
true. Generally speaking, a mathematical explanation for a conclusion has no 
value if the explanation cannot be supported by an acceptable proof. It is not 
enough to test the statement with a few examples.

the statement “Tim’s PC has more than 100 GB free hard disk space; and N is

Solution

faster than 2 GHz” This conjunction can be expressed more simply as ‘Tim’s 
PC has more than 100 GB free hard disk space, and its processor runs faster

The disjunction of M and N, MV N, is the statement “Tim’s PC has at least

This statement is true when Tim’s PC has at least 100 GB free hard disk 
space, when the PC’s processor runs faster than 2 GHz, and when both 
conditions are true. It is only false when both of these conditions are false, 
that is, when Tim’s PC has less than 100 GB free hard disk space and the 
processor in his PC runs at 2 GHz or slower.

Valid arguments 
Later in this chapter we will study proofs. Proofs in mathematics are valid 
arguments that establish the truth of mathematical statements. By an 
argument, we mean a sequence of statements that end with a conclusion. 
By valid, we mean that the conclusion, or final statement of the argument, 
must follow from the truth of the preceding statements, or premises, of the 
argument. That is, an argument is valid if and only if it is impossible for all the 
premises to be true and the conclusion to be false. To deduce new statements 
from statements we already have, we use rules of inference, which are templates 
for constructing valid arguments. Rules of inference are our basic tools for 
establishing the truth of statements.

Direct proofs lead from the premises of a theorem to the conclusion. They
