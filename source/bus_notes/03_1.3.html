1.3 — Business objectives

Key terms

  • Business objectives — a range of measurable results that a company hopes to achieve and/or maintain to ensure its growth and success, providing guidelines for decisions related to operations.
  • Vision statement — a statement describing what a company wants to become in the future, aligned with its core values, that directs strategic plans, motivates employees, and helps the business stand out from competitors.
  • Mission statement — a short statement communicating an organization's aims to customers, employees, shareholders, and other stakeholders, defining its purpose, functions, and long-term objectives, aligned with the vision statement.
  • Specific and measurable objectives — business objectives should be specific and measurable, relevant, and attainable, and follow a monitored schedule, allowing for easy accountability and periodic checks on progress.
  • Long-term and short-term goals — long-term goals often consist of several short-term goals and require considerable time and planning, while short-term goals tend to need less time and fewer resources.
  • Growth (objective) — a top business objective that may refer to profits, revenues, enhanced working conditions, or increased capacity, including increasing market share in the short term or size in the long term.
  • Profit (objective) — earning or enhancing profitability, often the main reason a business is set up, benefiting shareholders, creditors, and owners, subject to legal restrictions on how it is achieved.
  • Stability (objective) — an objective achieved through gaining customer loyalty and confidence, sustainable practices around employee satisfaction, and being well-equipped to survive market fluctuations.
  • Efficiency (objective) — the ability of a business to produce goods at the lowest cost possible so they can be sold at a low price while still making a profit, through innovation, low energy consumption, and high productivity.
  • Survival (objective) — the objective of remaining ahead of competition and enabling a business to eventually focus on other objectives, achieved through recognition, a good public image, and a loyal customer base.
  • Protecting shareholder value — ensuring the interests of investors, creditors, and shareholders are protected through periodic and assured returns on investment, increasing shareholder value through targeted goals and growth.
  • Ethical objectives — objectives applying moral principles when interacting with stakeholders, including complying with laws and regulations and avoiding conflict, which can build positive consumer relationships and attract investors.
  • Strategic objectives — long-term, big-picture objectives, made by executives and aligned with the vision statement, that define the purpose of a business and impact its survival and success; they can take decades to achieve.
  • Tactical objectives — short-term, flexible objectives that break down a business's long-term strategic goals into clear, action-oriented deliverables achieved in middle management; if unmet, they are unlikely to seriously harm the company.
  • Corporate social responsibility (CSR) — the principle that business organizations are part of society and have a responsibility to reduce their negative impacts on society and maximize their positive impacts, going beyond a simple set of goodwill policies.
