5.1 — Introduction to operations management

Key terms

  • Operations management — the functional area covering the design and control of production processes and business operations, aiming to use as few resources as possible while meeting customer requirements.
  • Demand and supply analysis — understanding product demand, supply requirements, and business forecasting, critical in manufacturing operations.
  • Inventory management — managing raw materials, the quality of goods and services, and finished products.
  • Supply chain management — managing everything from sourcing raw materials, through product storage, to distribution.
  • Capacity management — evaluating production capacity needed based on demand, productivity, and input inventory.
  • Process and layout strategy — decisions about factory and workplace layouts, operational processes, and production methods that are critical for business output.
  • Geography strategy — evaluating locations for factories, warehouses, distribution centers, and retail outlets.

Key theories & models

  • Triple bottom line (TBL) — a business concept suggesting firms should measure their social and environmental impact alongside financial performance rather than focusing solely on profit, including the sustainability of supply chains and the impact of operations on local communities.
