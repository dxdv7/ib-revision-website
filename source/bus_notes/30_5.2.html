5.2 — Operations methods

Key terms

  • Job production — a method that produces high-quality, usually one-off, customized products tailored to a specific customer's needs; it is highly specialized and labor intensive, ranging from small-scale projects like a haute couture dress to large, complex ones like luxury sports cars or one-off construction developments.
  • Batch production — a method in which identical or similar items are produced together in distinct groups (batches) of varying sizes, with changes (such as color, size, or style) made between batches.
  • Mass production (flow / continuous production) — the production of large amounts of standardized products in a constant flow along an assembly line, typically highly automated with reduced need for skilled labor.
  • Mass customization — the use of flexible computer-aided manufacturing systems to produce output that customers can personalize from a list of predefined features, combining the personalization of custom-made products with the low unit costs of mass production.
  • Lights-out factory (dark factory) — a factory where human activity is so minimal it can operate in the dark with zero human intervention on site, made possible by automated machines, robots, and manufacturing operations management (MOM) software.

Key theories & models

  • Factors influencing choice of production method — a framework used to justify the most suitable method of production for a given situation, including the required quantity/output level, available technology, production timing, input combinations (labor and capital), target market and price sensitivity, the nature and size of the firm, government policies (grants, subsidies, tax breaks), and the nature of the product itself.
