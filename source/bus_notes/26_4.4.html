4.4 — Market research

Key terms

  • Market research — any systematic activity an organization carries out to obtain information about a market, including customers, competitors, and the market itself, to support decision-making.
  • Primary research (field research) — gathering first-hand information directly for a specific purpose, often carried out by a firm's own team or an external market research agency.
  • Secondary research (desk research) — collecting and analyzing information that already exists, having been gathered previously for another purpose.
  • Quantitative research — research that gathers and analyzes results in the form of numbers, such as rankings or percentages.
  • Qualitative research — research that finds out people's attitudes or opinions about something, where results cannot be expressed in numbers.
  • Survey — a set of questions given to many people, by mail, telephone, or online, to gather their thoughts, attitudes, preferences, and opinions.
  • Interview — a primary research method in which an interviewer asks an interviewee a series of questions face-to-face, by phone, or virtually, gathering detailed but time-consuming information.
  • Focus group — bringing a group of customers together to discuss feelings, perceptions, and feedback about a product, price, or advertisement.
  • Observation — gathering information by watching the behavior of potential or existing customers, such as tracking store traffic or website clicks.
  • Market analysis (reports) — secondary research consisting of studies and market intelligence published by specialist organizations, such as Mintel.
  • Academic journals — periodical collections of expert-written articles used as a source of secondary research.
  • Government publications — reports and data produced by governments on business-related issues, such as demographic or economic data.
  • Media articles — newspaper and magazine articles used as a source of secondary market research.
  • Primary sources — first-hand accounts of an event, such as a company's own annual reports, press releases, or social media posts.
  • Secondary sources — accounts created by someone not present at the event, often involving synthesis or analysis, such as a newspaper article about a company.
  • Sampling — contacting only a small, representative group (a sample) of a target population rather than every member, since gathering data from everyone is often unrealistic.
  • Random sampling — a sampling method in which every member of the population has an equal chance of being selected, producing fair but hard-to-achieve results.
  • Quota sampling — dividing a market into groups (e.g. by age, gender, or region) and taking a proportional sample from each group.
  • Convenience sampling — a non-random method of selecting individuals based on their availability, such as the first 100 people to enter a store; it is the most likely method to produce biased results.

Key theories & models

  • Four-step market research cycle — a model describing the market research process as: determine what information is needed, gather it through primary and/or secondary research, analyze and evaluate the data, then report the findings to decision-makers.
