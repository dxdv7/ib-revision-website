2.6 — Communication

Key terms

  • Formal communication — the official flow of information through predefined channels, usually following the organizational structure and hierarchical chain of command, from senior staff down to lower-level employees.
  • Informal communication — communication that flows freely throughout an organization outside formal channels; it is quick and natural, does not leave a paper trail, and is often called the "grapevine".
  • Chain of command — the hierarchical structure through which formal communication typically flows within an organization.
  • Grapevine — the informal network through which unofficial information spreads throughout an organization.
  • Single strand chain — an informal communication network in which one person shares information with a second person, who passes it to a third, and so on in sequence.
  • Gossip chain — an informal communication network in which one person initiates and shares information with a group, who then pass it on to further people.
  • Cluster chain — an informal communication network in which a person passes information on to their own cluster of contacts (or keeps it to themselves), and each recipient then passes it on to a further cluster, similar to how a social media challenge spreads.
  • Probability chain — an informal communication network in which each individual randomly tells another individual the same piece of information.
  • Communication barrier — anything that hinders a business from delivering the correct message to the right person at the right time, or that prevents a receiver from receiving the right message at the right time.
  • Organizational structure complexity — a communication barrier arising from having many administrative levels for a message to pass through, causing transmission loss and poor retention of information.
  • Status barrier — a communication barrier arising when people of different statuses or positions are unable to speak freely with one another.
  • Insufficient incentives — a barrier where employees with useful ideas remain silent because they are not rewarded or appreciated for contributing them.
  • Lack of confidence in subordinates — a barrier where a supervisor who doubts their subordinates' skills does not seek their advice or comments.
  • Reluctance to communicate — a barrier where employees are unwilling to communicate, often due to fear of rejection or other negative consequences.
  • Linguistic differences — a barrier caused by differences in language, dialect, or jargon that prevent a shared understanding between communicators.
  • Emotional barrier — a mental block that influences how a person perceives others' actions and prevents them from clearly communicating their own feelings.
  • Physiological barrier — a barrier caused by physical limitations, such as ill health, a vision or hearing impairment, or limited mobility.
  • Psychological barrier — any of several mental or perceptual barriers to communication, including filtering, selective perception, information overload, emotional disconnect, and biased language.
  • Filtering — withholding or reframing information to manage how the receiver reacts to it.
  • Selective perception — filtering information, often subconsciously, to suit one's own needs or assumptions based on past experience.
  • Information overload — occurring when there is too much information to process in the time available.
  • Emotional disconnect — occurring when a communicator is upset or preoccupied, whether caused by the communication itself or by unrelated personal events.
  • Biased language — language that offends or stereotypes people, often without the speaker being aware of it.
