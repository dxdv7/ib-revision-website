4.1 — Introduction to marketing

Key terms

  • Marketing — the process of identifying, anticipating, and meeting the needs and wants of customers and society; it covers the full marketing mix, not just promotion, and is about how an organization communicates the value it brings to its target audience.
  • Marketing mix — the set of controllable elements an organization uses in its marketing activity: product, price, promotion, place, people, processes, and physical evidence.
  • Content marketing — the strategic marketing approach of creating and distributing valuable, relevant, and consistent content to attract and acquire a clearly defined audience, with the objective of driving profitable customer action.
  • Social marketing — the application of marketing principles and techniques to change social behaviors and promote awareness of social issues, commonly used in areas such as public health.
  • Product orientation — a marketing approach that focuses on making the best product possible and assumes there will always be a market for it; it is internally focused, uses little or no market research, and is driven by technical innovation rather than consumer needs.
  • Market orientation — a marketing approach that focuses on identifying and satisfying consumer needs through market research and analysis before developing a product; it is externally focused and aims to minimize risk.
  • Market — a place where buyers and sellers gather; its size can be measured by the value of sales or the number of units sold, and it can be categorized by geography, size, industry, or product type.
  • Revenue market share — sales revenue of the organization ÷ total sales revenue of the market × 100; the percentage of total market sales revenue captured by a single organization.
  • Unit market share — units of sales of the organization ÷ total unit sales of the market × 100; the percentage of total market unit sales captured by a single organization.
  • Revenue market growth — (current sales revenue − previous sales revenue) ÷ previous sales revenue × 100; the percentage change in market size, measured by revenue, over a given period.
  • Unit market growth — (current units of sales − previous units of sales) ÷ previous units of sales × 100; the percentage change in market size, measured by units sold, over a given period.
  • Market leader — the organization with the highest market share in a particular market; market leadership can bring the ability to influence competitors and prices, stronger brand recognition, growth through economies of scale, and human resource benefits, but also attracts competition and regulatory attention.

Key theories & models

  • Seth Godin's lock and key analogy — a way of distinguishing the two orientations: product orientation makes a key (product) and hopes to find the right lock (market), while market orientation starts with the lock (customers' needs and desires) and makes a key (product) specifically for it.
