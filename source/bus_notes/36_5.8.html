5.8 — Research and development

Key terms

  • Research and development (R&D) — the process by which an organization gathers knowledge to create new products or find new ways to improve existing products and processes.
  • First mover advantage — the advantage gained by a company that is first to introduce a new product or service to the market.
  • Incremental innovation — a series of small, lower-cost improvements made to a company's existing products or services over time, e.g. the Gillette razor or the iPhone.
  • Disruptive innovation — a process in which new entrants challenge incumbent firms, often by targeting overlooked market segments or creating an entirely new market, eventually displacing established competitors, e.g. Netflix displacing Blockbuster.
  • Unmet needs — customer needs or wants not yet addressed by existing products, which businesses seek to identify in order to innovate and gain a unique market position.
  • Intellectual property rights (IPR) — legal rights that give creators an exclusive right over the use of their ideas and creations for a certain period, promoting innovation and creativity.
  • Patent — an exclusive right granted for a new technical invention; the holder can license others to produce it in return for a royalty payment.
  • Copyright — protection ensuring authors, composers, artists, and filmmakers receive recognition, payment, and protection for their creative work.
  • Trademark — protection of a word, logo, or symbol that prevents its use by competitors.
  • Registered design protection — protection of the outward appearance or shape of a product, giving the owner an exclusive right to that design.
  • Geographical indication — protection of a location's name associated with a product, assuring it was produced in that place to a specific recipe or practice, e.g. Champagne or Scotch whisky.
  • Trade secret — a valuable, closely guarded piece of knowledge that provides a competitive advantage, e.g. KFC's original recipe.

Key theories & models

  • The R&D process (six-stage model) — a model of how R&D typically progresses: foster ideas, focus ideas, develop ideas, prototypes and trials, regulatory/marketing/product development activities, and launch, with the idea able to be scrapped at any stage.
