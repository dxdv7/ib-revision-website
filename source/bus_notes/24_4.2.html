4.2 — Marketing planning

Key terms

  • Marketing plan — a document setting out an organization's market analysis, marketing objectives, marketing strategy, financial projections (budget and sales forecasts), and a detailed action plan.
  • Market analysis — background research into a market, including market research and tools such as a SWOT or STEEPLE analysis, used to inform a marketing plan.
  • Marketing objectives — the specific goals of a marketing plan, including identification of the target market.
  • Marketing strategy — the methods, such as the 7Ps of the marketing mix, that explain how an organization will accomplish its marketing objectives.
  • Market segmentation — the process of collecting, organizing, and analyzing market research to identify and categorize groups of consumers (segments) who share common characteristics.
  • Demographic segmentation — dividing a market by measurable population characteristics such as age, education, income, or religion.
  • Psychographic segmentation — dividing a market by attitudes, tastes, values, and behaviors.
  • Geographic segmentation — dividing a market by location, such as country, state, town, or climate.
  • Target market — the particular market segment an organization chooses to focus its marketing strategy and marketing mix on.
  • Market positioning — the process by which a business seeks to identify and establish its brand within a certain part of the market relative to competitors, e.g. as a luxury or budget brand.
  • Position map (perception map) — a visual tool that plots an organization's product and its competitors' products against two chosen variables (commonly price and quality) to identify gaps and guide positioning strategy.
  • Mass market — a broad target market with no attempt at segmentation, where a business tries to reach the largest possible audience and competes mainly on quantity.
  • Niche market — a small, narrow segment of a market with specific needs, reached through segmentation and targeting; organizations here tend to use specialized processes and charge higher prices, aiming to be a large fish in a small pond.
  • Mass customization — a production and marketing technique that uses consumer data and technology (e.g. machine learning) to deliver individually customized products or messages on a massive scale.
  • Dynamic pricing — a strategy of charging different prices to individual customers depending on how and when they access a company's website or product.
  • Unique selling point/proposition (USP) — a distinguishing feature of a product or brand that separates it from competitors and can come from any element of the marketing mix.
  • Social media — any website or application that facilitates the sharing of content through virtual networks and communities.
  • Differentiation — the ways an organization distinguishes itself and its products from competitors, e.g. through unique product features, lower price, convenient/unique place, exceptional people/service, distinctive promotion or packaging, or innovative processes.

Key theories & models

  • SWOT analysis — a framework examining an organization's internal Strengths and Weaknesses alongside external Opportunities and Threats, often used within market analysis.
  • STEEPLE analysis — a framework analyzing the Social, Technological, Economic, Environmental, Political, Legal, and Ethical external factors affecting a market, used within market analysis.
  • Segmentation, targeting, and positioning (STP) process — a marketing model in which segmentation leads to targeting, which leads to positioning, which in turn leads to the marketing strategy.
  • Porter's generic strategies — a strategic model emphasizing differentiation and focus, arguing that a business cannot be superior in all aspects and should instead identify what it does best, highlight its USP, and use segmentation and targeting to meet the needs of a defined market.
