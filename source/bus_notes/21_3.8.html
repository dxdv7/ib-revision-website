3.8 — Investment appraisal

Key terms

  • Capital expenditure — spending on long-term or fixed assets that will be used for more than one year, such as buildings, machinery, and equipment.
  • Investment appraisal — the process of assessing the future financial returns of capital expenditures, using the initial capital cost, projected net cash flows, and the life expectancy of the investment.
  • Payback period — the time it takes to recover the initial capital cost of an investment; found by tracking cumulative net cash flow until it equals the initial cost, or by (initial capital cost ÷ annual net cash flow) when annual cash flows are equal; most businesses want a payback period under three years, and the shorter the better.
  • Average rate of return (ARR) — [((total returns − capital cost) ÷ years of use) ÷ capital cost] × 100; expresses the average annual net cash flow of an investment as a percentage of the initial capital cost, considering the entire life of the investment; businesses generally want an ARR above 10 per cent, and the higher the better.
  • Net present value (NPV) (HL) — the sum of the present values of an investment's net cash flows minus its original cost; NPV should generally be positive to be considered, and the higher the better.
  • Present value (HL) — annual net cash flow × discount factor; converts a future cash flow into today's money.
  • Discount factor (HL) — a value less than 1, found in a discount table for a given interest rate and year, used to reduce (discount) a future cash flow to its present value.
  • Time value of money (HL) — the principle that cash received now is worth more than the same amount of cash received in the future, because it can be used or invested immediately; ARR and payback period ignore this, but NPV accounts for it.
  • Cumulative net cash flow — the running total of a project's annual net cash flows, added year by year; used to identify the year in which the initial capital cost is recovered when calculating payback period.
