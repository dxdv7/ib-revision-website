3.3 — Costs and revenues

Key terms

  • Fixed costs — costs that do not change in the short term regardless of the level of output or sales, such as rent, insurance, and salaries.
  • Variable costs — costs that vary in direct proportion to the change in output or level of production, such as raw materials, utilities, and delivery costs.
  • Semi-variable cost — a cost that combines fixed and variable elements, such as a salary based partly on a fixed amount and partly on commission or performance-related pay.
  • Total costs — fixed costs plus variable costs; the total cost line does not start at the origin on a cost–output graph because fixed costs are incurred even at zero output.
  • Direct costs — costs that directly relate to the production of a good or service and can be traced to a specific cost object, such as packaging and raw materials; included in gross profit calculations.
  • Indirect costs (overheads) — costs that cannot be linked directly to a specific product, service, or department, such as management salaries and rent for multi-purpose facilities; excluded from gross profit calculations but included in net profit calculations.
  • Revenue (sales revenue / sales turnover) — price × quantity sold; the income generated by the main operations of a business from the sale of goods or services, shown as the top line of a profit and loss statement.
