1.2 — Types of business entities

Key terms

  • Sector — a group of businesses that engage in similar or related business activities (for example, primary, secondary, or tertiary).
  • Public sector — industries and services owned and controlled by regional and/or national governments, whose main aim is to supply essential services rather than make a profit.
  • Private sector — the economy’s commercial sector, owned and operated by private individuals and organizations with a profit incentive.
  • Privatization — the process of selling a public sector organization to the private sector.
  • Public-private partnership — an arrangement in which a government outsources a project or service to a private sector company, using its expertise to reduce the cost of providing services to the public.
  • Sole trader (sole proprietorship) — a business owned by a single individual who has complete control and unlimited liability, and is not recognized as a separate legal entity from the business.
  • Unlimited liability — where the owner(s) of a business are personally responsible for paying the complete cost of any debt or liability incurred, because the business has no separate legal identity from its owners.
  • Partnership — a business owned by two or more people who operate it together and share its profit and loss, under a partnership agreement.
  • Partnership agreement — a legal document outlining the details of a partnership, including each partner’s obligations and profit-sharing percentage.
  • Limited company (corporation) — a company recognized as a separate legal entity from its owners, whose shareholders have limited liability and can enter contracts and own property in the company’s name.
  • Limited liability — where shareholders can only lose the amount they invested in a company, not their personal assets, if the company fails.
  • Privately held company — a limited company whose shares are not openly traded on a public stock exchange and can only be transferred with the agreement of other shareholders.
  • Publicly held company — a limited company owned by at least two people whose shares are freely bought and sold on a stock exchange.
  • Initial public offering (IPO) — the first time a private company sells shares to the public by listing them on one or more stock exchanges, also known as ‘floating’ on the stock market.
  • Board of directors — the group elected by shareholders to determine a company’s strategic direction, appoint its management team, and monitor their actions.
  • For-profit social enterprise — a profit-making business with social and/or environmental objectives at the heart of its operations, generating revenue in ethically responsible ways.
  • Public sector company (government-owned corporation) — a legal organization established by a central government, which may be its sole or partial owner, to engage in commercial operations on its behalf.
  • Public corporation — an organization under government ownership and management whose main goal is to serve the general public rather than make a profit.
  • Cooperative — an organization owned and democratically controlled by an association of people to meet their common economic, social, and/or cultural needs, with profits shared equally between members as dividends.
  • Producer cooperative — a cooperative whose members work together to produce goods.
  • Consumer cooperative — a cooperative that purchases goods in bulk on behalf of its members to benefit from economies of scale.
  • Worker cooperative — a cooperative owned and run by its employees that provides services and sells goods.
  • Non-governmental organization (NGO) — a non-profit group that works independently of government control, sometimes with government funding, to address social, political, or environmental problems.
  • Non-profit organization (NPO) — an organization operating without the aim of making a profit; closely related to, but not always identical to, an NGO, depending on how a country defines the terms.
