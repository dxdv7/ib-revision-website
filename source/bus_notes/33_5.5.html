5.5 — Break-even analysis

Key terms

  • Break-even analysis — a financial calculation that weighs up a business's costs and revenues to work out how many products or services it must sell to at least cover its costs.
  • Break-even point — the level of output at which total revenue equals total costs, so the business makes neither a profit nor a loss.
  • Contribution per unit — selling price per unit minus variable cost per unit; the amount each unit sold contributes toward covering fixed costs and then profit.
  • Total contribution — total sales revenue minus total variable cost; the overall amount all units sold contribute toward fixed costs and profit.
  • Fixed costs — costs that do not change with the level of output, such as rent, and must be paid even if nothing is produced.
  • Variable costs — costs that vary directly with the level of output, rising as more units are produced and falling to zero at zero output.
  • Total costs — the sum of total fixed costs and total variable costs at a given level of output.
  • Total revenue — selling price per unit multiplied by the quantity sold.
  • Margin of safety — the difference between the current (actual) level of sales and the break-even level of output.
  • Target profit — a specific profit level a business aims to achieve, used to work out how many units must be sold to reach it.
  • Target profit output — the level of output needed to achieve a target profit, calculated as (fixed costs + target profit) ÷ contribution per unit.
  • Target price — the selling price required, given fixed and variable costs and a target profit output, to achieve a specific target profit.

Key theories & models

  • Break-even formula — break-even point = total fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit; used to calculate the exact output level at which a business breaks even.
  • Break-even chart — a graph plotting fixed costs (horizontal line), variable/total costs (upward-sloping lines) and total revenue (upward-sloping line from the origin) against output, used to visually identify the break-even point and the effect of cost or price changes.
