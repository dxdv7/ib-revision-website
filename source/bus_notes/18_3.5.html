3.5 — Profitability and liquidity ratio analysis

Key terms

  • Ratio analysis — a quantitative technique used to evaluate the financial performance and stability of a business by comparing figures from its final accounts over time or against other businesses in the same industry.
  • Profitability ratios — ratios that measure the degree to which a business achieves financial gain from its sales; calculated using figures from the profit and loss account (statement of profit or loss).
  • Gross profit margin (GPM) — (gross profit ÷ sales revenue) × 100; measures the percentage of revenue remaining after the cost of sales has been deducted.
  • Net profit margin (NPM) / profit margin — (profit before interest and tax ÷ sales revenue) × 100; measures the percentage of revenue remaining after both cost of sales and expenses (overheads) have been deducted, and is generally considered a better measure of profitability than GPM.
  • Return on capital employed (ROCE) — (profit before interest and tax ÷ capital employed) × 100; measures the profit generated as a percentage of the capital invested in the business, and is considered a prime or key ratio by investors and analysts.
  • Capital employed — non-current liabilities plus equity (share capital and retained earnings); represents all shareholders’ funds and long-term sources of finance, taken from the balance sheet.
  • Liquidity ratios — ratios that analyze the financial stability of a business and its ability to meet short-term debt obligations, using information from the balance sheet only.
  • Current ratio — current assets ÷ current liabilities; shows how many dollars of current assets are available to cover each dollar of short-term debt.
  • Current assets — items owned by a business that will be used up or converted into cash within a year, such as cash, debtors (accounts receivable), and stock (inventory).
  • Current liabilities — debts or payables due within one year, such as bank overdrafts, creditors, and other short-term loans.
  • Acid test (quick) ratio — (current assets − stock) ÷ current liabilities; a more conservative liquidity measure that excludes stock, usually the least liquid current asset.
  • Recommended current ratio range — 1.5–2.0 is considered healthy for most businesses; below 1.5 suggests liquidity problems, while above 2.0 suggests too many assets are tied up in current assets rather than invested in growth.
  • Recommended acid test ratio range — 1.0–1.5 is considered healthy for most businesses; below 1.0 suggests liquidity problems, while above 1.5 suggests too much cash is tied up rather than invested in fixed assets.
  • Limitations of ratio analysis — ratios provide only quantitative data, are not applicable to non-profit organizations, rely on historical financial statements, and are most reliable for publicly held companies that must publish audited final accounts; a single ratio without comparative data is of limited use.

Key theories & models

  • Simple linear regression — a statistical technique used to identify the trend in a ratio, such as ROCE, over multiple time periods by fitting a trendline to the data.
  • Coefficient of determination (R²) — a value between 0 and 1 that indicates how closely data points fit a trendline; values close to 1 indicate little variation from the trend (steady performance), while values close to 0 indicate large variation (volatility).
