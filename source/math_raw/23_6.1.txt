6.1 Measuring correlation
A functional relationship exists between variables when there 
is a formula (or function) that relates one variable to the other, so 
that all the data points lie on the same line or curve (the graph of 
the formula).
A statistical relationship means that there is not such a direct relation 
ship, but the data points, when plotted, may indicate a tendency to be 
close to some line or curve.

The points (3,14) and (10,15) here are called outliers. Outliers were introduced 
in Chapter 3, and you learned how to identify them from a data set by using the 
upper and lower quartiles and the interquartile range. Once you have identified 
any outliers in a data set, you can then consider whether they might affect the 
correlation and whether they should be removed. From what we have just seen, 
an outlier that does not follow the general trend has more of an effect on the 
correlation coefficient than one that does.
